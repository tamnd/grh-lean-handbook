## The chapters of Volume 9

The twelve later chapters of the volume prove the inputs of Section 3. The list gives each chapter with the chapters of this volume that it uses. An entry in brackets is an earlier volume.

```
 2 upper-bound-objects    <- (Vol 4, 6)
 3 theta-kernels-gauges   <- 2, 10
 4 seesaw-identity-1      <- 2
 5 seesaw-identity-2      <- 4
 6 archimedean-size       <- 2, 3
 7 field-at-primes        <- (Vol 3)
 8 cm-formal-group        <- 7
 9 normal-forms           <- 8
10 integral-vacuum        <- 9
11 elementary-divisors    <- (Vol 5)
12 divisibility           <- 2, 3, 6, 9, 11
13 proof-of-theorem-a     <- 5, 6, 12
```

There are three lines of work. The seesaw line is 2, 4, 5. The size line is 2, 3, 6. The divisibility line is 7, 8, 9, 10, then 3, 11 and 12. Chapter 3 uses chapter 10, which comes later in the order of the volume. A reader of chapter 3 can take the statements of chapter 10 as known, or read chapters 7 to 10 first.

*Chapter 2.* [The objects of the upper bound](/chapter/upper-bound-objects/) sets up the labels, the weights $\kappa$, the grids, the laws of the lifts at the level $N$, the chains, the columns and rows of the diagrams, the composite lift and the chain data of Section 3, and the Poincaré map as a Bessel period.

*Chapter 3.* [Theta kernels and gauges](/chapter/theta-kernels-gauges/) studies one theta kernel: its conjugates under $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$, which act on jets semilinearly, and the raising operators. It proves that a kernel with integral vacuum data maps integral forms to integral forms, measured by gauges.

*Chapter 4.* [The seesaw identity, part 1](/chapter/seesaw-identity-1/) defines admissible combinations and neighbour lattices, unfolds the inner product with the Poincaré series in stages, and writes the integral as a sum over cosets.

*Chapter 5.* [The seesaw identity, part 2](/chapter/seesaw-identity-2/) cuts the sum into classes, assembles the chain datum, and proves the seesaw identity of Section 3, with the constant of the remark there.

*Chapter 6.* [The archimedean size of the composite lift](/chapter/archimedean-size/) proves the theorem on the size of the composite lift, the theorem on short source forms, and the lemma on the good part. It also bounds the allowed losses.

*Chapter 7.* [The field $E$ at the primes](/chapter/field-at-primes/) describes the splitting of the small primes and of $N$ in $E$, the different, the Weil data and the chosen places.

*Chapter 8.* [The CM curve and its formal group](/chapter/cm-formal-group/) studies the derivatives of theta functions of a CM elliptic curve through its formal group: the Frobenius congruence and the exponent theorem.

*Chapter 9.* [Normal forms and the count](/chapter/normal-forms/) brings a jet of derivatives to a normal form at a prime and counts the divisibility that it has. The bounds are of Legendre type, as in Exercise 3.

*Chapter 10.* [The integral vacuum and tight frames](/chapter/integral-vacuum/) builds the vacuum data that are integral at every prime, from tight frames, and fixes their archimedean scale by the Chowla-Selberg formula.

*Chapter 11.* [Elementary divisors of a Hecke operator](/chapter/elementary-divisors/) computes elementary divisors with a Frobenius cycle, a root lemma, a cone of Pieri type, a defect count and an average over a Schur algebra.

*Chapter 12.* [Divisibility prime by prime](/chapter/divisibility/) proves the theorem on the divisibility of the composite lift. It treats the marked primes and scaled Hecke operators, the valuation of a chain, the level $N$, and the nonsplit primes, and it chooses the submodule $S$.

*Chapter 13.* [The proof of Theorem A](/chapter/proof-of-theorem-a/) gives the proof of Section 4 with the passage to the target in full.

### Prerequisites

The volume uses the following chapters of earlier volumes.

*Volume 3.* The CM field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$, its infinite places and the group $(\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times$ that acts on them are in [CM fields, towers and places](/chapter/cm-fields/). The twists are characters of finite ideles, from [class field theory on finite ideles](/chapter/class-field-theory/). Chapters 7 and 8 use [CM elliptic curves](/chapter/cm-elliptic-curves/).

*Volume 4.* The hermitian spaces $X_j$, $Y_j$ and their unitary groups over the adeles are in [adeles and hermitian spaces](/chapter/adeles-hermitian-spaces/). The algebraic forms on definite unitary groups, their Petersson inner products and the Weyl lattices are in [automorphic forms on unitary groups](/chapter/automorphic-forms/). The classes and the levels are in [quotients and reduction theory](/chapter/reduction-theory/) and [congruence subgroups and Haar moduli](/chapter/congruence-subgroups/).

*Volume 5.* Chapter 11 uses Schur modules and Green's theory of the characters of finite general linear groups, from [integral Weyl modules and branching](/chapter/weyl-modules/) and [characters of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a finite field](/chapter/finite-gln/). The Bessel subgroups of the Poincaré map are in [Bessel subgroups and Bruhat measures](/chapter/bessel-bruhat/).

*Volume 6.* The theta kernels come from [the Weil representation from a vacuum](/chapter/weil-representation/) and [local theta in the Fock model](/chapter/local-theta/). The lifts and the classical seesaw are in [theta kernels, theta lifts and the seesaw](/chapter/theta-lifts-seesaw/), and the first step of the unfolding is in [Bessel periods and one step of unfolding](/chapter/bessel-periods/).
