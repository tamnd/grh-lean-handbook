## The chapter and its place

This chapter prepares the divisibility side of Theorem A. It proves four groups of results. First, at a small exceptional prime $p$ there is a full mark: a set $\Lambda$ of primes of $E$ above $p$ that meets each pair of a prime induced by an a-place and its conjugate exactly once. Second, at each prime of a marked set there is a Hecke operator, scaled by a power of $p$, on the space of source forms. Third, a combination of chains of theta lifts that is invariant under the levels obeys a $p$-adic gauge bound: if each kernel multiplies gauges by at most $p^{r_j}$, the whole chain multiplies them by at most $p^{\sum_j r_j}$. Fourth, the glued tensor of a chain datum expands into a plain combination of translates of the integral vacuum, and this combination is a weak chain datum. The chapter [divisibility prime by prime](/chapter/divisibility/) uses all four groups.

### The notation

Fix a prime $p$ and a ring embedding $\iota_p : \mathbb C \to \mathbb C_p$. The field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ is a CM field of degree $2f$, and $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. The infinite places of $E$ form the group $\Phi \cong (\mathbb Z/q_1)^\times \cong \mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$. For an integer $\nu$ prime to $q_1$, write $[\nu] \in \Phi$ for the place named by the class of $\nu$. An a-place is a place of class $1$ modulo $q_0$. A b-place is one of the chosen places of class $-1$ modulo $q_0$. For $\sigma \in \Phi$, the composite $\iota_p \circ \sigma$ is an embedding of $E$ into $\mathbb C_p$. It induces a prime $w_\sigma$ of $E$ above $p$. The bar $\bar w$ is the complex conjugate of a prime $w$.

### A sketch of the proofs

*Exceptional primes.* A prime $p$ is exceptional if $\chi_K(p) = -1$, $p \not\equiv \pm 1 \pmod m$, and the order $o_0$ of $-p$ in $(\mathbb Z/q_0)^\times$ is odd and at most $r = \lceil \log_p m \rceil$. The decomposition group of $p$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$ is generated by $-p$ times complex conjugation. So the places $[-p]^i \tau$ induce $w_\tau$ for even $i$ and $\bar w_\tau$ for odd $i$. Lifting the exponent shows that $m$ divides the order of $[-p]$. This order is even, so $w_\tau \ne \bar w_\tau$.

*Full marks.* Order the primes linearly. Keep, among the primes induced by a-places, those that are smaller than their conjugates. Since $o_0$ is odd, the place $[-p^{o_0}]\tau$ is again an a-place and induces $\bar w_\tau$. So each pair $\{w_\tau, \bar w_\tau\}$ with $\tau$ an a-place meets the set in exactly one prime.

*The scaled Hecke operator.* A prime $\mathfrak l$ of a marked set is split and prime to $MN$. So the maximal compact subgroup at $\mathfrak l$ lies in the level of source forms, and $T_{\mathfrak l, r}$ acts on them. The scale is $p^{A_{\mathfrak l}}$, with $A_{\mathfrak l} = a$ times the number of a-places that induce $\mathfrak l$.

*Chains.* Joint invariance turns the integral in each lift into a finite sum over classes, each of volume $1$, as in [chains and free levels](/chapter/upper-bound-objects/#chains-of-lifts-and-free-levels). The value of the chain is then a plain sum of products of kernels. The gauge is ultrametric, so the sum obeys the bound of each term, and each term obeys the product of the bounds of its kernels.

*Expanding the gluing.* Each glued pair is a sum of translates by matrices with simple poles above $N$, indexed by classes modulo $N$. Collect these translates with the coefficients of $\Psi$. The result is a combination with at most $N^{fmn_m}$ times as much mass, which is less than $\exp(x^{60})$. The projection on vacua above $N$ undoes the gluing. It commutes with the integral levels, so the plain tensor of a chain datum is invariant too.
