## The Frobenius element and the decomposition of a prime

This section describes how a prime $p \nmid q_1 d$ splits in $E$. The answer is the usual one for an abelian extension: the decomposition group of a place above $p$ is generated by the Frobenius, and the norm of the place is $p$ to the order of the Frobenius. The source writes the Frobenius as a pair in $\Phi \times \mathbb{Z}$, and this section explains the pair.

### The action of $\Phi$ on the finite places

For $v \in \Phi$, the automorphism $\sigma_v \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$ restricts to a ring automorphism of $\mathcal{O}_E$, which we also write $\sigma_v$.

**Definition (The action on finite places).** For $v \in \Phi$ and a finite place $w$ of $E$, $v \cdot w$ is the finite place whose prime ideal is $\{x \in \mathcal{O}_E : \sigma_v(x) \in w\}$. So $x \in v \cdot w$ if and only if $\sigma_v(x) \in w$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.galOfPlaceInt`, `New.Parameters.coe_galOfPlaceInt`, `New.Parameters.mem_asIdeal_smul_iff` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The action is the pullback, so the prime ideal of $v \cdot w$ is $\sigma_v^{-1}(w)$ in the notation of a textbook, which writes $\sigma(w)$ for the image. The two conventions differ by $v \mapsto v^{-1}$. As $\Phi$ is commutative, the pullback is still an action, and the two conventions have the same stabilizers and the same orbits. All statements below are about stabilizers and orbits, so the convention does not change their content. The instance of `SMul` is unnamed in the source (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean, line 436).

The full Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is generated by $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$ and the complex conjugation $c$. The conjugation commutes with every $\sigma_v$: both sides of $c\sigma_v c = \sigma_v$ fix $\sqrt{-d}$ and send $\zeta$ to $\zeta^a$, where $a$ is the class of $v$. So $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(E/K) \times \{1, c\}$, and every element is $\sigma_v$ or $\sigma_v c$. The source writes the pair $(v, 1)$ for $\sigma_v$ and $(v, -1)$ for $\sigma_v c$. To say "$c$ is the identity with sign $1$, or $c$ is the conjugation with sign $-1$", it uses one predicate.

**Definition (Identity or conjugation with sign).** For a ring automorphism $c$ of $\mathcal{O}_E$ and $e \in \mathbb{Z}$, the pair $(c, e)$ is the identity with a sign if $c = \mathrm{id}$ and $e = 1$, or $c$ is complex conjugation and $e = -1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.IsIdOrConjWithSign` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

The pullback of $w$ along complex conjugation is the conjugate place $\bar w$ of [CM fields](/chapter/cm-fields/).

### The Frobenius pair

**Definition (The Frobenius pair).** For a prime $p$, the Frobenius pair is
$$\mathrm{Frob}_p = \left(v_{\chi_K(p) p},\ \chi_K(p)\right) \in \Phi \times \mathbb{Z},$$
where $\Phi \times \mathbb{Z}$ is a monoid with the group law of $\Phi$ and the multiplication of $\mathbb{Z}$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.frobElem` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

**Lemma.** Let $p$ be prime with $p \nmid d$. For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\chi_K(p)^n$ is $1$ or $-1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.chiK_pow_eq_one_or_neg_one` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* The Legendre symbol of a number prime to $d$ is $\pm 1$, and so are its powers. $\square$

For $p \nmid q_1 d$, $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is the Frobenius element of $p$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \Phi \times \{\pm 1\}$. If $p$ splits in $K$, it is $(v_p, 1)$: the automorphism $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^p$ that fixes $\sqrt{-d}$. If $p$ is inert in $K$, it is $(v_{-p}, -1)$: the automorphism $\sigma_{v_{-p}} c$, which sends $\zeta$ to $\sigma_{v_{-p}}(\zeta^{-1}) = \zeta^{p}$ and $\sqrt{-d}$ to $-\sqrt{-d}$.

**Remark (Formalization).** For $p \mid q_1 d$ the pair has junk values. If $p = d$, then $\chi_K(d) = 0$, the place $v_0$ is the junk value $1$ of [fields, places and twists](/chapter/fields-places-twists/), and $\mathrm{Frob}_d = (1, 0)$. If $p \mid q_1$, then $\chi_K(p) = 1$ and $v_p = 1$ is again a junk value. Every theorem below that uses $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ assumes $p \nmid q_1 d$.

We need two small facts on $p$.

**Lemma.** Let $p$ be prime with $p \nmid q_1$. Then $p \ne 2$, and $p$ is prime to $q_0$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.ne_two_of_not_dvd_q₁`, `New.Parameters.isCoprime_q₀_of_not_dvd` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* $2^{s+1}$ divides $q_1$, so $2 \mid q_1$. And $q_0 \mid q_1$. $\square$

### The Frobenius modulo a prime

Let $p$ be prime and let $\varphi : \mathcal{O}_E \to R$ be a ring homomorphism to a commutative ring of characteristic $p$.

**Lemma.** Two ring homomorphisms $f, g : \mathcal{O}_E \to R$ to a commutative ring are equal if $f(\zeta) = g(\zeta)$ and $f(\beta_K) = g(\beta_K)$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.ringHom_ext_zetaInt_betaKInE` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* By Section 1, $\mathcal{O}_E = \mathbb{Z}[\beta_K, \zeta]$, and a ring homomorphism is fixed by its values on generators. $\square$

The next lemma uses $\sqrt{-d} = 2\beta_K - 1$ in place of $\beta_K$, which is allowed since $2$ is invertible in $R$ when $p \ne 2$.

**Lemma.** Let $p \nmid q_1$ be prime, $\varphi : \mathcal{O}_E \to R$ as above, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $v \in \Phi$, and let $(c, e)$ be the identity or the conjugation with its sign. Then $\varphi(\sigma_v x) = \varphi(c x)^{p^n}$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_E$ if and only if both:

1. $\varphi(\sigma_v \zeta) = \varphi(\sigma_u \zeta)$, where $u = v_{e p^n}$;
2. $\chi_K(p)^n\, e\, \varphi(\sqrt{-d}) = \varphi(\sqrt{-d})$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.forall_map_galOfPlaceInt_eq_pow_iff` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* Both sides are ring homomorphisms in $x$, since $y \mapsto y^{p^n}$ is a ring homomorphism in characteristic $p$. So they agree if and only if they agree at $\zeta$ and at $\sqrt{-d}$. At $\zeta$: $c(\zeta) = \zeta^e$, so $\varphi(c\zeta)^{p^n} = \varphi(\zeta^{e p^n}) = \varphi(\sigma_u \zeta)$, which is condition 1. At $\sqrt{-d}$: $\sigma_v$ fixes $\sqrt{-d}$, and $c(\sqrt{-d}) = e\sqrt{-d}$. In characteristic $p$, $\sqrt{-d}^{\,p} = (-d)^{(p-1)/2}\sqrt{-d}$, and $(-d)^{(p-1)/2} \equiv (-d/p) = (p/d) \bmod p$ by Euler's criterion and quadratic reciprocity, since $d \equiv 3 \bmod 4$. So $\varphi(c\sqrt{-d})^{p^n} = e\,\chi_K(p)^n \varphi(\sqrt{-d})$, as $e^{p^n} = e$ for odd $p$. This gives condition 2. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $R$ be a domain of characteristic $p$ with $p \nmid q_1$, and $\varphi : \mathcal{O}_E \to R$ a ring homomorphism. For $u, v \in \Phi$, $\varphi(\sigma_v \zeta) = \varphi(\sigma_u \zeta)$ if and only if $v = u$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.map_galOfPlaceInt_zetaInt_inj` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* The image $\varphi(\zeta)$ is a primitive root of unity of order $q_1$ in $R$, since $q_1$ is invertible in $R$ and $X^{q_1} - 1$ is separable. With $a$ and $b$ the classes of $v$ and $u$, $\varphi(\zeta)^a = \varphi(\zeta)^b$ if and only if $a = b$ in $\mathbb{Z}/q_1$. $\square$

**Theorem.** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be prime, $R$ a domain of characteristic $p$, $\varphi : \mathcal{O}_E \to R$ a ring homomorphism, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and $(c, e)$ the identity or the conjugation with its sign. Then $\varphi(\sigma_v x) = \varphi(c x)^{p^n}$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_E$ if and only if $(v, e) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.map_galOfPlaceInt_eq_pow_iff_eq_frobElem_pow` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* Since $p \nmid d$, $\varphi(\sqrt{-d})^2 = -d \ne 0$ in $R$, so $\varphi(\sqrt{-d}) \neq 0$ and condition 2 of the first lemma says $\chi_K(p)^n e = 1$ in $R$. Both factors are $\pm 1$, and $-1 \ne 1$ in $R$ since $p \neq 2$. So condition 2 says $e = \chi_K(p)^n$. By the second lemma, condition 1 says $v = v_{e p^n}$. Now $\mathrm{Frob}_p^n = (v_{\chi_K(p) p}^n, \chi_K(p)^n) = (v_{\chi_K(p)^n p^n}, \chi_K(p)^n)$. So the two conditions together say $(v, e) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$. $\square$

### Decomposition groups and norms

**Lemma.** Let $w$ be a place above a prime $p$, $v \in \Phi$, and $c$ a ring automorphism of $\mathcal{O}_E$. If $v \cdot w$ is the pullback of $w$ along $c$, then for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\sigma_v x \equiv (c x)^{p^n} \bmod w$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_E$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.exists_mk_galOfPlaceInt_eq_pow_of_smul_eq` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* The hypothesis says $\sigma_v x \in w$ if and only if $c x \in w$. So $\sigma_v \circ c^{-1}$ maps $w$ into $w$ and induces a ring endomorphism of the finite field $\mathcal{O}_E/w$ of characteristic $p$. Every such endomorphism is a power $y \mapsto y^{p^n}$ of the Frobenius. Apply it to the class of $c x$. $\square$

**Theorem (Decomposition).** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be prime, $w$ a finite place above $p$, $v \in \Phi$, and $(c, e)$ the identity or the conjugation with its sign. Then $v \cdot w$ is the pullback of $w$ along $c$ if and only if $(v, e) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$. In particular:

1. $v \cdot w = w$ if and only if $(v, 1)$ is a power of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$;
2. $v \cdot w = \bar w$ if and only if $(v, -1)$ is a power of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.smul_eq_iff_exists_frobElem_pow`, `New.Parameters.smul_eq_self_iff_exists_frobElem_pow`, `New.Parameters.smul_eq_star_iff_exists_frobElem_pow` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* Let $\pi : \mathcal{O}_E \to \mathcal{O}_E/w$ be the quotient map, a homomorphism to a domain of characteristic $p$. If $v \cdot w$ is the pullback of $w$ along $c$, the last lemma gives $n$ with $\pi(\sigma_v x) = \pi(c x)^{p^n}$ for all $x$, and the last theorem gives $(v, e) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$. Conversely, if $(v, e) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$, the last theorem gives $\pi(\sigma_v x) = \pi(c x)^{p^n}$. Then $\sigma_v x \in w$ if and only if $c x \in w$, since $y^{p^n} = 0$ only for $y = 0$ in a field. This says that $v \cdot w$ is the pullback of $w$ along $c$. $\square$

In the language of a textbook, the decomposition group of $w$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is the cyclic group generated by $\mathrm{Frob}_p$.

**Theorem (The norm of a place).** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be prime and $w$ a place above $p$. Then $q_w = p^m$, where $m$ is the order of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ in $\Phi \times \mathbb{Z}$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.q_eq_pow_orderOf_frobElem` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* Let $q_w = p^g$. For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $g \mid n$ if and only if $y^{p^n} = y$ for all $y$ in the residue field $\mathcal{O}_E/w$. By the theorem of the last subsection with $v = 1$ and $(c, e) = (\mathrm{id}, 1)$, this holds if and only if $(1, 1) = \mathrm{Frob}_p^n$, that is, if and only if the order $m$ divides $n$. Two natural numbers with the same multiples are equal, so $g = m$. $\square$

Since the second coordinate of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is $\pm 1$, the order $m$ is the least common multiple of the order of the place $v_{\chi_K(p)p}$ in $\Phi$ and the order of $\chi_K(p)$ in $\{\pm 1\}$.

**Lemma (No ramification).** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be prime and $w$ a place above $p$. Then $p \notin w^2$, and $\mathrm{ord}_w(p) = 1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.natCast_notMem_sq`, `New.Parameters.intValuation_natCast_eq_exp_neg_one` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* If $p \in w^2$, then $w$ is ramified over $p$, and a ramified prime divides the different $\mathfrak{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}$. As $q_1 d \in \mathfrak{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}$ ([CM fields](/chapter/cm-fields/)), $q_1 d \in w$, so $p \mid q_1 d$, against the hypothesis. So $p \in w \setminus w^2$, and $\mathrm{ord}_w(p) = 1$. $\square$

In Lean the order appears as the multiplicative valuation: $w$-adic valuation of $p$ equal to $\exp(-1)$.

### Transitivity

**Lemma.** Let $w$ and $w'$ be finite places above the same prime $p$.

1. Some $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ satisfies $\sigma x \in w \iff x \in w'$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_E$.
2. Some $v \in \Phi$ satisfies $v \cdot w = w'$ or $\overline{v \cdot w} = w'$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.exists_gal_smul_mem_iff`, `New.Parameters.exists_smul_eq_or_star` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* Part 1 is the transitivity of the Galois group of a Galois extension on the primes above $p$ (Neukirch, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Chapter I, Proposition 9.1). For part 2, $\sigma$ is $\sigma_v$ or $\sigma_v c$ for some $v$. The source finds $v$ as the place of the embedding $\Phi_1 \circ \sigma$, which is $\Phi_v$ or its conjugate. In the first case $w' = v \cdot w$, and in the second case $w' = \overline{v \cdot w}$. $\square$

Part 1 holds for every prime $p$, also for $p \mid q_1 d$.

**Example.** In the model of Section 1 ($q_1 = 12$, $d = 23$), take $p = 29$. Then $(29/23) = (6/23) = 1$, since $11^2 = 121 \equiv 6 \bmod 23$. So $p$ splits in $K$, $29 \equiv 5 \bmod 12$, and $\mathrm{Frob}_{29} = (v_5, 1)$, of order $2$. A place $w$ above $29$ has $q_w = 29^2$. Its stabilizer in $\Phi$ is $\{1, v_5\}$, and no $v$ sends $w$ to $\bar w$, since no power of $\mathrm{Frob}_{29}$ has sign $-1$. So $29$ has $8/2 = 4$ places in $E$, in two conjugate pairs. Take instead $p = 13$. Then $(13/23) = 1$ since $6^2 = 36 \equiv 13 \bmod 23$, and $13 \equiv 1 \bmod 12$. So $\mathrm{Frob}_{13} = (1, 1)$, the places above $13$ have norm $13$, and there are $8$ of them.
