## The eigenquotient

Step 3 of the model case restricts the cohomology to the part on which the Hecke operators act by the eigenvalues of a form. The classical choice is the isotypic part, or the generalized eigenspace. The source makes a different choice. It takes the largest quotient on which each Hecke operator acts by its eigenvalue. This quotient has a universal property, and the three facts that this chapter needs follow from the universal property with little work: the quotient is crystalline when the inputs are, a relation between Hecke operators and Frobenius passes to the quotient, and the Hodge-Tate weights of the quotient are controlled.

This section is pure algebra. The group $\Gamma$ is an arbitrary topological group, with a map to $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ for the parts on periods. Section 6 applies it with $\Gamma = \operatorname{Gal}(\bar E/E)$ and $V_i$ the cohomology groups of the model.

### Definition and existence

Let $(V_i)_{i \in I}$ be finitely many finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector spaces with representations $\rho_i$ of $\Gamma$. Let $T_i(s) \in \operatorname{End}(V_i)$ be operators indexed by a set $\Sigma$, and let $\theta : \Sigma \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$.

**Definition (Eigenquotient).** A homomorphism $r : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}_N(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ with $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-linear maps $F_i : V_i \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$ is an *eigenquotient* for $(\rho, T, \theta)$ if:

1. each $F_i$ is equivariant: $r(g) F_i(x) = F_i(\rho_i(g) x)$;
2. the values $F_i(x)$ span $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$;
3. each $F_i$ turns $T_i(s)$ into $\theta(s)$: $F_i(T_i(s) x) = \theta(s) F_i(x)$;
4. every family of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-linear maps $f_i : V_i \to M$ to a $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-vector space $M$ with $f_i(T_i(s) x) = \theta(s) f_i(x)$ factors as $f_i = \varphi \circ F_i$ for a $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-linear $\varphi$.

Lean: `New.Variety.IsEigenQuotient` (Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean).

Conditions 2 and 4 say that $(F_i)$ is the quotient map
$$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \bigoplus_i V_i \longrightarrow \Big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \bigoplus_i V_i\Big) \Big/ \big\langle T_i(s) x - \theta(s) x \big\rangle,$$
written in a basis. The quotient by a span of differences is the space of *coinvariants* for the twisted action. In the language of modules: if $\mathcal{H}$ is the algebra generated by the $T_i(s)$ and $\mathfrak{m}_\theta$ the kernel of the character $\theta$, the quotient is $V / \mathfrak{m}_\theta V$ after the base change to $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$.

**Proposition (Existence).** Assume that each $T_i(s)$ commutes with $\rho_i(\Gamma)$. Assume that $g \mapsto f(\rho_i(g) x)$ is continuous for every $x \in V_i$, every $N$ and every $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-linear $f : V_i \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$. Then an eigenquotient $(r, F)$ exists, and $r$ is continuous.

Lean: `New.Variety.exists_isEigenQuotient` (Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean).

*Proof.* Let $G_i : V_i \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \otimes \bigoplus_j V_j$ be $x \mapsto 1 \otimes x$ in the $i$-th place, and let $S$ be the $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-span of the vectors $G_i(T_i(s) x) - \theta(s) G_i(x)$. The quotient by $S$ is finite-dimensional. Fix a basis, so that the quotient is $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$, and let $F_i$ be $G_i$ followed by the quotient map. Conditions 2 and 3 hold by construction. For condition 4, the family $(f_i)$ defines a $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-linear map on $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \otimes \bigoplus V_i$ that kills $S$.

For the action: fix $g \in \Gamma$. The family $x \mapsto F_i(\rho_i(g) x)$ turns $T_i(s)$ into $\theta(s)$, since $T_i(s)$ commutes with $\rho_i(g)$. Condition 4 gives a linear map $R(g)$ with $R(g) F_i(x) = F_i(\rho_i(g) x)$. Two linear maps that agree on the spanning set of values $F_i(x)$ are equal. So $R(1) = 1$ and $R(gg') = R(g) R(g')$, and $r(g)$ is the matrix of $R(g)$.

For continuity: the vectors $y$ such that $g \mapsto R(g) y$ is continuous form a subspace. It contains each $F_i(x)$, because $R(g) F_i(x) = F_i(\rho_i(g) x)$ and the hypothesis applies to $f = F_i$. So it is all of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$, and each matrix entry of $r$ is continuous. $\square$

**Example.** Let $I$ have one element, $V = \mathbb{Q}_\ell^2$, and let one operator $T$ act with $\Gamma$ trivial.

* If $T = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2)$ and $\theta = 2$, the span $S$ is the image of $T - 2$, which is the first coordinate line. The quotient has $N = 1$, and $F$ is the second coordinate.
* If $T = \begin{pmatrix} a & 1 \\ 0 & a \end{pmatrix}$ and $\theta = a$, then $S$ is the image of $T - a$, the first coordinate line. Again $N = 1$. The eigenspace of $T$ is the first coordinate line, so the quotient is not the eigenspace: it is the dual notion.
* If $\theta$ is not an eigenvalue of $T$, then $T - \theta$ is invertible after base change, so $S$ is everything and $N = 0$.

**Remark (Formalization).** The dimension $N$ is not controlled. It is $0$ when $\theta$ does not occur in the cohomology, and the theorems of this section then hold trivially. The action $R(g)$ is chosen by `choose` from condition 4, but it is unique by condition 2. The continuity hypothesis is stated for maps to $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$, so that no topology on $V_i$ is needed. The basis of the quotient is a chosen basis.

### Crystalline representations and Frobenius

For the places above $\ell$ the source works with the period ring $B$ (the ring `Bmax` of [the period rings](/chapter/period-rings/)) and with a map $j : \Gamma \to \operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ with open image. A representation is crystalline if the $\Gamma$-invariants of $B \otimes T$ span it over $B$.

**Definition (Weil pair; linear Frobenius; killing polynomial).** A pair $(g, f)$ with $g \in \operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ and $f \in \mathbb{N}$ is a *Weil pair* if $g^{-1}$ is a Frobenius element for $\ell^f$. For a representation $\rho$ on $T$, the *linear Frobenius* $\beta_{s, f}$ is a chosen $B$-linear endomorphism of $B \otimes T$ that agrees with $\varphi^f j(s) \otimes \rho(s)$ on $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(T)$, where $\varphi$ is the Frobenius of $B$. A polynomial $P$ *kills* $(s, f)$ if $\rho$ is potentially crystalline and $P(\beta_{s, f}) = 0$ on $B \otimes T$, with coefficients acting on $T$.

Lean: `New.Period.IsWeilPair`, `New.Period.linearFrob` and `New.Period.IsKilledBy` (Lib/PadicHodge/Crystalline/Basic.lean).

The textbook object is the Weil-Deligne representation of Fontaine, with a linear Frobenius on $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}$ (Fontaine, *Représentations l-adiques potentiellement semi-stables*, Astérisque 223 (1994), Exposé VIII). The source tests the polynomial on $B \otimes T$ in place of $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}$. The chapter [on the comparison theorems](/chapter/comparison-theorems/) explains the definitions.

**Remark (Formalization).** If no $B$-linear map agrees with $\varphi^f j(s) \otimes \rho(s)$ on $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}$, then $\beta_{s, f}$ is an arbitrary endomorphism, chosen by `Classical.epsilon`. When $\rho$ is potentially crystalline and $(j(s), f)$ is a Weil pair, such a map exists and is unique, so the junk value never enters below.

**Proposition (The eigenquotient is crystalline).** Let $(r, F)$ be an eigenquotient for $(\rho, T, \theta)$, and let each $\rho_i$ be crystalline. Then $r$ is crystalline.

Lean: `New.Variety.IsEigenQuotient.isCrystalline` (Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean).

*Proof.* It is enough to show that $1 \otimes y$ lies in the $B$-span of the invariants of $B \otimes \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$, for every $y$. These $y$ form an additive subgroup. By condition 2 the vectors $c F_i(x)$, with $c \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, generate $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$ additively. The map $c F_i$ is equivariant from the crystalline $\rho_i$ to $r$. An equivariant map carries invariants of $B \otimes V_i$ to invariants of $B \otimes \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$. So $1 \otimes c F_i(x)$, the image of $1 \otimes x$, lies in the span of the invariants. $\square$

**Proposition (Frobenius relations pass to the quotient).** Let $(r, F)$ be an eigenquotient with each $\rho_i$ crystalline, let $g \in \Gamma$ with $(j(g), f)$ a Weil pair, and let $t_k \in \Sigma$, $m_k \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $d_k \in \mathbb{N}$ for $k$ in a finite set $K$. Assume that, for every $i$,
$$\sum_{k \in K} m_k \, T_i(t_k) \circ \beta_{g^{d_k}, d_k f} = 0 \quad \text{on } B \otimes V_i.$$
Then the polynomial $\sum_{k \in K} m_k \theta(t_k) X^{d_k}$ kills $(g, f)$ for $r$.

Lean: `New.Variety.IsEigenQuotient.isKilledBy` (Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean).

*Proof.* The representation $r$ is crystalline by the last proposition. Test the operator $P(\beta_{g, f})$ on $a \otimes y$ with $a \in B$. As before it is enough to take $y = c F_i(x)$. Write $\phi = c F_i$, and let $\phi_B$ be its base change to $B$. Two facts do the work. First, $\beta$ is natural for equivariant maps and multiplicative in the pair: $\beta_{g, f}^d \circ \phi_B = \phi_B \circ \beta_{g^d, d f}$. Both sides agree with $\varphi^{df} j(g)^d \otimes \rho(g^d)$ on $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}$, and the uniqueness of the last remark applies. Second, $\phi$ turns $T_i(t_k)$ into $\theta(t_k)$. So
$$P(\beta_{g, f}) \, \phi_B(a \otimes x) = \phi_B\Big(\sum_k m_k \, T_i(t_k) \, \beta_{g^{d_k}, d_k f}(a \otimes x)\Big) = \phi_B(0) = 0. \qquad \square$$

### A lemma on joint eigenvectors

The last property needs a lemma of linear algebra. Its point is the following. If commuting operators have no common eigenvector for the eigenvalues $\eta$, then some polynomial in them is $1$ at $\eta$ and $0$ as an operator.

**Lemma.** Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field $k$. Let $\Phi$ map words in an alphabet $\Sigma$ to $\operatorname{End}(W)$ with $\Phi([s]) \Phi(L) = \Phi(L s)$, and assume that the $\Phi([s])$ commute. Let $\eta : \Sigma \to k$, and assume that no nonzero $x \in W$ has $\Phi([s]) x = \eta(s) x$ for all $s$. Then there are finitely many scalars $c_L$ with
$$\sum_L c_L \prod_{s \in L} \eta(s) = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_L c_L \Phi(L) = 0.$$

Lean: `New.Variety.exists_words_comb_eq_zero` (Lib/General/LinearAlgebra/Eigenvalues.lean).

*Proof.* Work in the algebra $k \times \operatorname{End}(W)$. Let $w(L) = (\eta(L), \Phi(L))$, with $\eta(L) = \prod_{s \in L} \eta(s)$, and let $S$ be the span of all $w(L)$. Left multiplication $\phi_s$ by $(\eta(s), \Phi([s]))$ sends $w(L)$ to $w(L s)$. So $S$ is stable, and the $\phi_s$ commute on $S$. The first projection $\lambda$ satisfies $\lambda \circ \phi_s = \eta(s) \lambda$, and $\lambda(w([\,])) = 1$. We need $(1, 0) \in S$.

Decompose $S$ into joint generalized eigenspaces $S_\chi$ of the $\phi_s$. On $S_\chi$ with $\chi \neq \eta$, some $\phi_s - \chi(s)$ is nilpotent, while $\lambda \circ (\phi_s - \chi(s)) = (\eta(s) - \chi(s)) \lambda$ with $\eta(s) \neq \chi(s)$. So $\lambda$ kills $S_\chi$. Hence $\lambda$ is nonzero on $S_\eta$. The second projection maps $S_\eta$ to a subspace of $\operatorname{End}(W)$ on which each $\Phi([s]) - \eta(s)$ acts by left multiplication and nilpotently. If this image is nonzero, the commuting nilpotent operators have a common kernel vector $M \neq 0$. Then $\Phi([s]) M = \eta(s) M$ for all $s$, and a nonzero column of $M$ is a forbidden joint eigenvector. So the second projection kills $S_\eta$. Take $y \in S_\eta$ with $\lambda(y) = 1$. Then $y = (1, 0)$, and its expansion in the $w(L)$ gives the $c_L$. $\square$

### Hodge-Tate weights of the quotient

Let $\gamma : \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$ be an embedding. A form of weight $u$ at $\gamma$ on a representation $T$ is a $\gamma$-semilinear $f : T \to \mathbb{C}_\ell$ with $f(g x) = \chi(g)^{-u} \, g f(x)$ for $g$ near $1$, with $\chi$ the cyclotomic character. The *jump* of $T$ at $(\gamma, u)$ is the dimension of the space of these forms. A vector $y \in \mathbb{C}_\ell \otimes V$ has Hodge-Tate weight $u$ if $(g \otimes \rho(g)) y = \chi(g)^{-u} y$.

Lean: `New.Period.jump` (Lib/PadicHodge/Crystalline/Basic.lean).

**Proposition (No jump gives a killing polynomial).** Let $(r, F)$ be an eigenquotient with each $\rho_i$ crystalline. Assume that the $T_i(s)$ commute with each other and with $\rho_i(\Gamma)$. Let $\gamma$ agree with an element $a$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ on $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, and assume that the jump of $r$ at $(\gamma, u)$ is $0$. Then there are scalars $c_L$, indexed by words in $\Sigma$, such that

1. $\sum_L c_L \prod_{s \in L} a(\theta(s)) = 1$, and
2. for every $i$, the operator $\sum_L c_L \prod_{s \in L} T_i(s)$ kills every vector of Hodge-Tate weight $u$ in $\mathbb{C}_\ell \otimes V_i$.

Lean: `New.Variety.IsEigenQuotient.exists_sum_baseChange_eq_zero_of_jump_eq_zero` (Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean).

The idea: the jump of $r$ at $u$ counts the weight-$u$ forms that factor through the eigenquotient. If there are none, then the weight-$u$ part of the cohomology has no eigenvector for $a \circ \theta$, and the lemma gives a Hecke operator that is $1$ at $\theta$ and $0$ on weight $u$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{W}$ be the space of families $(f_i)$ of weight-$u$ forms on the $V_i$. It is finite-dimensional over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. A word $L$ acts on it by $\Phi(L) f = (f_i \circ T_i(L))_i$. The result has weight $u$ because $T_i$ commutes with $\Gamma$, and the $\Phi([s])$ commute because the $T_i(s)$ do.

Suppose $f \in \mathcal{W}$ has $f_i \circ T_i(s) = a(\theta(s)) f_i = \gamma(\theta(s)) f_i$ for all $s$. Let $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ act on $\mathbb{C}_\ell$ through $\gamma$. Condition 4 then gives $\varphi$, $\gamma$-semilinear on $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^N$, with $f_i = \varphi \circ F_i$. The form $\varphi$ has weight $u$ for $r$, because its values on the spanning set $F_i(x)$ transform correctly. The jump is $0$, so $\varphi = 0$ and $f = 0$. So the lemma applies with $\eta = a \circ \theta$. It gives $c$ with property 1 and $\sum_L c_L \Phi(L) = 0$ on $\mathcal{W}$.

For property 2, let $y$ have weight $u$, and write $c(T) = \sum_L c_L T_i(L)$. A crystalline representation is Hodge-Tate, so $\mathbb{C}_\ell \otimes V_i$ has a basis of forms $b_l$, each of some weight $w_l$. It is enough to show that $b_l(c(T) y) = 0$ for all $l$. Write $b_l(c(T) y) = z_l(y)$ with $z_l = b_l \circ c(T)$, a form of weight $w_l$. If $w_l = u$, then $z_l$ is the $i$-th entry of $\sum_L c_L \Phi(L)$ applied to a family in $\mathcal{W}$, so $z_l = 0$. If $w_l \neq u$, the scalar $z_l(y) \in \mathbb{C}_\ell$ is fixed by an open subgroup after a twist by $\chi^{w_l - u}$. Tate's theorem that $\mathbb{C}_\ell(m)$ has no nonzero invariants under an open subgroup for $m \neq 0$ (Tate, *p-divisible groups*, Proceedings of a Conference on Local Fields, 1967, Section 3) gives $z_l(y) = 0$. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The jump is a `finrank`, so it is $0$ if the space of forms is infinite-dimensional. For the eigenquotient the space of forms is finite-dimensional, and jump $0$ means that no nonzero form exists. The proposition gives a combination of words, not a polynomial in a polynomial ring. This is the same thing for commuting operators, and it avoids the choice of an algebra generated by the $T_i(s)$. The proposition does not say that $u$ is a jump of some $V_i$. Section 6 uses it in the contrapositive: if $\theta$ occurs in weight $u$, then $u$ is a jump of $r$.
