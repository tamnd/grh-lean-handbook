## The group, the Bessel subgroup and the length

This section fixes the group $G_1$, the subgroup $S$ on whose left the Bessel functions transform, and a function $\ell$ on $S\backslash G_1$ that measures how far a point is from the origin. All decay statements of the chapter are statements in $\ell$, and the cut $W^\flat$ is a cut in $\ell$.

### The group and the frame

The group $G_1 = $ [`New.Shape.G₁`](/decl/New.Shape.G₁/) is defined in [the chapter on Bessel subgroups and the big cell](/chapter/bessel-bruhat/). It is the unitary group of a hermitian form $h$ on $\mathbb{C}^r$ with the basis $e_a, x_i, z, e^*_a$, for $a < \varrho$ and $i \in \kappa$. The form pairs $e_a$ with $e^*_a$, it is the identity on $X_0 = \operatorname{span}(x_i)$, and $h(z, z) = -1$. So $G_1 \cong U(\varrho + k, \varrho + 1)$ and $r = 2\varrho + k + 1$. The *frame* is the sequence $(f_0, \dots, f_\varrho) = (e_0, \dots, e_{\varrho-1}, z)$ of $n = \varrho + 1$ vectors. For $y \in G_1$, the vectors $y^{-1}f_a$ are the *moved frame*, written `frameVector y a` in the source.

The group $\mathsf U$ = [`besselU`](/decl/New.Shape.Form.besselU/) consists of the elements of $G_1$ that are block upper unitriangular for the levels of the basis, with the identity as the middle block on $X_0 \oplus \mathbb{C}z$. The group $H = U(k)$ acts on $X_0$ and fixes the other basis vectors, through the embedding $\iota = $ `embedH`. The Bessel subgroup $S$ = [`besselS`](/decl/New.Shape.Form.besselS/) is generated by $\mathsf U$ and $\iota(H)$. Each $s \in S$ maps every frame vector $f_a$ to $f_a$ plus a combination of $f_0, \dots, f_{a-1}$. The character $s \mapsto \operatorname{wave}(\sigma(s)) = \exp(2i\operatorname{Im}\sigma(s))$ of $S$, where $\sigma(s)$ is the sum of the entries of $s$ just above the diagonal of its frame block, is the one of [the section on Bessel functions and the Schur bound](/chapter/bessel-bruhat/#bessel-functions-and-the-schur-bound).

### The length

**Definition (Length).** For $y \in G_1$,
$$\ell(y) = \inf_{s \in S}\bigl(\|sy\| + 1\bigr),$$
with the operator norm of the matrix of $sy$.

Lean: `New.besselLength` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

So $\ell(y) \geq 1$, and $\ell$ is the distance from the coset $Sy$ to $0$ in the operator norm, plus one. The infimum is over a set of reals bounded below by $1$, so it is a real infimum in the usual sense and no junk value arises.

**Lemma.** Let $s \in S$ and $x, y \in G_1$. Then:

1. $\ell(sy) = \ell(y)$;
2. $\ell(y) \leq \|x^{-1}y\|\,\ell(x)$;
3. $\ell$ is continuous on $G_1$;
4. if $\ell(y) \leq T$, then some $u \in \mathsf U$ has $\|uy\| \leq T$.

Lean: `New.besselLength_besselS_mul`, `New.besselLength_le_norm_inv_mul_mul_besselLength`, `New.continuous_besselLength`, `New.RealPlace.exists_norm_le_of_besselLength_le` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* (1) Left multiplication by $s$ permutes $S$. (2) For $s \in S$, $\|sy\| \leq \|sx\|\,\|x^{-1}y\|$ and $1 \leq \|x^{-1}y\|$, so $\|sy\| + 1 \leq \|x^{-1}y\|(\|sx\| + 1)$. Take the infimum over $s$. The bound $1 \leq \|g\|$ holds for every $g \in G_1$: the Gram matrix $J_1$ is unitary, $g^{-1} = J_1 g^* J_1$, so $\|g^{-1}\| = \|g\|$ and $1 \leq \|g\|\,\|g^{-1}\| = \|g\|^2$. (3) By (2) applied in both directions, $\ell(x)$ lies between $\ell(y)/\|y^{-1}x\|$ and $\|y^{-1}x\|\,\ell(y)$, and both bounds tend to $\ell(y)$ as $x \to y$. (4) The infimum gives $s \in S$ with $\|sy\| + 1 < T + 1$. Write $s^{-1} = u\,\iota(w)$ with $u \in \mathsf U$ and $w \in U(k)$. Then $\iota(w)s = u^{-1}$, and $\iota(w)$ is unitary for the Euclidean product, so $\|u^{-1}y\| = \|sy\| < T$. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** Item 4 asks for $\ell(y) \leq T$ and gives $\|uy\| \leq T$. The proof gives the stronger $\|uy\| < T$, and it does not need a compactness argument, since a strict inequality in the infimum gives a point.

### Boxes

**Definition (Frame box).** For $\rho \in \mathbb{R}$, the set $\operatorname{Box}(\rho)$ consists of the $y \in G_1$ with $\|y^{-1}f_a\| \leq \rho$ for every frame vector $f_a$.

Lean: `New.frameBox` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

The box bounds only half of the columns of $y^{-1}$. The other half are bounded by a separate hypothesis.

**Lemma.** Let $1 \leq M$, let $y \in \operatorname{Box}(M)$, and suppose that the columns of $y^{-1}$ at the indices $e^*_a$ have norm at most $M$. Then $\|y\| + 1 \leq 8(\varrho + 1)M$.

Lean: `New.norm_add_one_le_of_mem_frameBox` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* Put $A = y^{-1}$, so $\|A\| = \|y\|$. The Euclidean product is the form $h$ plus a correction: $1 = J_1 + (1 - J_1)$, and $1 - J_1$ is twice the projection onto the span of the vectors $z$ and $e_a - e^*_a$, on which $h$ is negative. Since $A J_1 A^* = J_1$, this gives $\|A\|^2 \leq 1 + \|A(1 - J_1)\|\,\|A\|$. The columns of $1 - J_1$ are $0$, $2z$ and $\pm(e_a - e^*_a)$, so the columns of $A(1 - J_1)$ are bounded by $2M$ by the two hypotheses. Divide by $\|A\| \geq 1$. Then $\|A\| + 1 \leq 1 + (4\varrho + 2)M + 1 \leq 8(\varrho + 1)M$, as $M \geq 1$. $\square$

### Gram-Schmidt for the moved frame

Apply the Gram-Schmidt process, for the Euclidean product, to the moved frame $y^{-1}f_0, \dots, y^{-1}f_\varrho$. The result is a sequence of pairwise orthogonal vectors $\omega_0(y), \dots, \omega_\varrho(y)$. They are not normalized.

**Definition.** $\omega_a(y)$ is the $a$-th Gram-Schmidt vector of the moved frame at $y$.

Lean: `New.orthoFrame` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

**Proposition (The length and the frame).** Let $y \in G_1$.

1. For $s \in S$, $\omega_a(sy) = \omega_a(y)$ for all $a$.
2. If $\ell(y) \leq M$, then $M^{-1} \leq \|\omega_a(y)\| \leq M$ for all $a$.
3. If $1 \leq M$ and $M^{-1} \leq \|\omega_a(y)\| \leq M$ for all $a$, then $\ell(y) \leq 8(\varrho + 1)M$.

Lean: `New.orthoFrame_besselS_mul`, `New.norm_orthoFrame_mem_Icc`, `New.besselLength_le_of_norm_orthoFrame` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* (1) The moved frame at $sy$ is $y^{-1}s^{-1}f_a$, and $s^{-1}f_a = f_a + \sum_{c < a} t_c f_c$. So the moved frame at $sy$ differs from the one at $y$ by an upper unitriangular change of basis, and Gram-Schmidt does not see such a change. (2) Item 4 of the lemma gives $u \in \mathsf U$ with $\|uy\| \leq M$, and by (1) $\omega_a(y) = \omega_a(uy)$. The Gram-Schmidt process does not make vectors longer, so $\|\omega_a\| \leq \|(uy)^{-1}f_a\| \leq \|(uy)^{-1}\| = \|uy\| \leq M$. For the lower bound, the source proves $1 \leq \|uy\|\,\|\omega_a(uy)\|$. The vector $\omega_a$ is $(uy)^{-1}f_a$ minus a combination of the earlier moved frame vectors. Pair it by $h$ with $(uy)^{-1}e^*_a$ for $a < \varrho$, or with $(uy)^{-1}z$ for $a = \varrho$. The earlier frame vectors pair to $0$, and $f_a$ pairs to $1$ or to $-1$. Since $J_1$ is unitary, $|h(v, w)| \leq \|v\|\,\|w\|$, and the second vector has norm at most $\|uy\|$. (3) The source finds $u \in \mathsf U$ such that the moved frame at $uy$ is the orthogonal family $\omega_a(y)$. The vectors $J_1\omega_a/\|\omega_a\|^2$ are a dual system of length $\|\omega_a\|^{-1} \leq M$. Such a system is the image of $(e^*_a)$ under $(uy)^{-1}u_q$ for a unipotent element $u_q \in \mathsf U$. The lemma on boxes, applied to $u_q^{-1}uy$, bounds $\ell(y) \leq \|u_q^{-1}uy\| + 1$. $\square$

The third item has a form that asks for a dual system instead of orthogonal vectors. It follows from the third item.

**Lemma (Bounded dual).** Let $1 \leq M$ and let $V_0, \dots, V_{2\varrho}$ be vectors with $V_a = y^{-1}f_a$ for $a \leq \varrho$, $\|V_a\| \leq M$ for all $a$, and $h(V_a, V_c) = -1$ if $a + c = 2\varrho$ and $0$ if $a + c < 2\varrho$. Then $\ell(y) \leq 8(\varrho + 1)M$.

Lean: `New.besselLength_le_of_dual` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* The vector $\omega_a(y)$ is $V_a$ minus its projection onto the span of $V_0, \dots, V_{a-1}$. So $\|\omega_a\| \leq \|V_a\| \leq M$. The vectors $V_c$ with $c < a$ pair to $0$ with $V_{2\varrho-a}$, so $h(\omega_a, V_{2\varrho-a}) = h(V_a, V_{2\varrho-a}) = -1$, and $1 \leq \|\omega_a\|\,M$. Now item 3 of the proposition applies. $\square$

### The cut region and the scaled length

The cut $W^\flat$ lives on the points of length at most $b^{6n+2}$. The decay of $W$ is measured in the length divided by $b^{6n}$.

**Definition.** Let $b \in \mathbb{R}$ and $y \in G_1$.

1. $y$ is *in the cut* if $\ell(y) \leq b^{6n+2}$.
2. The *scaled length* is $\ell_b(y) = \max(1, \ell(y)/b^{6n})$.

Lean: `New.InCut`, `New.scaledBesselLength` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

**Lemma.** If $2 \leq b$ and $\ell(y) > b^{6n+2}/2$, then $\ell_b(y) \geq b$.

Lean: `New.le_scaledBesselLength` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* $\ell(y)/b^{6n} > b^2/2 \geq b$, since $b \geq 2$. $\square$

**Example.** Take $\varrho = 0$. Then the frame is $(z)$, $n = 1$, $\mathsf U$ is trivial, and $S = \iota(H)$ is the compact group $U(k)$. So $\ell(y) = \|y\| + 1$, the group is $U(k, 1)$, and the cut region is the set $\|y\| + 1 \leq b^8$, a compact set. The Gram-Schmidt vector $\omega_0(y) = y^{-1}z$ has $h(\omega_0, \omega_0) = -1$, so $\|\omega_0\| \geq 1$. Item 2 of the proposition gives $\|\omega_0(y)\| \leq \ell(y)$. In the source $\varrho \geq 10^5$, so this case does not occur in the argument, but it shows the meaning of the definitions.

### The group R and its Haar measure

The averages of the next section are integrals over a group $R$ that maps onto $S$. The group $R$ is the semidirect product $\mathsf U \rtimes H$, realized as a graph.

**Definition.** $R$ is the subgroup of $G_1 \times U(k)$ of the pairs $(g, h)$ with $\iota(h)^{-1}g \in \mathsf U$. The chart $(u, h) \mapsto (\iota(h)u, h)$ is a homeomorphism $\mathsf U \times U(k) \to R$. The measure $ds = du\,dh$ on $R$ is the image of the product of the Haar measure on $\mathsf U$ and the Haar probability measure on $U(k)$.

Lean: `New.Shape.besselGraph`, `New.Shape.besselGraphChart`, `New.Shape.besselGraphMeasure` (New/Spanning/Bessel/Average.lean).

The file Average.lean proves that $du\,dh$ is a Haar measure on $R$, invariant on the left and on the right, and that it is positive on open sets and finite on compact sets. These are instances in the source. Right invariance holds because $\mathsf U$ is unimodular and $H$ is compact, so the modular function of $R$ is trivial.

**Remark (Formalization).** The map $(g, h) \mapsto g$ from $R$ onto $S$ has the kernel $\{(1, h) : \iota(h) \in \mathsf U\}$. It is trivial, because $\mathsf U$ has the identity as its middle block and $\iota(h)$ acts by $h$ on $X_0$. So $R$ and $S$ are isomorphic as groups. In the graph the component $h$ of $s = (g, h)$ is a coordinate of $s$, so the representation $\pi(s) = \operatorname{wave}(\sigma(g))\,\rho(h)$ of the next section is defined on $R$ with no decomposition of $g$. The modular function of $R$ is trivial, as $\mathsf U$ is unimodular and $H$ is compact, and this is what the left and right invariance of $du\,dh$ express.
