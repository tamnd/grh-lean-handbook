## What the chapter proves

This chapter is the first chapter of layer 4 of the proof of Theorem B. It takes one term $\gamma$ of an amplitude sum and takes it apart. It defines the numbers and the spaces that the later chapters attach to $\gamma$: the matrix of frame products, the discriminant, the cell rank, the link, the gauges and two lattices. It proves the facts about these objects that the bounds of layer 4 use. It does not bound a sum of amplitudes. The bounds are in [the chapter on the remaining terms](/chapter/remaining-terms/), [the chapter on the main term](/chapter/main-term/) and [the chapter on the moments](/chapter/moments/).

The place of the chapter in the whole argument is shown in [the theorem and the plan](/chapter/theorem-and-plan/). Its place in the proof of Theorem B is shown in [Theorem B and its plan](/chapter/theorem-b-plan/). That chapter describes the four layers. Layer 3 unfolds the Hilbert-Schmidt norms of the translates of a Poincaré operator into sums $\mathcal{A}(c, s, c')$ over blocks. Layer 4 bounds these sums term by term. The sizes of the data are fixed in [the data and its sizes](/chapter/data-and-sizes/).

### The three kinds of term

Fix admissible filling data $S$ over a CM field $E$ ([Definition](/decl/New.Filling/) and [Definition](/decl/New.Filling.IsAdmissible/)). A *term* is a rational element $\gamma$ of the unitary group $G$ of the hermitian space $V$. A term lies over a block $(c, s, c')$ if $h_c^{-1}\gamma h_{c'}$ is integral and has the reduction $s$. Here $c, c'$ are classes, $h_c$ is a chosen representative of $c$ and $s$ lies in the finite group $\bar G$ of the reductions at the chosen places. [Theorem B and its plan](/chapter/theorem-b-plan/#unfolding-and-bounds) sorts the terms into three kinds. This chapter gives the tests that separate them.

1. *Identity terms.* The term $\gamma$ lies in the Bessel subgroup $R_0$ (`besselS`), the group generated by the unipotent group $\mathsf{U}$ and $H = U(X_0)$. [Lemma](/decl/New.Filling.eq_and_mem_classUnipotent_of_besselS/) shows that such a term over $(c, s, c')$ with $s = 1$ has $c = c'$ and lies in $\mathsf{U}$. So the identity terms are the integral points of the unipotent group, and they give the diagonal contribution.
2. *Main terms.* The discriminant $\mathsf{g}(\gamma) = |D_{\varrho+1}|^2 - N^2|D_\varrho|^2$ of the frame products is not zero. [Lemma](/decl/New.Filling.hasCellRank_last/) shows that these are exactly the terms of the top cell rank $\varrho + 1$. On the big cell the discriminant and the link have closed forms ([Theorem](/decl/New.Filling.frameDisc_weylElem_mul/) and [Theorem](/decl/New.Filling.link_weylElem_mul_eq_iff/)).
3. *Remaining terms.* All other terms. A remaining term with a cell rank $j \leq \varrho$ has $\mathsf{g}(\gamma) = 0$, and [Theorem](/decl/New.Filling.termDisc_eq_zero_iff/) says that then the frame and its image share a nonzero vector.

The three kinds are not the only data of a term. Each term also has a size. The size depends on the cell rank and on a gauge, and it is the number $\tau^2(\mathcal{D}_j/(n_p(M^{2j'f}x)^k))^{1/2}$ of [Definition](/decl/New.Filling.orbitBound/). The chapter on the remaining terms adds these sizes over the near terms.

### A sketch

The chapter has six more sections.

[The terms of a block](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#the-terms-of-a-block) sets up the frame $\mathfrak{f} = (e_0, \dots, e_{\varrho-1}, z)$, the matrix $Z(\gamma)$ of the products $h(\mathfrak{f}_a, \gamma\mathfrak{f}_b)$, its leading minors $D_l$ and the discriminant $\mathsf{g}(\gamma)$. It proves that $\mathsf{g}(\gamma)$ is totally nonnegative.

[The level structure of the Bessel data](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#the-level-structure-of-the-bessel-data) relates the rational points of $\mathsf{U}$ to the finite group $\bar G$.

[Cells and the discriminant](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#cells-and-the-discriminant) defines the cell rank. It proves that the leading minors, the discriminant and the cell rank do not change under $\gamma \mapsto u^{-1}\gamma u'$ for $u, u' \in \mathsf{U}$. It also proves that a term has at most one cell rank, and that $\mathsf{g}(\gamma) = 0$ exactly when the spans of $\mathfrak{f}$ and $\gamma\mathfrak{f}$ meet.

[The link](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#the-link) defines the link of $\gamma$, a space of dimension $k + 1$ of pairs of middle vectors. The link is the graph of an isometry $\Upsilon_\gamma$ of the positive space $X_0 \perp Ez^+$.

[The box and the size of a near term](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#the-box-and-the-size-of-a-near-term) defines the boxed points and the near terms. A near term has frame products of size at most $b^{134n}$. Its orbit bound at the top rank is at least $b^{-k^3f}$.

[Siegel's lemma and the gauges of a term](/chapter/anatomy-of-a-term/#siegels-lemma-and-the-gauges-of-a-term) proves a form of Siegel's lemma and two convergent sums over subspaces and sublattices. It then defines the gauges and the lattices of a term, the data that the count of the remaining terms uses.

### Notation

The notation follows [Theorem B and its plan](/chapter/theorem-b-plan/) and [the chapter on frames, depth and gains](/chapter/local-frames/). The space $V$ has the basis $e_0, \dots, e_{\varrho-1}$, $x_1, \dots, x_k$, $z$, $e^*_{\varrho-1}, \dots, e^*_0$. The vector $e_a$ has level $a$, the middle vectors $x_i$ and $z$ have level $\varrho$, and $e^*_a$ has level $2\varrho - a$. The form is $h(v, w) = v^*Jw$, with $h(e_a, e^*_b) = \delta_{ab}$, the vectors $e_a$ and $e^*_a$ isotropic, $h(x_i, x_j) = \delta_{ij}$ and $h(z, z) = -N$ for the prime $N$ of the data. The middle space is $X_0 \perp Ez$, with $X_0$ spanned by the $x_i$. Write $A$ for the span of $e_0, \dots, e_{\varrho-1}$. The frame is $\mathfrak{f} = (\mathfrak{f}_0, \dots, \mathfrak{f}_\varrho) = (e_0, \dots, e_{\varrho-1}, z)$, and $n = \varrho + 1$. We write $f$ for the number of infinite places of $E$, $k$ for the rank of $X_0$, $M = p_0p_1$ for the level and $b$ for the base of the sizes. Some docstrings of the source index the frame from $1$. This chapter indexes it from $0$, as the source does in the statements.

The admissible data satisfy $10^5 \leq \varrho$, $1000 \leq b$, $1000(2\varrho + 2)^9 \leq k$, $b^{19} \leq M \leq b^{23}$, $N \leq b^{60n}$ and $1 \leq |\kappa_v| \leq b^{23}$ at each infinite place $v$. The number $\tau = b^{kf/4}$ (`tailScale`) is the unit in which the losses are counted. [Lemma](/decl/New.Filling.pow_le_tailScale/) says that $b^e \leq \tau$ for every $e \leq 10^5n^9f$. So any power of $b$ with an exponent polynomial of degree at most $9$ in $n$ is negligible against $\tau$.

**Lemma (Powers of the base are small against the tail scale).** Let $S$ be admissible and let $e \leq 10^5n^9f$. Then $b^e \leq \tau$.

Lean: `New.Filling.pow_le_tailScale` (New/Spanning/Unfolding/Terms.lean).

*Proof.* Since $b \geq 1$ it is enough that $e \leq kf/4$. The data give $1000(2\varrho + 2)^9 \leq k$, that is $1000 \cdot 2^9 n^9 \leq k$. So $kf/4 \geq 128000\, n^9 f \geq 10^5 n^9 f \geq e$. $\square$
