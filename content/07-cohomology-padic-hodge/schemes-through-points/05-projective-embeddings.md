## Projective embeddings

A scheme over $S$ is projective if it is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^n_S$. The source does not take this as the definition. It defines a projective embedding through points: a proper morphism with a linear system whose coordinates separate the points of each chart. Then it proves that such an embedding is a closed immersion into projective space. This section gives the definition, the closed immersion, the affine charts, and the Segre and Veronese constructions. At the end it gives the class `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding`, which is a hypothesis of the cohomology of Volume 7.

### The definition

**Definition (Projective embedding).** Let $s \colon X \to S$ be a morphism and $L = (U_i, g_{ij})$ a line bundle on $X$. Then $L$ is a *projective embedding* of $X$ over $S$ if

1. $L$ is a linear system;
2. $s$ is proper;
3. two points $a, b \colon \operatorname{Spec} A \to X$ that land in one chart $U_i$, with $g_{ij}(A, a) = g_{ij}(A, b)$ for all $j$ and $s \circ a = s \circ b$, are equal.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

The third condition says that the affine coordinates $x_j / x_i$ and the image in $S$ determine a point of $U_i$. Classically, this is the statement that $U_i \to \mathbb{A}^{\iota}_S$ is a monomorphism. The index set $\iota$ can be infinite in the definition. Most theorems need it finite.

**Theorem.** Under a projective embedding, two points with the same image in $S$ and the same vector $v$ of homogeneous coordinates are equal, if one of them lands in some chart.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.eq_of_isHomogeneousCoords` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* If $a$ lands in $U_i$, then $v_i$ is a unit, so the other point lands in $U_i$ too. Both have the affine coordinates $v_j v_i^{-1}$. $\square$

The hypothesis that one point lands in a chart holds when $A$ is a local ring. In general a point lands in no single chart, and the theorem says nothing about it. The gluing theorems of Section 2 handle such points through a cover.

**Theorem.** Projective embeddings are stable under base change. If $L$ embeds $X$ over $S$ and $Y = X \times_S T$, then the pull-back of $L$ to $Y$ embeds $Y$ over $T$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.of_isPullback` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

**Theorem.** For a finite $\kappa$, $\mathbb{P}^\kappa$ is proper over $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$. For every scheme $S$, the pull-back of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ to $\mathbb{P}^\kappa \times S$ is a projective embedding over $S$ for the second projection.

Lean: `New.ProjSpace.isProper_specZIsTerminal_from`, `New.isProjEmbedding_projSpace` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a graded ring finitely generated over its degree $0$ part is proper over $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the degree $0$ part, and the proof finds this by instance search. Here the degree $0$ part is $\mathbb{Z}$. For the separation of points, the chart $D_+(X_i)$ is affine. A point of it is a ring map from its coordinate ring, and that ring is generated by the fractions $X_j / X_i$. So the values of the $x_{ij}$ at a point determine it. The case of a general $S$ is the base change from $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$, which is terminal. $\square$

### Properties

**Theorem.** Let $L$ embed $X$ over $S$.

1. Two morphisms $a, b \colon Y \to X$ with $a^* L = b^* L$, as line bundles, and $s \circ a = s \circ b$ are equal.
2. If $p \colon Y \to X$ is a closed immersion, then $p^* L$ embeds $Y$ over $S$.
3. If $N$ embeds $S$ over $T$ along $b$, then $L \otimes s^* N$ embeds $X$ over $T$ along $b \circ s$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.ext`, `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.comp`, `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.mul` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* (1) Test on points of each chart of $a^* L$, by `New.hom_ext_of_isOpenCover`. (2) A closed immersion is proper and a monomorphism, so the third condition passes to $Y$. (3) Composites of proper morphisms are proper. In the chart $(i, k)$, the coordinate $(j, k)$ of the product is the coordinate $j$ of $L$, since $g^N_{kk} = 1$. In the same way the coordinate $(i, j)$ is the coordinate $j$ of $N$. So two points with the same coordinates have the same image in $S$, and then they are equal. $\square$

**Theorem (Closed immersion).** Let $L$ embed $X$ over $S$, with $\iota$ finite. Then there is a closed immersion $j \colon X \to \mathbb{P}^\iota \times S$ with $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ j = s$ and $j^* \operatorname{pr}_1^* \mathcal{O}(1) = L$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.exists_isClosedImmersion` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* By Section 4, the linear system $L$ is the pull-back of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ along some $j$ over $S$. The composite $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ j = s$ is proper and $\operatorname{pr}_2$ is separated, so $j$ is proper. If $j \circ a = j \circ b$, then $a$ and $b$ pull $L$ back to the same line bundle and agree over $S$, so $a = b$ by the first property. So $j$ is a monomorphism. A proper monomorphism is a closed immersion (EGA IV, Corollaire 18.12.6). The proof cites this as `IsClosedImmersion.iff_isProper_and_mono`, which the source does not define, so it comes from a library. $\square$

So, with finitely many coordinates, a projective embedding in the sense of the source makes $X$ a closed subscheme of projective space. The converse holds by the second property, applied to $\mathbb{P}^\iota \times S$.

**Theorem (Affine charts).** Let $L$ embed $X$ over $\operatorname{Spec} R$, with $\iota$ finite. Then each chart $U_i$ is affine, and there is a surjective ring map $\varphi \colon R[X_j : j \in \iota] \to \Gamma(X, U_i)$ such that

1. $\varphi$ on constants induces the structure map $U_i \to \operatorname{Spec} R$;
2. for each point $t$ of $U_i$ and each polynomial $P$, the value of the section $\varphi(P)$ at $t$ is $P$ evaluated at the coordinates $X_j = g_{ij}(A, t)$, with the coefficients mapped to $A$ by the ring map that induces $s \circ t$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.exists_affine_chart` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* By the closed immersion, $U_i$ is the inverse image of the affine chart $D_+(X_i) \times \operatorname{Spec} R$, so it is affine. The map $\varphi$ sends $X_j$ to the coordinate $g_{ij}$ at the tautological point of $U_i$. For the surjectivity, sections over $U_i$ lift to sections over the chart of projective space, because a closed immersion is surjective on sections over affines. A section over the chart is a polynomial in the $X_j / X_i$. $\square$

This is the classical statement that the affine chart $X \cap D_+(x_i)$ has coordinate ring $R[x_j / x_i] / I$. It is used in `Lib/General/Geometry/SerreTheorem.lean`, `Lib/Shimura/Uniformisation/HolomorphicFamilies.lean` and `Lib/Shimura/ThetaModuli/FlatClosure.lean`.

### Segre and Veronese

**Definition (Products and powers).** Let $\Lambda$ be finite and $(N_l)_{l \in \Lambda}$ line bundles on $X$, where $N_l$ has the index set $\nu_l$. The product $\prod_l N_l$ has index set $\prod_l \nu_l$, opens $\bigcap_l (N_l)_{c_l}$ for $c \in \prod_l \nu_l$, and transition functions $\prod_l g^{N_l}_{c_l d_l}$. The power $L^{\otimes m}$ is the product of $m$ copies of $L$, with the index set of $m$-tuples $\iota^m$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.pi`, `New.LineBundle.pow` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

`New.LineBundle.pi` has an optional argument $s \subseteq \Lambda$ for the factors in the transition function. The opens always run over all of $\Lambda$. The default is $s = \Lambda$. In this file the general case is used for the induction in the next theorem.

**Theorem.** $[\prod_{l \in s} N_l] = \sum_{l \in s} [N_l]$ and $[L^{\otimes m}] = m [L]$. A product of linear systems is a linear system.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.picClass_pi`, `New.LineBundle.picClass_pow`, `New.LineBundle.IsLinearSystem.pi` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* Induction on $s$, with the refinement theorem of Section 3 at each step. For the linear system, a product is a unit if and only if each factor is one, and the coordinates multiply factor by factor. $\square$

**Theorem (Segre).** Let $W$ be proper over $S$, and let $\pi_l \colon W \to Z_l$, for $l$ in a finite set $\Lambda$, be morphisms over $S$ that jointly separate morphisms: if $\pi_l \circ x = \pi_l \circ y$ for all $l$, then $x = y$. If each $N_l$ embeds $Z_l$ over $S$, then $\prod_l \pi_l^* N_l$ embeds $W$ over $S$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.isProjEmbedding_pi` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* In the chart $c$, the Segre coordinate with index equal to $c$ except at $l$, where it is $j$, is the coordinate $j$ of the factor $l$, since the other factors are $g_{c_{l'} c_{l'}} = 1$. So two points with the same Segre coordinates have the same image in each $Z_l$, and the separation hypothesis makes them equal. $\square$

Classically, this is the Segre embedding $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m \to \mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1) - 1}$ (Hartshorne, Chapter I, Exercise 2.14), applied to a closed subscheme of a product. The separation hypothesis holds, for instance, for the projections of a fibre product.

**Theorem (Veronese).** If $L$ embeds $X$ over $S$ and $m \neq 0$, then $L^{\otimes m}$ embeds $X$ over $S$.

Lean: `New.LineBundle.IsProjEmbedding.pow` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

*Proof.* The Segre theorem with $W = Z_l = X$ and all $\pi_l$ the identity. The family of identities separates morphisms because $m \neq 0$. $\square$

Classically, this is the $d$-uple embedding (Hartshorne, Chapter I, Exercise 2.12).

**Remark (Formalization).** The coordinates of $L^{\otimes m}$ are indexed by ordered $m$-tuples in $\iota^m$, not by monomials of degree $m$. So there are $|\iota|^m$ coordinates, and the coordinates for two tuples that differ by a permutation are equal functions. The docstring of `New.LineBundle.pow` calls this "the Veronese system of all monomials of degree m". That is true up to these repetitions. The embedding of the source is the $m$-uple embedding followed by a linear map into a larger projective space, which repeats coordinates. The repetition does not harm the results: the statement on spans in Section 6 is about a span, and repeated vectors do not change a span.

### The class of projective schemes

**Definition (Has a projective embedding).** A morphism $s \colon X \to S$ *has a projective embedding* if some line bundle with a finite index set is a projective embedding of $X$ over $S$.

Lean: `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding` (Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean).

It is a `class inductive` with one constructor, so it is a proposition that type class search can find. By the closed immersion theorem, it says that $X$ is a closed subscheme of a projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_S$, and so that $X$ is projective over $S$ in the sense of Grothendieck. A morphism with a projective embedding is proper; the source has this as an instance.

**Theorem.** A finite product of proper schemes in the category of schemes over $S$ is proper over $S$. If $W$ has a closed immersion over $S$ into a finite fibre product $\prod_S A_l$, and each $A_l$ has a projective embedding over $S$, then $W$ has a projective embedding over $S$. If $A$ has a projective embedding over $X$ and $X$ has one over $S$, then $A$ has one over $S$.

Lean: `New.isProper_pi`, `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding.of_isClosedImmersion_pi` (Lib/General/Geometry/SerreTheorem.lean).

*Proof.* The first statement forms the product in the category of proper schemes over $S$ and uses that the forgetful functor preserves it. For the second, the Segre theorem embeds the product, and the closed immersion property restricts the embedding to $W$. The third is an instance in the same file, and it uses the tensor product of the two embeddings. $\square$

The second statement is how the source makes its projective schemes. `Lib/Shimura/IntegralModel/UniversalFamily.lean` declares instances of `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding` for fibre powers of the universal family and for graph closures, and both come from `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding.of_isClosedImmersion_pi`.

**Remark (Formalization).** `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding` is the hypothesis `h_proj` of the theorem of the Woods Hole type in `Lib/Cohomology/WoodsHole.lean`, and it is the field `projective` of the model in `Lib/Shimura/HeckeTower/Model.lean`, as Section 1 says. The name is in the namespace `New.Variety`, but the class is declared in `Lib/General/Geometry/ProjectiveSpace.lean`. The proof of `New.Variety.HasProjEmbedding.of_isClosedImmersion_pi` uses `choose` to pick the index sets and line bundles of the embeddings of the factors. The result is a proposition, so the choice does not appear in it.
