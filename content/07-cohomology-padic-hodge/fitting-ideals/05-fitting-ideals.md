## Fitting ideals under added relations

This section gives the zeroth Fitting ideal and the main theorem of the chapter. The theorem says how much the Fitting ideal of a quotient $R^\iota / N$ can shrink when relations are added to $N$: if the added relations are forced by linear forms modulo $p$, the cost is an element $c \in O$ whose valuation is at most a length of a module of such forms. In Ribet's method the module of forms is a module of cocycles, its length is bounded by a Selmer group, and the theorem turns that bound into an element of a Fitting ideal of a Hecke algebra. The use is in [the Ribet elements](/chapter/ribet-elements/) of Volume 12.

### The classical Fitting ideal

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ a finitely presented $R$-module, with a presentation $R^m \xrightarrow{\ A\ } R^\iota \to M \to 0$ and $\iota$ finite. The zeroth Fitting ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)$ is the ideal generated by the $\iota \times \iota$ minors of the matrix $A$. It does not depend on the presentation (Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra with a View Toward Algebraic Geometry*, Section 20.2). It has three properties that matter here.

1. *Base change.* For a ring map $R \to R'$, $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M \otimes_R R') = \operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) R'$ (The Stacks Project, Tag 07ZA).
2. *Products.* For an exact sequence $M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$, $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M') \operatorname{Fitt}_0(M'') \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)$ (Mazur and Wiles, *Class fields of abelian extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$*, Invent. Math. 76 (1984), Appendix).
3. *Length.* Over a discrete valuation ring $O$, if $M$ has finite length, then $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) = \varpi^{\operatorname{length}(M)} O$. This follows from the theorem on elementary divisors: for $M = \prod_i O/\varpi^{a_i}$ the relation matrix is diagonal.

The source proves the cases of these properties that it needs, for a fixed presentation.

**Definition (Fitting ideal).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $\iota$ a finite set, and $N$ a submodule of $R^\iota$. The Fitting ideal of $N$ is the ideal of $R$ generated by the determinants of all $\iota \times \iota$ matrices whose rows lie in $N$:
$$\operatorname{Fitt}(N) = \bigl( \det B : B \in M_\iota(R),\ B_i \in N \text{ for all } i \bigr).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.fittingIdeal` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ is the classical $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(R^\iota / N)$. Take any family of generators of $N$ as the rows of a relation matrix. Each $\iota \times \iota$ minor of that matrix is the determinant of a square matrix with rows in $N$. Conversely, the rows of a square matrix with rows in $N$ are combinations of the generators, so by multilinearity its determinant is a combination of minors. The source never states this comparison, and it never proves that the ideal depends only on the module $R^\iota / N$. Its statements fix $R^\iota$ and compare submodules $N \subseteq K$ of the same free module, so the argument does not need the independence of the presentation.

### Three rules

**Lemma (Base change, one inclusion).** For every ideal $I$ of $R$,
$$\operatorname{Fitt}(N + I R^\iota) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N) + I .$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.sup_smul_top_le` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* Let $B$ have rows in $N + I R^\iota$, and write row $i$ as $n_i + m_i$ with $n_i \in N$ and $m_i \in I R^\iota$. Modulo $I$ the matrix $B$ is congruent to the matrix with rows $n_i$. So $\det B$ is congruent modulo $I$ to a determinant of a matrix with rows in $N$. $\square$

The module $R^\iota / (N + I R^\iota)$ is $M \otimes_R R/I$. So the lemma is the inclusion $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M \otimes R/I) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) + I$ of property 1, read inside $R$.

**Lemma.** Let $r \in R$ and $v \in R^\iota$ with $r v \in N$. Let $s$ be a square matrix whose rows each lie in $N$ or are equal to $v$. Then $r \det s \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.mul_det_mem_fittingIdeal` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* If all rows lie in $N$, this is the definition. If two rows equal $v$, then $\det s = 0$. If exactly one row equals $v$, multiply that row by $r$. The new matrix has all rows in $N$ and determinant $r \det s$. $\square$

**Proposition (Adding one vector).** Let $r \in R$ and $v \in R^\iota$ with $r v \in N$. Then $r d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ for every $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N + R v)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.mul_mem_of_smul_mem` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* It suffices to take $d = \det B$ with rows in $N + R v$. Write each row as a finite combination of elements of $N$ and of $v$. By multilinearity, $\det B$ is a combination of determinants of matrices $s$ whose rows lie in $N$ or are equal to $v$. Apply the lemma to each term. $\square$

This is property 2 for the sequence $R/(r) \to R^\iota / N \to R^\iota / (N + R v) \to 0$, where the first map sends $1$ to the class of $v$, since $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(R/(r)) = (r)$. Iterated along a filtration it gives the next result.

**Corollary.** Let $J$ be a linearly ordered set, $I \subseteq J$ finite, and $v_j \in R^\iota$, $r_j \in R$ for $j \in J$. Suppose that for each $j \in I$ and each submodule $K \supseteq N$ that contains $v_i$ for all $i \in I$ with $i < j$, the vector $r_j v_j$ lies in $K$. Then there is a submodule $K \supseteq N$ that contains $v_j$ for all $j \in I$ and has
$$d \prod_{j \in I} r_j \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N) \quad \text{for every } d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(K).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_ge_mul_prod_mem` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* Induct on $I$, adding its largest element $e$ at each step. The induction hypothesis gives $K$ for $I \setminus \{e\}$. By hypothesis $r_e v_e \in K$, and the proposition passes from $K + R v_e$ to $K$ at the cost $r_e$. $\square$

### Linear forms modulo $p$

The main theorem measures the cost of added relations by linear forms. Fix a discrete valuation ring $O$, a commutative $O$-algebra $R$, finite sets $n$ and $\iota$, an $O$-linear map $\Phi \colon O^n \to R^\iota$, a set $G$ and a family $z \colon G \to O^n$. In the application $G$ is a Galois group, $z(g)$ is the vector of a universal cocycle at $g$, and $\Phi$ sends it to a row over a Hecke algebra.

**Definition (Forms modulo $p$).** For $N \subseteq R^\iota$ and $p \in O$, let $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$ be the $O$-module of functions $G \to O/p$ of the form $g \mapsto \Psi(z(g))$, where $\Psi \colon O^n \to O/p$ runs over the $O$-linear maps that vanish on $\Phi^{-1}(N)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.formsMod` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean). The source writes the values in $O/p$ as vectors of length one. Membership is `New.Selmer.mem_formsMod`. If $N \subseteq N'$, then $\mathcal{F}(N', p) \subseteq \mathcal{F}(N, p)$ (`New.Selmer.formsMod_anti`).

**Lemma (Duality).** Let $p \neq 0$ and $\Phi(p O^n) \subseteq N$. If every $O$-linear $\Psi \colon O^n \to O/p$ that vanishes on $\Phi^{-1}(N)$ vanishes at $v$, then $\Phi(v) \in N$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.mem_of_forall_form_eq_zero` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* The lattice $L = \Phi^{-1}(N)$ contains $p O^n$. By the duality lemma of Section 4, `New.exists_matrix_addVal_det_le_length`, $L$ is the annihilator of a module $S$ of vectors modulo $p$. Each $w \in S$ gives the form $u \mapsto \sum_j u_j w_j$, which vanishes on $L$. By hypothesis it vanishes at $v$, so $v \in L$. $\square$

**Lemma (A strict drop).** Let $p \neq 0$, $\Phi(p O^n) \subseteq N \subseteq N'$, and let $V$ be a submodule of the functions $G \to O/p$. Let $v \in O^n$ be killed by every $O$-linear $\Psi \colon O^n \to O/p$ with $\Psi \circ z \in V$. If $\Phi(a v) \in N'$ and $\Phi(a v) \notin N$ for some $a \in O$, then the image of $\mathcal{F}(N', p)$ modulo $V$ is strictly smaller than the image of $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$ modulo $V$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.map_mkQ_formsMod_lt` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* The inclusion holds since $\mathcal{F}$ reverses inclusions. Suppose the images are equal. Let $\Psi$ vanish on $\Phi^{-1}(N)$. Then some $\Psi'$ that vanishes on $\Phi^{-1}(N')$ has $\Psi' \circ z - \Psi \circ z \in V$. So $\Psi' - \Psi$ kills $v$, and $\Psi(a v) = \Psi'(a v) = 0$, because $\Phi(a v) \in N'$. So every such $\Psi$ kills $a v$, and duality gives $\Phi(a v) \in N$. This contradicts the hypothesis. $\square$

### The main theorem

**Theorem.** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring, $R$ a Noetherian commutative $O$-algebra, $\Phi \colon O^n \to R^\iota$ an $O$-linear map, $z \colon G \to O^n$, $N \subseteq R^\iota$ a submodule, and $p \neq 0$ in $O$ with $\Phi(p v) \in N$ for all $v \in O^n$. Let $V$ be any $O$-submodule of the functions $G \to O/p$. Then there are $c \in O$ and a submodule $K \supseteq N$ of $R^\iota$ such that:

1. $v(c) \leq \operatorname{len}\bigl(\mathcal{F}(N, p) \bmod V\bigr)$;
2. $\Phi(v) \in K$ for every $v \in O^n$ that is killed by all $O$-linear $\Psi \colon O^n \to O/p$ with $\Psi \circ z \in V$;
3. $c\, d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ for every $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(K)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_addVal_le_relLength` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

In words: one may add to $N$ all relations $\Phi(v)$ that the condition $V$ forces. The price in the Fitting ideal is a factor $c$, and $v(c)$ is at most the length of the forms modulo the condition. If $V$ already contains all forms, the length is $0$, and $c$ is a unit.

*Proof.* The ring $R$ is Noetherian and $\iota$ is finite, so $R^\iota$ is a Noetherian module, and one can argue by Noetherian induction on $N$: assume the theorem for every submodule strictly larger than $N$. If every $v$ as in 2 already has $\Phi(v) \in N$, take $c = 1$ and $K = N$. Otherwise take such a $v$ with $\Phi(v) \notin N$, and a uniformizer $\varpi$. Then $p$ is a unit times $\varpi^m$, and $\Phi(\varpi^m v) \in N$. So there is $k$ with $\Phi(\varpi^k v) \notin N$ and $\Phi(\varpi^{k+1} v) \in N$ (`New.Selmer.exists_pow_smul_notMem_and_succ_mem`). Put $N' = N + R\, \Phi(\varpi^k v)$. It is strictly larger than $N$ and it contains $\Phi(p O^n)$. The induction hypothesis gives $c'$ and $K \supseteq N'$. Take $c = c' \varpi$ and the same $K$.

Property 2 holds for $K$ by the hypothesis on $N'$. For property 3, $\varpi \cdot \Phi(\varpi^k v) \in N$, so the proposition on adding one vector gives $\varpi \operatorname{Fitt}(N') \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$, and $c' \operatorname{Fitt}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N')$. For property 1, the lemma on a strict drop with $a = \varpi^k$ shows that the image of $\mathcal{F}(N', p)$ modulo $V$ is strictly smaller than that of $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$. A strict inclusion of submodules raises the length by at least $1$, and $v(\varpi) = 1$. So
$$v(c) = v(c') + 1 \leq \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(N', p) \bmod V) + 1 \leq \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(N, p) \bmod V). \qquad \square$$

The proof does not need the lengths to be finite. If the length in 1 is $\infty$, the statement is still true, but the induction stops only by the Noetherian hypothesis.

**Remark (Formalization).** The docstring of the source calls this "a relative, dual form of the bound of a Fitting ideal by length". Property 3 of the classical list says that over $O$ the Fitting ideal of a module of length $\lambda$ is $\varpi^\lambda O$. Here the module is over the larger ring $R$, and only the part forced by forms over $O$ is measured. The source does not state the classical property 3. The proof takes a uniformizer from `exists_irreducible` at each step, so $c$ is a product of uniformizers that can differ by units. The statement is existential, so this is not a junk value. Only its valuation and property 3 matter, and both are invariant under units.

### How Volume 12 uses the theorem

In `New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean` the source takes $R = O[X_j]_{j \in J}$, a ring of polynomials, which is Noetherian when $J$ is finite. One lemma there, `New.Selmer.exists_mem_fittingIdeal_addVal_constantCoeff_le`, applies the theorem with $V$ equal to the $C$-torsion of the functions $G \to O/p$, for some $C \in O$. It states: let $\Phi \colon O^n \to R^s$ with $s$ a finite subset of $G$, let $p \neq 0$ with $\Phi(p O^n) \subseteq T$, and suppose that $\Phi(z(g))$ is the basis vector $e_g$ modulo $T$ for each $g \in s$. Let $T_0 = T + \mathfrak{a} R^s$, where $\mathfrak{a}$ is the ideal of polynomials without constant term. Then $\operatorname{Fitt}(T)$ contains an element $d$ whose constant coefficient has valuation at most
$$\operatorname{len}\bigl(\mathcal{F}(T_0, p) \bmod C\text{-torsion}\bigr) + \# s \cdot v(C).$$
Its proof uses the main theorem, then the base change inclusion for the ideal $\mathfrak{a}$. The lemma `New.Selmer.exists_mul_prod_X_mem_fittingIdeal` of the same file uses in addition the corollary on filtrations, with $r_j = C X_j$, to find an element $F \prod_{j \in I} X_j$ of the Fitting ideal. [The Ribet elements](/chapter/ribet-elements/) states and proves both lemmas.
