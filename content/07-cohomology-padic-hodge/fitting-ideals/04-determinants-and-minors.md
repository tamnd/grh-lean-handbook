## Lengths and determinants

This section makes the model case of the first section exact and general. Over a discrete valuation ring $O$ with valuation $v$ it proves four facts. The kernel of a square matrix $M$ on $(O/p)^n$ has length at most $v(\det M)$. A lattice $L$ with $a O^n \subseteq L \subseteq O^n$ is the annihilator of a module of vectors modulo $a$ whose length bounds $v(\det M)$ for a matrix $M$ with rows in $L$. The quotient of $O^k$ by the rows of a square matrix $N$ has length $v(\det N)$. And the kernel of any matrix $A$ on $(O/a)^k$ has length equal to the least valuation of a maximal minor of $A$ stacked on $a \cdot 1$. All four rest on the theorem on elementary divisors.

### Elementary divisors

The classical theorem says: a matrix $M$ over a principal ideal domain $O$ can be written $M = Q D P$ with $P$, $Q$ invertible and $D$ diagonal with entries $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \dots$ (Lang, *Algebra*, Chapter III, Section 7). Over a discrete valuation ring the entries are $d_i = u_i \varpi^{a_i}$ for units $u_i$, and the divisibility says that $a_1 \leq a_2 \leq \dots$.

**Remark (Formalization).** The source proves the diagonal form in `New.IsValuationPair.smith_normal_form` (`Lib/General/LinearAlgebra/IntegralLattices.lean`, line 257), for a subring with the property `New.IsValuationPair`, and it applies the result with the subring equal to all of $O$. Its docstring says: "No divisibility among the entries of d is stated." None of the proofs of this section uses the divisibility. Each proof uses only that $\det M$ is a unit times $\prod_i d_i$, and that the kernel and the cokernel of a diagonal matrix are products of cyclic modules.

### The kernel bound

**Theorem.** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring, $n$ a finite set, $M$ an $n \times n$ matrix over $O$, and $p \in O$. Let $M$ act on $(O/p)^n$. Then
$$\operatorname{length}_O \ker\bigl(M \colon (O/p)^n \to (O/p)^n\bigr) \leq v(\det M).$$

Lean: `New.length_ker_le_addVal_det` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* Write $M = Q \operatorname{diag}(a_1, \dots, a_n) P$ with $P$, $Q$ invertible. If $M w = 0$, then $\operatorname{diag}(a) P w = 0$, since $Q$ is invertible. So coordinate $i$ of $P w$ lies in the $a_i$-torsion of $O/p$. This torsion is a submodule of the cyclic module $O/p$, so it is cyclic, generated by some $g_i$, and it is a quotient of $O/a_i$. So the kernel lies in the image under $P^{-1}$ of a quotient of $\prod_i O/a_i$. Its length is at most $\sum_i v(a_i) = v(\prod_i a_i) \leq v(\det M)$. The last step holds because $\det M = \det Q \cdot \prod_i a_i \cdot \det P$. $\square$

There is no hypothesis on $\det M$ or on $p$. If $\det M = 0$ the bound is $\infty$. If $p = 0$, then $O/p = O$ and the kernel is $0$ or of infinite length. If $p$ is a unit, the module is $0$.

The theorem is used in three files of the tied Selmer groups: `Selmer/Untied.lean`, `Selmer/MainSummands.lean` and `Selmer/SelmerData.lean`. Its first consequence is a bound by points.

**Corollary.** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring, $B$ an $O$-module with an isomorphism $B \cong O/p$, and $Z$ an $O$-submodule of the functions $\Gamma \to B$. Let $q_1, \dots, q_m \in \Gamma$ and let $n_1, \dots, n_m$ be natural numbers. Suppose that $n_i\, z(q_i) = 0$ for all $z \in Z$ and all $i$, and that the only $z \in Z$ with $z(q_i) = 0$ for all $i$ is $z = 0$. Then
$$\operatorname{length}_O Z \leq v\Bigl(\prod_i n_i\Bigr),$$
where the product of the natural numbers $n_i$ is read in $O$.

Lean: `New.OddPart.length_le_of_detecting_points` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* Evaluation at the points, $z \mapsto (z(q_i))_i$, is injective on $Z$, and it sends $Z$ into the kernel of the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(n_1, \dots, n_m)$ on $(O/p)^m$. Apply the theorem. $\square$

The corollary is used in `Lib/Cyclotomic/OddSha.lean`, which belongs to [the odd classes](/chapter/odd-classes/) of Volume 3. If some $n_i$ is $0$ in $O$, the bound is $\infty$ and says nothing.

### The duality lemma for lattices

**Lemma.** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring, $a \neq 0$ in $O$, and $L \subseteq O^n$ a submodule with $a v \in L$ for every $v \in O^n$. Then there are an $n \times n$ matrix $M$ over $O$ and a submodule $S \subseteq (O/a)^n$ such that:

1. every row of $M$ lies in $L$;
2. a vector $v \in O^n$ lies in $L$ if and only if $\sum_j v_j w_j = 0$ in $O/a$ for every $w \in S$;
3. $v(\det M) \leq \operatorname{length}_O S$.

Lean: `New.exists_matrix_addVal_det_le_length` (Lib/General/Algebra/FittingIdealsAndLengths.lean).

*Proof.* Since $a O^n \subseteq L \subseteq O^n$, the lattice $L$ is free of rank $n$. So $L$ is the image of $O^n$ under a matrix $M'$, and there is a matrix $N$ with $N M' = a \cdot 1$. Take $M = M'^{\mathsf T}$, so that its rows are the columns of $M'$, which lie in $L$. Take for $S$ the image of $N^{\mathsf T}$ on $(O/a)^n$. A vector $v$ pairs to $0$ with all of $S$ exactly when $N v \equiv 0 \bmod a$, which means $v \in M' O^n = L$: if $N v = a x$, then $N (v - M' x) = 0$, and $N$ is injective because $\det N \det M' = a^n \neq 0$. For 3, write $M' = Q \operatorname{diag}(d) P$. Then $N = a P^{-1} \operatorname{diag}(d)^{-1} Q^{-1}$, so up to invertible matrices $N^{\mathsf T}$ acts on $(O/a)^n$ as $\operatorname{diag}(a/d_i)$. Its image is isomorphic to $\prod_i O/d_i$, of length $v(\det M') = v(\det M)$. $\square$

The proof gives equality in 3. The source states only the inequality, which is what its uses need. Section 5 uses the lemma for duality: a vector lies in $L$ if every linear form modulo $a$ that vanishes on $L$ vanishes on it. Section 6 uses it to find rows of small determinant.

### Maximal minors

The kernel bound is an inequality. The source also needs the exact length of a kernel, for a matrix that need not be square, in the chapter on [the tied Selmer groups](/chapter/tied-selmer-groups/). The answer is the classical description of the elementary divisors by minors.

**Definition (Maximal minors, kernel, row lattice).** Let $\Lambda$ be a commutative ring, $A$ a matrix over $\Lambda$ with rows indexed by a set $r$ (possibly infinite) and columns indexed by a finite set $k$, and $a \in \Lambda$. Stack $A$ on the square matrix $a \cdot 1_k$. The *maximal minors* of $(A, a)$ are the determinants of the $k \times k$ matrices whose rows are rows of this stack, chosen by any function $f \colon k \to r \sqcup k$. A choice that repeats a row is allowed and gives the minor $0$.

For a matrix $A$ over $O$ and an $O$-module $B$, the *kernel* of $A$ on $B^k$ is the set of $w \in B^k$ with $\sum_j A_{ij} w_j = 0$ for every row $i$. The *row lattice* of $(A, a)$ is the submodule of $O^k$ spanned by the rows of $A$ and of $a \cdot 1_k$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.maximalMinors`, `New.Selmer.matrixKernel`, `New.Selmer.rowLattice` (Lib/General/Algebra/SharpMinors.lean).

**Lemma.** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring and $N$ a $k \times k$ matrix over $O$. Then the quotient of $O^k$ by the span of the rows of $N$ has length $v(\det N)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.length_quotient_span_row` (Lib/General/Algebra/SharpMinors.lean).

*Proof.* Write $N = Q \operatorname{diag}(d) P$. The rows of $N$ span the kernel of the map from $O^k$ onto $\prod_i O/d_i$ that sends $v$ to the classes of the coordinates of $v P^{-1}$. So the quotient is $\prod_i O/d_i$, of length $\sum_i v(d_i) = v(\det N)$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $Q$ be a finitely generated $O$-module, and $a \neq 0$ with $a Q = 0$. Then $\operatorname{Hom}_O(Q, O/a)$ has the same length as $Q$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.length_linearMap_quotSpan` (Lib/General/Algebra/SharpMinors.lean). The cyclic case, $\operatorname{Hom}(O/d, O/a) \cong O/d$ for $d \mid a$, is `New.Selmer.length_linearMap_quotSpan_of_dvd`.

*Proof.* The module $Q$ is a finite sum of cyclic modules $O/d$ with $d \mid a$, and the maps from $O/d$ to $O/a$ are the multiples of the map $1 \mapsto a/d$. $\square$

**Proposition.** Let $A$ be a matrix over $O$ with columns indexed by $k$, and $a \neq 0$. Then the length of $O^k$ modulo the row lattice of $(A, a)$ is the least of the valuations $v(P)$, where $P$ runs over the maximal minors of $(A, a)$, and some minor attains it.

Lean: `New.Selmer.isLeast_length_quotient_rowLattice` (Lib/General/Algebra/SharpMinors.lean).

*Proof.* Each maximal minor is the determinant of a square matrix whose rows lie in the row lattice. Its rows span a smaller lattice, so by the lemma on the quotient by the rows the length of the quotient is at most $v(P)$. For the reverse inequality, the row lattice contains $a O^k$, so it is free of rank $k$ and is spanned by the rows of a square matrix $N$ (`New.Selmer.exists_span_row_eq_rowLattice`). Let $P_0$ be a minor of least valuation. It divides every maximal minor. The rows of $N$ are combinations of the rows of the stack, so by the multilinearity of the determinant $\det N$ is a combination of maximal minors, and $P_0$ divides $\det N$ (`New.Selmer.dvd_det_of_forall_row_mem_span`). So $v(P_0) \leq v(\det N)$, which is the length. $\square$

**Theorem.** Let $\Lambda$ be a commutative ring, $A$ a matrix over $\Lambda$ with columns indexed by a finite set $k$, $a \in \Lambda$, and $\operatorname{ev} \colon \Lambda \to O$ a ring homomorphism to a discrete valuation ring with $\operatorname{ev}(a) \neq 0$. Let $\bar A$ be the matrix $A$ with $\operatorname{ev}$ applied to each entry. Then the length of the kernel of $\bar A$ on $(O/\operatorname{ev}(a))^k$ is the least element of the set $\{ v(\operatorname{ev}(P)) : P \text{ a maximal minor of } (A, a) \}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.isLeast_length_matrixKernel` (Lib/General/Algebra/SharpMinors.lean).

*Proof.* A ring homomorphism maps the maximal minors of $(A, a)$ onto those of $(\bar A, \operatorname{ev}(a))$ (`New.Selmer.image_maximalMinors`). A vector $w$ in the kernel of $\bar A$ on $(O/a)^k$ is a linear map from $O^k$ to $O/a$ that kills the rows of $\bar A$ and of $a \cdot 1$. So the kernel is $\operatorname{Hom}_O(O^k/\text{row lattice}, O/a)$ (`New.Selmer.length_matrixKernel_eq_length_linearMap`). The quotient is killed by $a$, so the lemma on $\operatorname{Hom}(Q, O/a)$ gives it the length of the quotient, and the proposition computes that length. $\square$

The ring $\Lambda$ is there for the application. In `New/Congruences/Selmer/OneSystem.lean`, line 451, the source takes for $\Lambda$ a ring of polynomials, for $\operatorname{ev}$ an evaluation of the variables, and for $a$ a power $\ell^M$. Then the theorem computes a length for each specialization from minors that are polynomials, computed once.

**Example.** Let $O = \mathbb{Z}_p$, $k$ have one element, $A = (p^2)$ and $a = p^5$. The stack is the column $(p^2, p^5)$. Its maximal minors are $p^2$ and $p^5$, of least valuation $2$. The kernel of $p^2$ on $\mathbb{Z}/p^5$ is $p^3 \mathbb{Z}/p^5$, of length $2$. If $a = p$, the minors are $p^2$ and $p$, the least valuation is $1$, and the kernel of $p^2$ on $\mathbb{Z}/p$ is all of $\mathbb{Z}/p$, of length $1$. This is the term $\min(a_i, k)$ of the model case.
