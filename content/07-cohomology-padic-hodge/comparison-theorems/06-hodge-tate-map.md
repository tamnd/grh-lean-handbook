## The Hodge-Tate map and Hodge-Tate weights

This section builds the Hodge-Tate map $\lambda_h$ of the source and proves the result that the main argument uses: the value of $\lambda_h$ on a Hodge class comes from a vector of Hodge-Tate weight $h$ in $\mathbb{C}_p \otimes V^{q+h}(Y)$. The result gives the Hodge-Tate jumps of the Galois representations of Volume 8, together with the Woods Hole formula of [the chapter on cohomology](/chapter/cohomology-ext/).

### The classical result

Let $X$ be proper and smooth over a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Faltings, *p-adic Hodge theory*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 1 (1988), proves the Hodge-Tate decomposition $H^n_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{\overline{K}}, \mathbb{Q}_p) \otimes C \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H^i(X, \Omega^j) \otimes_K C(-j)$. Scholze, Forum Math. Pi 1 (2013), Corollary 1.8, proves it for proper smooth rigid analytic varieties. Bhatt, *The Hodge-Tate decomposition via perfectoid spaces*, Arizona Winter School 2017 notes, Section 3.4, builds the Hodge-Tate map from the torsor of lifts modulo the square of the kernel of Fontaine's map. The source follows this construction and names these three sources.

The source builds only one direction of the comparison. Its docstring says: "Only the natural multiplicative map into the part of weight h is built, from the torsor of lifts modulo the square of the kernel of Fontaine's map, and bijectivity is not proved."

### The thickening

Let $c$ be a $p$-adic test with ring $R$, and put $T = R[1/p]$. Let $\mu = [\varepsilon] - 1 \in A_{\inf}$.

**Definition (The thickening).** The thickening of $c$ is $B_c = W(R^\flat)[1/p]/(\ker \theta)^2$, as a functor of $c$ to $k$-algebras. Here $k$ is a field embedded in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$.
Lean: `New.Variety.thickeningOver` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/GenericFibre.lean).

**Definition (Multiplication by $\mu$).** The additive map $\iota : T \to B_c$ sends $\theta(x)$ to the class of $\mu x$. It is well defined because $\theta$ is onto and $\mu \cdot \ker \theta \subseteq (\ker \theta)^2$.
Lean: `New.Variety.thickeningOver.mulMu` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/GenericFibre.lean).

**Theorem.** The sequence $0 \to T \xrightarrow{\iota} B_c \to T \to 0$ is exact.
Lean: `New.Variety.thickeningOver.exact_mulMu_proj` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/GenericFibre.lean).

So $B_c$ is a square-zero extension of $T$ by $T$. The kernel of $\theta$ on $W(R^\flat)[1/p]$ is generated by $\xi$ (`New.Variety.PadicTest.ker_thetaGeneric_eq_span_xi`), and the map $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p \to T$ lifts uniquely to $B_c$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

### Pairs and the contraction class

**Definition (Hodge-Tate pairs).** A Hodge-Tate pair is a $p$-adic test $S$ and a rank $n$. It stands for the pair $(S, M)$ with $M = T^n$ and $T = S[1/p]$. A map of pairs is a map $f$ of tests with a semilinear map $\varphi = (\varphi_0, \varphi_1) : M \to T' \times M'$. The ring of a pair is $B_{S,M} = B_S \times_T (T \oplus M)$.
Lean: `New.Variety.HodgeTatePair` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

**Definition (Contraction cocycle).** The contraction cocycle from $\Lambda = \bigwedge_T M$ to itself assigns to a map $(f, \varphi)$ the map $\bigwedge M \to \bigwedge M'$ that contracts with $\varphi_0$ and applies $\varphi_1$ to the remaining factors. Its class lies in $\mathrm{Ext}^1(\Lambda, \Lambda)$.
Lean: `New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.contractionCocycle` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

**Definition (Powers of the contraction class).** For $h \ge 0$, the class $c_h$ in the total Ext group from $\Lambda$ to $(S, M) \mapsto T$ is the $h$-th Yoneda power of the contraction class, followed by the projection of $\bigwedge M$ to its degree-zero part $T$.
Lean: `New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.contractionPow` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

The class $c_h$ is $k$-linear (`New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.act_comp_contractionPow`), it has weight $h$ under scaling of $M$ (`New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.scaleModule_comp_contractionPow`), and it is homogeneous of degree $h$ (`New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.exists_contractionPow_eq_directSum_of`).

### The map $\lambda_h$

For a functor $N$ from $k$-algebras to abelian groups, Hodge cohomology $\mathrm{HodgeH}^q(N)$ is $\mathrm{Ext}^q$ from $(A, M) \mapsto N(A \oplus M)$ to $(A, M) \mapsto \bigwedge_A M$, on pairs of a $k$-algebra $A$ and a free module $M$. [The chapter on cohomology](/chapter/cohomology-ext/) treats it. Write $\mathrm{PairH}(N)$ for the total cohomology on Hodge-Tate pairs of $(S, M) \mapsto N(B_{S,M})$ with coefficients $(S, M) \mapsto T$.

**Definition (Hodge-Tate map).** The map $\lambda_h : \mathrm{HodgeH}^q(N) \to \mathrm{PairH}(N)$ pulls a class back along $B_{S,M} \to T \oplus M$, puts it in total degree $q$, and composes it with $c_h$. It is $k$-linear.
Lean: `New.Variety.hodgeTateMap` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

**Definition (Restriction to pairs).** The $k$-linear map $\mathrm{res} : H^n(S \mapsto N(T), T) \to \mathrm{PairH}(N)$, from cohomology on $p$-adic tests, pulls back along $B_{S,M} \to T$ and includes the result as the degree-$n$ summand.
Lean: `New.Variety.restrictToPairs` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

**Definition.** $N$ has bijective restriction to pairs if pull-back along $B_{S,M} \to T$ is bijective in every degree, from the cohomology of $S \mapsto N(T)$ on tests to the cohomology of $(S, M) \mapsto N(B_{S,M})$ on pairs, both with coefficients $T$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PairRestrictBijective` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

**Theorem.** If $N$ has bijective restriction to pairs, then for $x \in \mathrm{HodgeH}^q(N)$ there is a class $\xi$ of degree $q + h$ on $p$-adic tests with $\lambda_h(x) = \mathrm{res}(\xi)$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PairRestrictBijective.exists_hodgeTateMap_eq` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

*Proof sketch.* The class $c_h$ is homogeneous of degree $h$, so $\lambda_h(x)$ lies in degree $q + h$ of $\mathrm{PairH}(N)$. Bijectivity of the restriction in that degree gives $\xi$. $\square$

Under the same hypothesis, $\mathrm{res}$ is injective in each degree (`New.Variety.PairRestrictBijective.injective_restrictToPairs`). So $\xi$ is unique.

**Theorem (Charts).** Let $X$ be a functor from $k$-algebras to types such that every field-valued point of $X$ lies in an étale chart with $n$ coordinates. Then $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ has bijective restriction to pairs.
Lean: `New.Variety.IsChartCovered.pairRestrictBijective` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/Map.lean).

*Proof sketch.* The map $B_{S,M} \to T$ is a square-zero extension (`New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.isSquareZero_toGeneric`). On an étale chart, a point over $T$ has unique lifts to square-zero extensions, compatibly with the chart. So the lifts of a point of a chart of dimension $n$ are represented by one pair $(S, T^n)$, and this gives the bijection. $\square$

### The Galois twist

For $g \in G_p$, the twist of a $p$-adic test by $g$ keeps the ring and composes the structure map $\mathcal{O}_C \to R$ with $g$. It leaves $T$ and $B_c$ unchanged. Write $\chi$ for the cyclotomic character.

**Lemma.** For $g \in G_p$ and $t \in T$, the map $\iota$ of $c$ at $t$ equals the map $\iota$ of the twist of $c$ at $\chi(g^{-1}) t$.
Lean: `New.Variety.thickeningOver.mulMu_twist` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/GaloisWeight.lean).

The reason is $g^{-1}(\mu) \equiv \chi(g^{-1}) \mu$ modulo $\mu^2$.

**Theorem (Weight of the twist).** Let $g \in G_p$ fix a subfield $E$ of $k$. Let $x$ be a Hodge class of $N$ over $E$, and let $\xi$ be a class on $p$-adic tests with $\lambda_h(x) = \mathrm{res}(\xi)$. Let $t \in k$ map to $\chi(g^{-1})$. Then the pull-back of $\xi$ along the twist by $g$ has the same image under $\mathrm{res}$ as $t^h \xi$.
Lean: `New.Variety.hodgeTateMap_galois_twist` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/GaloisWeight.lean).

*Proof sketch.* The twist acts on pairs, and the classes $c_h$ are compatible with it (`New.Variety.HodgeTatePair.contractionPow_galTwist`). The twist changes $\iota$ by $\chi(g^{-1})$, and $c_h$ has weight $h$, so it changes $c_h$ by $\chi(g^{-1})^h$. The class $x$ is defined over $E$, so $g$ fixes it. $\square$

### Vectors of Hodge-Tate weight $h$

A vector $y$ of $\mathbb{C}_p \otimes V$ has Hodge-Tate weight $h$ for a representation $\rho$ if $(j(g) \otimes \rho(g))(y) = \chi(j(g))^{-h} y$ for all $g$. The Lean name is `New.HasHodgeTateWeight`, and [the chapter on period rings](/chapter/period-rings/) treats it.

**Theorem (Hodge-Tate weights).** Let $Y$ and $W$ be proper over $R$, with $Y$ smooth of relative dimension $d$ and charted over $\Omega$. Let $E$ be a field between $R$ and $\Omega$, mapped to $k \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, and let $A$ be a place of $\Omega$ that contains the image of $R$, with a $p$-adic place embedding $e$ compatible with $E \to k$. Let $\varepsilon$ be a section of $k \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. Let $h \ge 0$, and let $x$ be a Hodge class of degree $q$ of $Y$ over $E$. Then there are a vector $v \in \mathbb{C}_p \otimes V^{q+h}(Y)$ and an $\varepsilon$-semilinear map $\Theta : \mathbb{C}_p \otimes V^{q+h}(W) \to \mathrm{PairH}$ such that:

1. $v$ has Hodge-Tate weight $h$ for the decomposition group of $A$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E)$; and
2. $\lambda_h(u^* x) = \Theta(u^* v)$ for all $u : W \to Y$.

Lean: `New.Variety.exists_hasHodgeTateWeight` (Lib/PadicComparison/HodgeTate/PeriodMap.lean).

*Proof.* Take the period map $J$ of Section 5 for the period ring pair $(\mathcal{O}_C, \mathbb{C}_p)$, with $\Psi(c) = c[1/p]$ through Fontaine's map (`New.Variety.exists_periodAlg_iso_genericAlg`). The valuative criterion gives an isomorphism $B_Z$ from cohomology over the generic rings to cohomology over the tests, for each proper $Z$. The scheme $Y$ is smooth, so its points are covered by étale charts, and $\mathbb{Z}[Y]$ has bijective restriction to pairs. So $\lambda_h(x) = \mathrm{res}(\xi)$ for a class $\xi$. The map $J_Y$ is bijective, so $J_Y(v) = B_Y(\xi)$ for some $v$. Put $\Theta = \mathrm{res} \circ B_W^{-1} \circ J_W$. It is $\varepsilon$-semilinear because $J_W$ is $\mathbb{C}_p$-linear. Part 2 is naturality of $J$, $B$ and $\lambda_h$. For part 1, the equivariance of $J$ turns the action of $g$ into the pull-back along the twist by $j(g)$. The theorem on the weight of the twist multiplies $\xi$ by $\chi(j(g)^{-1})^h$. Injectivity of $\mathrm{res}$ and of $J_Y$ then gives part 1. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The theorem does not say that $\lambda_h$ is injective or bijective, that $\Theta$ is injective, or that $v \ne 0$ when $x \ne 0$. The source does not prove the Hodge-Tate decomposition. The map $\Theta$ is fixed before $u$ is chosen, so part 2 is a real constraint: if $u^* v = u'^* v$, then $\lambda_h(u^* x) = \lambda_h(u'^* x)$. The argument of Volume 8 uses the theorem in `New.Variety.Model.isNullHecke_mul_of_kills_weight`, at line 461 of `Lib/Shimura/Eigenspace.lean`. A reader must not read the bijectivity of the period map $J$ as a bijectivity of $\lambda_h$. Only $J$ is proved bijective.

**Remark (Formalization).** The field $k$ carries the class `New.Variety.EmbeddedInQpBar`, an embedding in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. The section $\varepsilon$ of $k \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ is a hypothesis. It exists only when the embedding is onto, for example when $k$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The theorem does not choose it. Several definitions in `Map.lean` carry `seal PadicTest.genericAlg in`, and `Map.lean` sets `backward.isDefEq.respectTransparency false`. These options control unfolding and do not change the statements.
