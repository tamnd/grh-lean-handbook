## Cells, p-adic tests and the tilt

This section defines the objects on which all comparison theorems of the source live. They are the $p$-adic tests: products of complete valuation rings with a map from $\mathcal{O}_C$. The section proves three facts about them. The kernel of Fontaine's map $\theta$ on a test is principal, generated by $\xi$. The test is recovered from its tilt by $\theta$, so tilting is fully faithful. Every algebra with a point in a test has a universal covering test. Section 3 and Section 5 use all three facts.

### Notation

Fix a prime $p$. Let $\mathcal{O}_C$ be the valuation ring of $\mathbb{C}_p$, and let $\mathcal{O}^\flat = \varprojlim_{x \mapsto x^p} \mathcal{O}_C/p$ be its tilt. Let $A_{\inf} = W(\mathcal{O}^\flat)$. For a ring $S$ in which $p$ is not a unit, write $S^\flat = \varprojlim_{x \mapsto x^p} S/p$ and $\theta_S : W(S^\flat) \to S$ for Fontaine's map. On Teichmüller lifts, $\theta_S([x]) = x^\sharp$, the limit of $\tilde{x}_n^{p^n}$ for any lifts $\tilde{x}_n$ of the coordinates. [The chapter on period rings](/chapter/period-rings/) gives these objects in detail.

The source fixes a compatible system $\varepsilon = (1, \zeta_p, \zeta_{p^2}, \dots)$ of $p$-power roots of unity in $\mathcal{O}^\flat$, and $\varepsilon^{1/p}$, its $p$-th root. It puts
$$\tau = [\varepsilon^{1/p}] - 1, \qquad \mu = [\varepsilon] - 1, \qquad \xi = \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} [\varepsilon^{1/p}]^i,$$
so that $\xi \tau = \mu$ in $A_{\inf}$. It is a classical fact that $\xi$ generates the kernel of $\theta : A_{\inf} \to \mathcal{O}_C$ (Fontaine, *Le corps des périodes p-adiques*, Astérisque 223 (1994), Exposé II). Write $p^\flat$ for the zeroth Witt coordinate of $\xi$, an element of $\mathcal{O}^\flat$. Then $p^\flat \equiv \xi \pmod p$.

**Definition (The element $p^\flat$).** $p^\flat \in \mathcal{O}^\flat$ is the zeroth Witt coordinate of $\xi$.
Lean: `New.Variety.pFlat` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean).

The element $p^\flat$ is the image of $\xi$ in $A_{\inf}/p = \mathcal{O}^\flat$. Its untilt $(p^\flat)^\sharp$ has the same valuation as $p$. So $p^\flat$ plays the role of the usual element $p^\flat$ with $(p^\flat)^\sharp = p$, up to a unit.

### Cells and tests

**Definition (Cell).** Let $T$ be a commutative ring. $T$ is a cell if it is a domain and a valuation ring, it is $p$-adically complete, it is absolutely integrally closed, and $p \neq 0$ in $T$.
Lean: `New.Variety.IsCell` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean).

In a cell, $p$ is not a unit, because a unit $p$ would make the $p$-adic topology discrete and the ring zero. Absolutely integrally closed means that every monic polynomial of positive degree has a root. So the fraction field of a cell is algebraically closed. The ring $\mathcal{O}_C$ is a cell. The rank of the valuation is not fixed: a cell can have a valuation of any rank.

**Definition ($p$-adic test).** A $p$-adic test is a nonempty family $(T_i)_{i \in I}$ of cells, together with a ring map $\mathcal{O}_C \to \prod_i T_i$. Its ring is $S = \prod_i T_i$. A map of tests is a map of $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebras between the rings.
Lean: `New.Variety.PadicTest` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean).

The index set $I$ is any type. It can be infinite. The ring $S$ is $p$-adically complete and $p$ is a nonzerodivisor in $S$, because both hold in each factor. The functor $S \mapsto S$ to $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebras is faithful and full by definition.

**Example.** The ring $\mathcal{O}_C$ with its identity is a test with one cell. For an element $g$ of $G_p$, the twist of a test keeps the ring and replaces the structure map $s$ by $s \circ g$. Twists give the Galois action in Section 5 and Section 6.

**Lemma.** The ring of a $p$-adic test is a test ring in the sense of [Chapter 2](/chapter/cohomology-ext/): it is reduced, absolutely integrally closed, and for all $a, b$ there is an idempotent $e$ with $a \mid eb$ and $b \mid (1 - e)a$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PadicTest.isTest` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* Each factor is a valuation ring, so in each factor $a \mid b$ or $b \mid a$. Let $e$ be the idempotent that is $1$ at the factors where $a \mid b$ and $0$ at the others. The other properties hold in each factor. $\square$

So a $p$-adic test gives a point of the category of test rings, and the cohomology of [Chapter 2](/chapter/cohomology-ext/) restricts to $p$-adic tests.

### The tilt and the kernel of theta

**Definition (Tilt of a test).** The tilt is the functor $S \mapsto S^\flat$ from $p$-adic tests to $\mathcal{O}^\flat$-algebras. The structure map $\mathcal{O}^\flat \to S^\flat$ is the tilt of $\mathcal{O}_C \to S$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PadicTest.tilt` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean).

The tilt of a product is the product of the tilts. Each $T_i^\flat$ is a perfect valuation ring of characteristic $p$.

**Lemma (Kernel of theta).** Let $S$ be the ring of a $p$-adic test. Then the kernel of $\theta_S : W(S^\flat) \to S$ is the principal ideal generated by the image of $\xi$ under $W(\mathcal{O}^\flat) \to W(S^\flat)$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PadicTest.ker_theta` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* The map $\theta$ commutes with the tilt of $\mathcal{O}_C \to S$, and $\theta(\xi) = 0$ over $\mathcal{O}_C$. So the image of $\xi$ lies in the kernel. For the other inclusion, the source uses a criterion for Witt vectors. Let $J$ be an ideal of $W(R)$, $R$ perfect, such that $p x \in J$ implies $x \in J$. If $J$ contains an element whose zeroth coordinate divides the zeroth coordinate of every element of $J$, then that element generates $J$. The kernel of $\theta_S$ is $p$-saturated, because $p$ is a nonzerodivisor in $S$. Let $a$ be in the kernel. Divisibility of zeroth coordinates in $S^\flat$ can be read after untilting, because $S$ is a product of valuation rings. The untilt of $p^\flat$ divides $p$, and $p$ divides $a_0^\sharp$, because $a_0^\sharp \equiv \theta_S(a) = 0 \pmod p$. So $p^\flat$ divides $a_0$. $\square$

**Theorem (Theta on tests).** The maps $\theta_S$ induce a natural isomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebras
$$\mathcal{O}_C \otimes_{A_{\inf}} W(S^\flat) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } S$$
on $p$-adic tests $S$. Here $\mathcal{O}_C$ is an $A_{\inf}$-algebra through $\theta$.
Lean: `New.Variety.PadicTest.thetaIso` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* Injectivity follows from the lemma on the kernel: the kernel of $\theta_S$ is generated by the image of $\xi$, and $\xi$ also generates the kernel of $A_{\inf} \to \mathcal{O}_C$. Surjectivity holds because $S$ is absolutely integrally closed. For such rings, every element of $S/p$ has a $p$-th root, and then $\theta_S$ is onto. $\square$

**Theorem (Points of perfect algebras).** Let $b$ be a perfect $\mathcal{O}^\flat$-algebra. Then $\mathcal{O}^\flat$-algebra maps $b \to S^\flat$ correspond to $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebra maps $\mathcal{O}_C \otimes_{A_{\inf}} W(b) \to S$, naturally in the $p$-adic test $S$. The correspondence sends $f$ to the map induced by $\theta_S \circ W(f)$.
Lean: `New.Variety.homTiltIso` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* For a perfect ring $R$ of characteristic $p$, ring maps $R \to S^\flat$ correspond to ring maps $W(R) \to S$ by $f \mapsto \theta_S \circ W(f)$ (`New.Variety.theta_comp_bijective`). For injectivity, the zeroth coordinate of $f$ is read modulo $p$ on Teichmüller lifts. For surjectivity, a map $g : W(R) \to S$ gives $x \mapsto (g([x^{1/p^n}]) \bmod p)_n$ in $S^\flat$. The $\mathcal{O}^\flat$-linear maps then correspond to the maps over $\mathcal{O}_C \otimes_{A_{\inf}} W(\mathcal{O}^\flat) = \mathcal{O}_C$. $\square$

Two consequences follow. The tilt is fully faithful on $p$-adic tests: the instances `tilt.Full` and `tilt.Faithful` of `Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean` conjugate $W(f)$ by the isomorphism of the theorem on theta. And the points of a perfect algebra $b$ in tilts of tests are the points of the $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebra $\mathcal{O}_C \otimes W(b)$ in tests. This is the form of the tilting equivalence of Scholze (*Perfectoid spaces*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 116 (2012), Section 5) that the source uses.

### Covering points

**Lemma (Cells over a prime).** Let $B$ be a ring in which $p$ is a nonzerodivisor, and let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a prime ideal of $B$ with $p \in \mathfrak{m}$. Then there are a cell $W$ and a ring map $y : B \to W$ such that $y(z)$ is not a unit for every $z \in \mathfrak{m}$.
Lean: `New.Variety.exists_cell_over` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* Let $L = B_\mathfrak{m}$, and let $K$ be an algebraically closed field that contains the fraction field of $L$. Since $p$ is a nonzerodivisor in $B$, it is a unit in $K$. Take a valuation subring $U$ of $K$ that dominates $L$, by the classical extension theorem for valuations. Then $U$ is absolutely integrally closed, because $K$ is algebraically closed, and $p \neq 0$ in $U$. But $U$ need not be complete. The source takes the ultrapower $G$ of $U$ over a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\mathbb{N}$, and $W$ the $p$-adic completion of $G$. In the ultrapower, a $p$-adic Cauchy sequence has a limit, so $G$ maps onto $W$. In $W$, an element that is divisible by all powers of $p$ is zero, and the source deduces that $W$ is a domain and a valuation ring. It is absolutely integrally closed, as a quotient of $\mathbb{N} \to U$. An element of $\mathfrak{m}$ goes to a non-unit, because an inverse in $G$ gives an inverse in $U$ at some index, and $L \to U$ is local. $\square$

**Theorem (Universal covering test).** Let $B$ be an $\mathcal{O}_C$-algebra. If $B$ has a point in some $p$-adic test, then there are a test $S_0$ and a point $y : B \to S_0$ such that every point $z : B \to S$ in any test $S$ is $f \circ y$ for some map of tests $f : S_0 \to S$.
Lean: `New.Variety.hasCoveringPoints_padic` (Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCovers.lean).

*Proof.* Every map from $B$ to a cell factors through a cell that is a quotient of one fixed polynomial ring over $B$. The variables of this polynomial ring name the solutions of all the equations that a cell must solve. The image of the polynomial ring in the cell is closed under these solutions, and so it is again a cell (`New.Variety.isCell_of_isSolutionClosed`). There is only a set of such quotients. Let $S_0$ be the product of all of them, with the product map $y$. A point $z : B \to \prod_j T_j$ gives, for each $j$, a quotient that appears as a factor of $S_0$. The projection to these factors is the map $f$. $\square$

The covering test is a product over a large set. Section 3 uses it to build a v-cover of a perfect algebra by the tilt of one test.

**Remark (Formalization).** The objects of this section differ from the perfectoid objects of Scholze in three ways. First, a cell is a valuation ring of any rank, and a test is a product over any index set, so a test ring is not perfectoid in the sense of Scholze in general. The source never uses perfectoid rings; it uses only the isomorphism of the theorem on theta and the lemma on the kernel. Second, the element $p^\flat$ is the zeroth coordinate of $\xi$, not an element with $(p^\flat)^\sharp = p$. Third, the source declares two global algebra structures in `Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean`: $A_{\inf} \to \mathcal{O}^\flat$ by the zeroth coordinate ([line 358](lean:Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean#L358)) and $A_{\inf} \to \mathcal{O}_C$ by $\theta$ ([line 360](lean:Lib/PadicComparison/Descent/TestCategory.lean#L360)). Every tensor product over $A_{\inf}$ in this chapter uses these two maps. A reader who uses another map, such as a Frobenius twist of $\theta$, gets a different tensor product. Section 7 uses exactly such a twist for $A_{\max}$, but there the source passes the algebra structure as an explicit argument.
