## The rings B_max and B_dR

This section builds the rest of the tower of Section 1: $A_{\max}$, $B^+$, $B_{\max}$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and its fraction field $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$. It constructs the period $t$ without logarithms, proves that $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $t$, and proves the fundamental exact sequence for $B_{\max}$. The source cites P. Colmez, *Théorie d'Iwasawa des représentations de de Rham d'un corps local*, Ann. of Math. 148 (1998), Sections III.2 and III.3, and J.-M. Fontaine, *Le corps des périodes p-adiques*, Astérisque 223 (1994).

### The rings

**Definition (The period rings).**

1. $A_{\max}$ is the $p$-adic completion of $A_{\inf}[X]/(pX - \xi)$. Write $\xi/p$ for the image of $X$.
2. $B^+ = A_{\max}[1/p]$.
3. $B_{\max} = B^+[1/\mu]$.
4. $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is the completion of $B^+$ for the ideal generated by $\xi$.

Lean: `New.Period.Amax` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean), `New.Period.BPlus`, `New.Period.Bmax`, `New.Period.BdRPlus`, `New.Period.Amax.xiDivP`, `New.Period.p_mul_X`.

The ring $A_{\max}$ is Colmez's $A_{\max}$. Colmez defines $B_{\max}^+ = A_{\max}[1/p]$ and $B_{\max} = B_{\max}^+[1/t]$ for Fontaine's period $t$. The source inverts $\mu$ in place of $t$. Its own period $t$ below is $p\mu$ times a unit of $A_{\max}$, and $p$ is a unit of $B^+$. So inverting $\mu$ and inverting the $t$ of the source give the same ring.

**Lemma (Universal property).** Let $R$ be a $p$-adically complete ring, $f : A_{\inf} \to R$ a ring homomorphism and $x \in R$ with $f(\xi) = px$. Then $f$ extends to $F : A_{\max} \to R$ with $F(\xi/p) = x$.

Lean: `New.Period.Amax.exists_lift` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean).

The source proves only existence. Uniqueness is true when $R$ is $p$-torsion free, but the source does not state it.

**Definition (Theta on the new rings).** $\theta_{A_{\max}} : A_{\max} \to O_C$ is an extension of $\theta$ with $\xi/p \mapsto 0$, given by the universal property. Then $\theta_{B^+} : B^+ \to \mathbb{C}_p$ is its extension to $B^+$, and $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}} : B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to \mathbb{C}_p$ is the projection to $B^+/\xi B^+$ followed by the map that $\theta_{B^+}$ induces.

Lean: `New.Period.thetaAmax` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean), `New.Period.thetaBPlus`, `New.Period.thetaBdR`, `New.Period.thetaAmax_algebraMap`.

**Remark (Formalization).** `New.Period.thetaAmax` is a `choose` of the existence statement `Amax.exists_lift theta 0`. The only property that the source extracts is that it agrees with $\theta$ on $A_{\inf}$ (`New.Period.thetaAmax_algebraMap`). This determines it: two extensions agree on the dense image of $A_{\inf}[X]$, since $p\cdot(\xi/p) = \xi$ forces $p\,F(\xi/p) = 0$ and $O_C$ is $p$-torsion free. So the choice has no effect, although the source does not state uniqueness.

### Frobenius and Galois actions on the rings

The actions of $\mathrm{FrobGal} = \mathbb{N} \times G_p$ extend from $A_{\inf}$ to $A_{\max}$, $B^+$ and $B_{\max}$. For $A_{\max}$ one needs that $\varphi(\xi)$ and $g(\xi)$ lie in the ideal $(p, \xi)$, which holds by `New.Period.smul_xi_mem_span` of Section 2. For $B_{\max}$ one needs that each $\varphi^n g(\mu)$ is a unit of $B_{\max}$.

**Lemma.** In $A_{\max}$, $\varphi(\xi)$ is $p$ times a unit.

Lean: `New.Period.associated_p_frobEnd_xi` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean).

*Proof.* By Section 2, $\xi = [\xi_0] + pu$ with $u$ a unit, so $\varphi(\xi) = [\xi_0]^p + p\varphi(u)$. In $A_{\max}$, $[\xi_0]^p \in (p,\xi)^p$ is divisible by $p^2$, so $\varphi(\xi) = p(pc + \varphi(u))$, and the second factor is a unit. $\square$

**Lemma.** If $a \in A_{\inf}$ divides $\mu$, then $\varphi^n g(a)$ is a unit of $B_{\max}$ for every $n$ and $g$.

Lean: `New.Period.isUnit_smul_of_dvd_mu` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean).

*Proof.* It is enough to show that $\varphi^n g(a)$ divides $\mu$ in $B^+$. For $n = 0$: $g(a) \mid g(\mu) = \varphi(g\tau)$, and $g\tau$ is associated to $\tau$ by Section 2, so $g(\mu)$ is associated to $\mu$. For the step, $\varphi(\mu) = \varphi(\xi)\mu$, and $\varphi(\xi)$ is a unit of $B^+$ by the lemma above. $\square$

### The kernel of theta on B^+ and the ring B_dR^+

**Theorem.** For $y \in B^+$, $\theta_{B^+}(y) = 0$ if and only if $\xi$ divides $y$ in $B^+$. The map $\theta_{B^+}$ is onto $\mathbb{C}_p$.

Lean: `New.Period.thetaBPlus_eq_zero_iff` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean), `New.Period.thetaBPlus_surjective`.

The proof reduces to $A_{\inf}$. Modulo $p$, $A_{\max}$ is the polynomial ring $(A_{\inf}/(p,\xi))[X]$ (`New.Period.exists_reduction_mod_p`). The source then approximates elements of $B^+$ by elements of $A_{\inf}$ (`New.Period.BPlus.exists_Ainf_approx`) and applies the theorem of Fontaine in Section 2. The ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ then inherits the kernel.

**Theorem.** In $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$:

1. $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}(x) = 0$ if and only if $\xi \mid x$;
2. $x$ is a unit if and only if $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}(x) \neq 0$;
3. every nonzero $x$ is associated to $\xi^n$ for some $n \geq 0$;
4. $\xi$ is prime, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a domain, and $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a discrete valuation ring.

As consequences, $B^+$, $A_{\max}$ and $B_{\max}$ are domains, and $B^+ \to B_{\max}$ is injective.

Lean: `New.Period.thetaBdR_eq_zero_iff` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean), `New.Period.BdRPlus.isUnit_iff`, `New.Period.BdRPlus.exists_associated_xi_pow`, `New.Period.BdRPlus.prime_xi`, `New.Period.injective_BPlus_Bmax`.

*Proof.* Part 1 follows from the theorem on $B^+$, since each element of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is congruent modulo $\xi^n$ to an element of $B^+$ (`New.Period.BdRPlus.exists_xi_pow_dvd_sub`). For part 2, if $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}(x) \neq 0$, choose $b \in B^+$ with $\theta(xb) = 1$; then $xb$ is a unit of the $\xi$-adically complete ring. For part 3, write $x = \xi^n u$ with $\xi \nmid u$ and apply part 2. Part 4 follows. $\square$

The source registers the instance `IsDiscreteValuationRing BdRPlus`.

### Contraction

The next lemma is the tool for showing that a period with controlled Frobenius comes from $A_{\inf}$. It is used for the kernel of the fundamental exact sequence below, and in Section 5.

**Lemma (Contraction).** Let $V \subseteq B^+$ be a set of elements in the image of $A_{\max}$. Let $n \geq 0$ and $u \in A_{\inf}$, and assume that for every $v \in V$ there is $w \in V$ with $\xi^n v = u\,\varphi(w)$. Then $p^{n+1}v$ lies in the image of $A_{\inf}$ for every $v \in V$.

Lean: `New.Period.exists_Ainf_eq_of_contraction` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/CrystallineRings.lean).

*Proof sketch.* By induction on $k$, $p^{n+1}v$ is congruent modulo $\tau^{n+1}p^kA_{\max}$ to an element $a_k \in A_{\inf}$. In the docstring's words, each application of $\varphi$ improves an approximation by $A_{\inf}$ by one power of $p$. The $a_k$ converge $(p,\xi)$-adically, and the limit is $p^{n+1}v$. $\square$

### The action on mu

**Lemma.** For every $g \in G_p$, $\mu^2$ divides $g\mu - \chi(g)\mu$ in $A_{\inf}$.

Lean: `New.Lift.mu_sq_dvd_smul_mu_sub` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean).

*Proof.* By the approximation lemma of Section 2, it suffices to prove the divisibility modulo $(p, \xi)^m$ for each $m$. Let $u = [\varepsilon^{1/p^m}]$ and let $a \in \mathbb{N}$ represent $\chi(g)$ modulo $p^m$. Then $\xi \mid gu - u^a$, and the $p^m$-th power gives $g(1+\mu) \equiv (1+\mu)^a = 1 + a\mu + \mu^2 x$ modulo $(p,\xi)^m$. $\square$

Classically, $g[\varepsilon] = [\varepsilon]^{\chi(g)} = (1+\mu)^{\chi(g)}$, and the lemma is the first two terms of the binomial series. The source does not define $(1+\mu)^{\chi(g)}$ for $\chi(g) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, and the proof above avoids it.

### The period t

**Theorem.** There is $t \in A_{\max}$ such that $t$ is $p\mu$ times a unit of $A_{\max}$, $\varphi(t) = pt$, and $g(t) = \chi(g)\,t$ for every $g \in G_p$.

Lean: `New.Period.exists_tPeriod` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean).

*Proof.* Write $\varphi(\xi) = pu$ with $u$ a unit of $A_{\max}$, and $pu - p = \mu w$, from $\tau \mid \xi - p$. Solve $c - pu^2\varphi(c) = w$ by successive approximation (`New.Period.Amax.exists_sub_mul_frobEnd_eq`), and put $t = \mu\,(p + \mu c)$. Since $\varphi(\mu) = pu\mu$, a computation gives $t = p\mu\,(u + \mu u^2\varphi(c))$ and $\varphi(t) = pt$. For the Galois action, $\delta = g(t) - \chi(g)t$ satisfies $\varphi(\delta) = p\delta$, and $\mu^2 \mid \delta$ by the lemma on $\mu$. From $\delta = p^n\mu^2 z$ one gets $\delta = p^{n+1}\mu^2 u^2\varphi(z)$, so $\delta$ is divisible by every power of $p$, and $\delta = 0$. $\square$

**Definition (The period t).** `tPeriodAmax` is a choice of the element of the theorem, and `tPeriod` is its image in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

Lean: `New.Period.tPeriodAmax` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean), `New.Period.tPeriod`, `New.Period.smul_tPeriod`, `New.Period.frobEnd_tPeriodAmax`.

**Remark (Formalization).** `New.Period.tPeriodAmax` is a `choose`, with no normalization. The three properties determine $t$ only up to a factor: if $t$ satisfies them, so does $at$ for every $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The docstring of `New.Period.tPeriod` says that it "plays the role of `p` times Fontaine's period `t`". Classically $t = \log[\varepsilon]$, and the theorem above holds for $p\log[\varepsilon]$ also; the source does not prove that its choice is $p\log[\varepsilon]$, and it does not define the logarithm. Every later statement uses only the three properties. A statement in which $t$ appears, such as the filtration below, does not depend on the choice: two choices differ by a unit of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

### The field B_dR and the embedding of Q̄_p

**Definition (The field B_dR).** $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is the fraction field of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. The group $G_p$ acts on it, and $B_{\max}$ embeds in it equivariantly, since $\mu$ is nonzero in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.

Lean: `New.Period.BdR` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean), `New.Period.BdRPlus.associated_xi_tPeriod`.

In $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, $t$ is associated to $\xi$: $t$ is associated to $p\mu = p\xi\tau$, and $p$, $\tau$ are units of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ because $\theta$ does not kill them. So $t$ is a uniformizer, which is Fontaine's result in the form that the source needs.

**Proposition.** There is a homomorphism of $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebras $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ that is a section of $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and that commutes with $G_p$.

Lean: `New.Period.thetaBdR_algebraMap_padicAlgCl` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean), `New.Period.BdR.smul_algebraMap_padicAlgCl`.

*Proof.* The extension $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is formally étale, so the map $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p \to \mathbb{C}_p \cong B^+/\xi$ lifts to each $B^+/\xi^{n}$ and to the completion. For equivariance, $g$ restricts to an automorphism $\sigma$ of the image of the normal extension $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, and $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}$ gives $\sigma = g$. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The algebra structure of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ is a global instance defined by a `choose` of the lifting statement. Lifts are unique, so the choice does not matter, but the instance applies everywhere in the files that import `DeRhamPeriods`. Any statement that uses $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p \subset B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ means this embedding.

### The filtration

**Definition (Filtration).** For $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, $\mathrm{Fil}^n B_{\mathrm{dR}} = t^nB_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, a $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$-submodule of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$.

Lean: `New.Period.hodgeFil` (Lib/PadicHodge/TateSen.lean), `New.Period.tUnit`, `New.Period.prime_tPeriod`, `New.Period.hodgeFil_anti`, `New.Period.mul_mem_hodgeFil`, `New.Period.smul_tUnit_zpow`.

The filtration decreases and is multiplicative, and $g$ acts on $t^k$ by $\chi(g)^k$ for every integer $k$. An element $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ lies in $\mathrm{Fil}^M$ if and only if $\xi^M \mid y$, and in $\mathrm{Fil}^1$ if and only if $\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}(y) = 0$ (`New.Period.algebraMap_mem_hodgeFil_iff_xi_pow_dvd`, `New.Period.algebraMap_mem_hodgeFil_one_iff`).

**Lemma.** An element $x \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ lies in $\mathrm{Fil}^n$ for every $n \leq 0$, and in $\mathrm{Fil}^n$ for some $n > 0$ only if $x = 0$.

Lean: `New.Period.coe_mem_hodgeFil_iff` (Lib/PadicHodge/TateSen.lean).

**Theorem (Invariants of B_dR).** Let $j : \Gamma \to G_p$ have open image, with fixed field $K$. Let $S$ be a $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$-submodule of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, and let $x \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ with $j(g)x - x \in S$ for every $g \in \Gamma$. Then $x - a \in S$ for some $a \in K$. In particular, with $S = 0$, $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{j(\Gamma)} = K$.

Lean: `New.Period.BdR.exists_sub_mem_of_smul_sub_mem` (Lib/PadicHodge/TateSen.lean).

*Proof.* Suppose that no $x - a$ with $a \in K$ lies in $S$. Write $x - a = u\,t^n$ with $u$ a unit. Since $j(g)(x - a) - (x - a) \in S$, $\theta$ kills $\chi(g)^n j(g)u - u$, so $\chi(g)^n\, g\theta(u) = \theta(u)$. By Tate, $n = 0$, and by Ax-Sen-Tate, $\theta(u) = b \in K$. Applied with $a = 0$ and then $a = b$, this gives a unit $x - b$ with $\theta(x - b) = 0$, a contradiction. $\square$

### The fundamental exact sequence

**Theorem (Fundamental exact sequence).** The sequence
$$0 \to \mathbb{Q}_p \to B_{\max}^{\varphi = 1} \to B_{\mathrm{dR}}/B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ \to 0$$
is exact. More precisely, the source proves two statements.

1. For every $a \in B_{\max}$ and $\beta \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ there is $y \in B_{\max}$ with $\varphi(y) - y = a$ and $y - \beta \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$.
2. If $y \in B_{\max}$ has $\varphi(y) = y$ and $y \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$, then $y \in \mathbb{Q}_p$.

Lean: `New.Period.Bmax.fundamental_sequence_surjective` (Lib/PadicHodge/PeriodRings/DeRhamPeriods.lean), `New.Period.Bmax.exists_padic_eq_of_frobEnd_fixed` (Lib/PadicHodge/Crystalline/FixedRatios.lean).

Statement 1 with $a = 0$ is the surjectivity at the right. With $\beta = 0$ it says that $\varphi - 1$ maps $B_{\max}$ onto $B_{\max}$. Statement 2 is the exactness at $B_{\max}^{\varphi=1}$, and $\mathbb{Q}_p \to B_{\max}^{\varphi=1}$ is injective because $B_{\max}$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra.

*Proof of 1.* Every $\beta \in B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is congruent modulo $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ to some $y_0 \in B_{\max}$ (`New.Period.Bmax.exists_coe_eq_sub`), since $\xi$ is a unit of $B_{\max}$. For every $a \in B_{\max}$ there is $y \in B_{\max} \cap B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ with $\varphi(y) - y = a$ (`New.Period.Bmax.exists_frobEnd_sub_eq_and_coe_eq`), by the Artin-Schreier-Witt equation of Section 2 and successive approximation. Then $y_0 + y_1$, with $y_1$ the solution for $a - (\varphi(y_0) - y_0)$, works. $\square$

*Proof of 2.* A lemma on bounded poles (`New.Period.pow_smul_mem_range_BPlus`) gives $y \in B^+$. The contraction lemma then gives that $p^{m+1}y$ comes from some $a \in A_{\inf}$ with $\varphi(a) = a$, and the fixed points of $\varphi$ on $W(O^\flat)$ are $\mathbb{Z}_p$. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The register of findings records as F-056 that the source uses $B_{\max}$ in place of Fontaine's $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$. The docstring of `New.Period.Bmax` says: "The two rings are known to give the same crystalline representations; that comparison is neither proved nor used here." So the exact sequence above is the sequence for $B_{\max}$. The classical sequence for $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is not in the source.
