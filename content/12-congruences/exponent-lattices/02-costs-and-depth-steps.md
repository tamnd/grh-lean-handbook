## Costs and depth steps

The depth lemma of the next section moves the value of an element at $p_a = \varphi^a(E)$ one step down the Hodge filtration. Each step uses an element $g$ of $\Gamma'$ and pays a power of $p$. This section finds $g$ and computes the power. All the work is in $A_{\inf}$ and in $\mathcal{O}_C$, through the maps $\theta$ and $\theta_a = \theta \circ \varphi^{-a}$. The kernel of $\theta_a$ is generated by $p_a$: an element $r$ is divisible by $p_a$ if and only if $\theta_a(r) = 0$ ([Kisin frames](/chapter/kisin-frames/)).

### The cost

**Definition (Cost).** For $a \geq 0$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the *cost* of the step $n$ at $p_a$ is $c_a(n) = v_p(n) + d + 3a$, where $d$ is the different bound of the chart and $v_p(0) = 0$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.cost` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The source uses `padicValInt p n`, which is $0$ at $n = 0$. So $c_a(0) = d + 3a$. The step $n = 0$ is the first step of the depth lemma, and it has its own lemma below. The different bound $d$ is $0$ when $e = 1$, so for an unramified chart $c_a(n) = v_p(n) + 3a$. The constant $c_0(n)$ is $0$ when $e = 1$ and $p \nmid n$: such a step is free.

**Example.** For an unramified chart, $c_0(n) = v_p(n)$, $c_1(1) = 3$ and $c_2(p^2) = 8$. For $e = p$, $d = 2$ and $c_0(1) = 2$.

### Four divisibilities

The coordinate $z$ and the element $\tau = [\varepsilon^{1/p}] - 1$ give $\delta = z\tau$. For every $g \in \Gamma'$ and $s \in S$, $\delta E$ divides $g s - s$ ([Kisin frames](/chapter/kisin-frames/)). Each $g \in \Gamma'$ multiplies $E$ by a unit: $g E = E \omega$.

**Lemma.** Let $g \in \Gamma'$ and $\omega \in A_{\inf}$ with $E\omega = gE$. Then $\delta$ divides $\omega - 1$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.zTau_dvd_sub_one_of_mul_eq_smul_E` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* The element $\delta E$ divides $gE - E = E(\omega - 1)$. Since $E \neq 0$ and $A_{\inf}$ is a domain, $\delta$ divides $\omega - 1$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $g \in \Gamma'$ with $g z = [\varepsilon]^{p^a} z$, and let $E\omega = gE$. Then $\theta(\omega - 1)$ divides $p^{d + a}\,\theta(\delta)$ in $\mathcal{O}_C$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.theta_sub_one_dvd_pow_mul_theta_zTau` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* By the bound for the different ([Kisin frames](/chapter/kisin-frames/#jump-lattices-and-kisin-modules)) there is a polynomial $F$ over $\Lambda$ with $F(z) = EG$ for some $G \in S$ and $\theta(F'(z)) \mid p^d$. The element $E\tau$ is associated to $\mu = [\varepsilon] - 1$: $E\tau u = \mu$ for a unit $u$. Put $w = \tau u \bigl(\sum_{j < p^a} [\varepsilon]^j\bigr) z$. Then

$$g z - z = ([\varepsilon]^{p^a} - 1) z = \mu \sum_{j < p^a} [\varepsilon]^j z = E w.$$

Taylor's formula gives $r \in A_{\inf}$ with $F(z + Ew) = F(z) + F'(z) E w + r E^2 w^2$. The element $g$ fixes $\Lambda$, so $F(gz) = gF(z) = gE \cdot gG = E\omega \cdot gG$. Divide by $E$:

$$\omega \cdot gG = G + F'(z) w + E r w^2.$$

Apply $\theta$. Since $\theta(E) = 0$ and $g$ fixes $\theta(G)$ (the elements of $\Gamma'$ fix the chart), we get $\theta(\omega - 1)\theta(G) = \theta(F'(z))\theta(w)$. Here $\theta([\varepsilon]) = 1$, so $\theta(w) = \theta(\tau)\theta(u) p^a \theta(z)$. So $\theta(\omega - 1)$ divides $\theta(F'(z))\theta(w)$, which divides $p^d \theta(w) = p^{d+a}\theta(\delta)\theta(u)$. As $\theta(u)$ is a unit, the claim follows. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $r \in A_{\inf}$ be divisible by $\delta$. Then $p\,\theta(r)\theta(z)$ divides $\theta(r)^p$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.p_mul_theta_mul_theta_z_dvd_pow` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* Write $r = z\tau d$. The element $\theta(\tau) + 1 = \theta([\varepsilon^{1/p}])$ is a $p$-th root of unity, so $(\theta(\tau) + 1)^p = 1$. Expand: $(\theta\tau + 1)^p = \theta\tau^p + 1 + p\,\theta\tau\, t'$ for some $t'$. So $p\,\theta\tau$ divides $\theta\tau^p$. Also $\theta(z)^2 \mid \theta(z)^p$ as $p \geq 2$, and $\theta(d) \mid \theta(d)^p$. Multiply the three divisibilities: $p\,\theta(\tau)\theta(z)^2\theta(d)$ divides $\theta(\tau)^p\theta(z)^p\theta(d)^p = \theta(r)^p$. The left side is $p\,\theta(r)\theta(z)$. $\square$

**Lemma.** For $a \geq 0$, $\theta(\delta)$ divides $p^{2a}\,\theta_a(\delta)$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.theta_zTau_dvd_pow_mul_thetaPhiInv` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* For $a = 0$, $\theta_0 = \theta$ and $p^0 = 1$. For $a \geq 1$, $\theta(z)$ divides $p$, and $\tau$ divides $\xi - p$ for the element $\xi$ of [period rings](/chapter/period-rings/) with $\theta(\xi) = 0$, so $\theta(\tau)$ divides $\theta(\xi - p) = -p$. So $\theta(\delta)$ divides $p^2$, which divides $p^{2a}$. $\square$

The *lifting of the exponent* in the Kisin library of Volume 7 says: let $f : A_{\inf} \to R$ be a surjective ring map, $v$ a non-unit with $v \mid x - 1$ and $p f(x-1) f(v) \mid f(x-1)^p$; then $f(x^n - 1) \mid p^{v_p(n)} f(x - 1)$ for $n \neq 0$ (`New.Lift.lifting_the_exponent`, see [Kisin frames](/chapter/kisin-frames/)).

### Depth steps

**Definition (Depth step).** Let $a \geq 0$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. A *depth step* for $n$ at $p_a$ is a tuple $(g, \omega, \upsilon, X)$ with $g \in \Gamma'$, $\omega$ a unit of $A_{\inf}$ and $\upsilon, X \in A_{\inf}$, such that:

1. $g p_a = \omega p_a$;
2. $p_a$ divides $g s - s$ for every $s \in S$;
3. $p_a$ divides $\upsilon X - p^{c_a(n)}$;
4. $\delta X = \omega^n - 1$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.IsDepthStep` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

So $g$ acts trivially on $S$ modulo $p_a$, the quotient $X = (\omega^n - 1)/\delta$ exists, and $\upsilon$ is an inverse of $X$ modulo $p_a$ up to the factor $p^{c_a(n)}$.

**Theorem (Depth steps exist).** For $a \geq 0$ and $n \neq 0$ there is a depth step for $n$ at $p_a$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.exists_isDepthStep` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean). The same statement with the four conditions written out is `New.Lift.KummerChart.exists_smul_frobE_eq_mul_and_zTau_mul_eq`.

*Proof.* By property 5 of a Kummer chart there is $g \in \Gamma'$ with $g z = [\varepsilon]^{p^a} z$. Let $\omega_0$ be the unit with $E\omega_0 = gE$. By the first lemma, $\delta \mid \omega_0 - 1$. Then $\delta$ divides $\omega_0^{|n|} - 1$, and $\omega_0^{|n|} - 1$ is associated to $\omega_0^n - 1$: for $n < 0$ they differ by the unit $-\omega_0^{n}$. The element $\delta$ divides $\varphi^a(\delta)$, so $\delta$ divides $\varphi^a(\omega_0^n - 1)$. Write $\varphi^a(\omega_0^n - 1) = \delta X$.

Now bound $\theta_a(X)$. The lifting of the exponent applies with $f = \theta$, $x = \omega_0$ and $v = z$. The coordinate $z$ is not a unit and divides $\omega_0 - 1$, and the hypothesis on $p f(x-1) f(v)$ is the third lemma for $r = \omega_0 - 1$. It gives $\theta(\omega_0^{|n|} - 1) \mid p^{v_p(n)}\theta(\omega_0 - 1)$. With the second and fourth lemmas:

$$\theta_a(\delta)\,\theta_a(X) = \theta(\omega_0^n - 1) \mid p^{v_p(n)} \cdot p^{d+a} \cdot p^{2a}\,\theta_a(\delta) = \theta_a(\delta)\, p^{c_a(n)}.$$

The element $p_a$ does not divide $\delta$, so $\theta_a(\delta) \neq 0$. Cancel it: $\theta_a(X) \mid p^{c_a(n)}$. So $p^{c_a(n)} = \theta_a(X) \cdot y$ for some $y \in \mathcal{O}_C$. Since $\theta$ is surjective, $y = \theta(\upsilon_0)$ for some $\upsilon_0$.

Take $\omega = \varphi^a(\omega_0)$ and $\upsilon = \varphi^a(\upsilon_0)$. Then:

1. $g p_a = \varphi^a(gE) = \varphi^a(E\omega_0) = \omega p_a$, since $g$ commutes with $\varphi$.
2. $p_a$ divides $g z - z = ([\varepsilon]^{p^a} - 1)z$, since $[\varepsilon]^{p^a} = \varphi^a([\varepsilon])$ and $\theta_a(\varphi^a[\varepsilon]) = \theta([\varepsilon]) = 1$. As $g$ fixes $\Lambda$, $p_a$ divides $g s - s$ for every $s \in S$.
3. $\theta_a(\upsilon X - p^{c_a(n)}) = \theta(\upsilon_0)\theta_a(X) - p^{c_a(n)} = 0$.
4. $\delta X = \varphi^a(\omega_0^n - 1) = \omega^n - 1$. $\square$

### The step $n = 0$

For $n = 0$ the element $\omega^0 - 1$ is $0$, and the construction above gives nothing. The first step uses an average over $p^{c_a(0)}$ elements of $\Gamma'$ instead.

**Lemma.** The element $\theta_a([\varepsilon])$ is a primitive $p^a$-th root of unity in $\mathcal{O}_C$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.isPrimitiveRoot_thetaPhiInv_teichmuller_epsTilt` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* The element $\varphi^{-a}([\varepsilon])$ is the Teichmüller lift of $\varepsilon^{1/p^a}$, and $\theta$ sends it to the first coordinate of $\varepsilon^{1/p^a}$, which is $\zeta_{p^a}$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $\zeta$ be a primitive $q$-th root of unity in a domain, $q \mid N$ and $q \nmid m$. Then $\sum_{l < N} (\zeta^m)^l = 0$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.sum_pow_eq_zero_of_isPrimitiveRoot` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* $\zeta^m \neq 1$ as $q \nmid m$. Multiply the sum by $\zeta^m - 1$: the result is $\zeta^{mN} - 1 = 0$, as $q \mid N$. Cancel $\zeta^m - 1 \neq 0$. $\square$

**Lemma (The averaging step).** Let $a \geq 0$ and $N = p^{c_a(0)}$. There are $t_0, \ldots, t_{N-1} \in \Gamma'$ such that for every $g \in \Gamma'$ and $s \in S$, $p_a$ divides $g\bigl(\sum_l t_l s\bigr) - \sum_l t_l s$.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.exists_frobE_dvd_smul_sum_sub_sum` (New/Congruences/Lifting/ExponentLattices.lean).

*Proof.* Choose $t_l \in \Gamma'$ with $t_l z = [\varepsilon]^l z$, by property 5 of a Kummer chart. It is enough to show that $\theta_a$ of the average is fixed by $g$. Modulo $p_a$, every $s \in S$ is a sum $\sum_{m < p^a e} w_m z^m$ with $w_m \in \Lambda$. So it is enough to treat one term $y_m = \theta_a(w_m z^m)$. Write $\zeta = \theta_a([\varepsilon])$. Since $t_l$ fixes $\Lambda$ and commutes with $\varphi^{-a}$, $t_l y_m = (\zeta^m)^l y_m$.

If $p^a \mid m$, write $m = p^a n$. Then $z^m = \varphi^a(z)^n$ and $y_m = \theta(w') \theta(z)^n$ for $w' = \varphi^{-a}(w_m) \in \Lambda$. Every element of $\Gamma'$ fixes $\theta(z)$ and $\Lambda$, so $g$ fixes every $t_l y_m$, and the average.

If $p^a \nmid m$, the average is $\bigl(\sum_{l < N}(\zeta^m)^l\bigr) y_m$. By the first lemma $\zeta$ is a primitive $p^a$-th root of unity. Also $a \leq d + 3a = c_a(0)$, so $p^a \mid N$. By the second lemma the sum is $0$. So the average is $0$, and $g$ fixes it. $\square$

### The model case

Let the chart be unramified with $f = 1$ and $a = 0$. Then $c_0(n) = v_p(n)$, and the step $n = 0$ uses $N = 1$ element, $t_0 = 1$: no average is needed, since $\theta(z)$ is fixed by $\Gamma'$ and every $s$ is a constant modulo $E$. For $n$ prime to $p$ and $\omega_0 - 1 = \delta X_1$, the quotient $X = (\omega_0^n - 1)/\delta$ is $X_1 (1 + \omega_0 + \cdots + \omega_0^{n-1})$, and $\theta(\omega_0) \equiv 1$ modulo the maximal ideal, as $\theta(\delta)$ divides $p^2$. So $\theta$ of the sum is congruent to $n$, a unit. The step is free, as the cost says. A step $n = p$ costs one factor $p$, by the lifting of the exponent.
