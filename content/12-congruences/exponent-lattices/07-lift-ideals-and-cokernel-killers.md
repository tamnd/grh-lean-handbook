## Lift ideals and cokernel killers

A side $S$ gives the exact sequence $0 \to Y \to Y \times C \to C \to 0$. On Kisin modules the map to $C$ need not be onto. This section measures the cokernel by an ideal of $A_{\inf}$ and puts $p^\kappa E^s$ into it. The tools are the Frobenius, the derivations $\alpha(g - 1)/z\tau$ of [Section 2](/chapter/exponent-lattices/#costs-and-depth-steps), and Nakayama's lemma.

### The ideals

**Definition.** Let $S$ be a side, $r, l \in \mathbb{N}$ and $i$ a letter.

1. The *constant* of $c \in C$ at $i$ is the projection of $1 \otimes c$ to the letter $i$.
2. The *lift ideal* $J_l^r(i)$ is generated by $r$ and by the $s \in A_{\inf}$ such that $s$ times the letter-$i$ part of $A_{\inf} \otimes C$ lies in the image under $\mathrm{pr}$ of the elements of $W(C^\flat) \otimes (Y \times C)$ with the standard orders of level $l$ at $i$.
3. $p^\kappa E^s$ *kills the cokernel at $i$* if $p^\kappa E^s \in J_0^0(i)$.

By [Section 5](/chapter/exponent-lattices/#hodge-expansions), the elements with the standard orders of level $l$ at $i$ are those of $\varphi^l(M) \cap \mathcal{A}_i$.

**Remark (Formalization).** The modulus $r$ is a natural number. With $r = 0$ the ideal holds the multipliers that lift constants exactly. With $r = p^{\kappa+1}$ the lifts are exact only modulo $p^{\kappa+1}$. The arguments of `KillsCokernel` come in the order $(i, \kappa, s)$.

**Lemma (Basic rules).**

1. $s \in J_l^r(i)$ if and only if there is $t \in A_{\inf}$ such that, for every $c \in C$, $(s - rt)$ times the constant of $c$ at $i$ is $\mathrm{pr}(x)$ for some $x$ with the standard orders of level $l$ at $i$.
2. $p^\kappa \in J_l^{p^\kappa}(i)$.
3. If $s \in J_0^r(i)$ and all jumps at $i$ are at most $n$, then $sE^n \in J_1^r(i)$.
4. (Nakayama) If $p^c \in J_l^{p^{c+1}}(i)$, then $J_l^{p^{c+1}}(i) \subseteq J_l^0(i)$. So if $p^\kappa \in J_0^{p^{\kappa+1}}(i)$, then $p^\kappa$ kills the cokernel at $i$.

*Proof.* (1) and (2) unfold the definition. (3) Multiply each lift by $E^n = p_0^n$. At level $0$ the order goes from $-u$ to $n - u \geq 0$, and the other orders stay; this is the twist rule of [Section 3](/chapter/exponent-lattices/#lattices-with-prescribed-orders). (4) Write $I = J_l^0(i)$. The ideal $J_l^{p^{c+1}}(i)$ is $I + (p)(p^c)$, and the hypothesis gives $(p^c) \subseteq I + (p)(p^c)$. As $A_{\inf}$ is $p$-adically complete, $p$ is in the Jacobson radical, and Nakayama's lemma gives $(p^c) \subseteq I$. $\square$

### Constants and Frobenius

**Lemma.** (1) If $\Gamma$ acts trivially on $L$, then $M$ is the $S$-span of the vectors $1 \otimes t$. (2) If $\Gamma$ acts on $L$ through $\chi^c$ and $x$ has the standard orders of level $0$ at $i$, then $\tau^c x \in A_{\inf} \otimes L$. (3) The constant of $c$ at $i$ is in the standard part of level $l$ at $i$ of the trivial lattice. (4) $\varphi^l s \in J_l^r(i^{p^l})$ if and only if $s \in J_0^r(i)$. (5) $p^cz^{pa} \in J_1^r(i^p)$ if and only if $p^cz^a \in J_0^r(i)$.

### Derivations keep the ideals

**Lemma.** (1) If $x \in N_{\mathrm{loc}}$ has the standard orders of level $1$ at $i$ and $g \in \Gamma'$, then $gx - x$ has the order $1$ at level $0$ and $-u$ at levels $a \geq 1$. (2) Let $d \in A_{\inf}$ be a nonzero divisor on $W(C^\flat) \otimes C$ that divides $gt - t$ for all $t \in S$, and let $gx - x = dy$ with $y$ of standard orders for every $x$ in the standard part of level $l$. If $f \in S \cap J_l^r(i)$ and $ds = \alpha(gf - f)$, then $s \in J_l^r(i)$. (3) If $f \in S \cap J_l^r(i)$ and $z\tau(s + \beta f) = \alpha(gf - f)$, then $s \in J_l^r(i)$. (4) If $f \in S \cap J_1^r(i)$ and $z\tau E(s + \beta f) = \alpha(gf - f)$, then $s \in J_1^r(i)$.

*Proof.* (1) The value of $x$ at level $0$ lies in $\mathcal{K}_0 \cap \mathrm{Fil}^0$. As $E$ divides $gs - s$ for $s \in S$, the first-order action of [Section 4](/chapter/exponent-lattices/#invariance-and-the-depth-lemma) puts $g$ of the value minus the value in $\mathrm{Fil}^1$. At levels $a \geq 1$ nothing changes. (2) Lift $(f - rt)$ times the constant of $c$ to $x$, by the lemma on lifts. Let $gt - t = dv$ and $gx - x = dy$. The element $g$ fixes the constant. So
$$d\,\mathrm{pr}(\alpha y) = \alpha\,\mathrm{pr}(gx - x) = \alpha\bigl(gf - f - r(gt - t)\bigr)\cdot\text{constant} = d\,(s - r\alpha v)\cdot\text{constant}.$$
Cancel $d$. So $\alpha y$ lifts $(s - r\alpha v)$ times the constant. (3) Take $d = z\tau$. Division by the unit $z\tau$ keeps the standard orders, by the triviality modulo $\delta$ of [Section 3](/chapter/exponent-lattices/#lattices-with-prescribed-orders). Then $s + \beta f \in J$ by (2), and $\beta f \in J$. (4) Take $d = z\tau E$. By (1), $gx - x$ gains one order at level $0$, and division by $E$ uses it up. $\square$

### Monomials in the ideals

**Lemma (Moves of monomials).** Let $d$ be the different bound.

1. (Down a letter) If $p^cz^n \in J_1^r(i^p)$, $pa \leq n$ and $c + v_p(n!) \leq \kappa + v_p((pa)!)$, then $p^\kappa z^a \in J_0^r(i)$.
2. ($E$ for $z$) If $p^cz^aE^n \in J_1^r(i)$, $c + dn + v_p(n!) \leq b$ and $a + n \leq \kappa$, then $p^bz^\kappa \in J_1^r(i)$.
3. (Lower $z$) If $p^cz^n \in J_0^r(i)$ and $n \geq 1$, then $p^{c + v_p(n) + 1}z^{n-1} \in J_0^r(i)$. Hence if $m \leq n$ and $c + n + v_p(n!) \leq \kappa + m + v_p(m!)$, then $p^\kappa z^m \in J_0^r(i)$.
4. (Back a letter) If all jumps at $j^p$ are at most $\omega$ and $p^cz^n \in J_0^r(j^p)$, then $p^{c + d\omega + v_p(\omega!)}z^{\lfloor (n+\omega)/p\rfloor} \in J_0^r(j)$.

$$z\tau E\bigl(p^{c + v_p(n+1)}z^n + \beta f\bigr) = \alpha(tf - f).$$
By (4) of the last lemma, $p^{c+v_p(n+1)}z^n \in J_1^r(i^p)$. The cost adds up as $v_p((n+1)!) = v_p(n+1) + v_p(n!)$. At $n = pa$, use (5) of the lemma on constants.

Also $gz = z(1 + \tau Ev)$, so $g(z^a) = z^a(1 + \tau Evb_2)$. For $f = p^cz^aE^{n+1}$ this gives
$$z\tau E\bigl(p^{c + \mathrm{cost}(0,n+1)}z^{a+1}E^n + \beta f\bigr) = z\upsilon(gf - f)$$
for some $\beta$. By (4) of the last lemma, one factor $E$ becomes one factor $z$ at the cost $\mathrm{cost}(0, n+1) = v_p(n+1) + d$.

### Cokernel killers

**Theorem (Small jumps).** If all jumps of $S$ are at most $\omega$ and $e\omega \leq p - 2$, then $1$ kills the cokernel at every letter.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.PairingSide.killsCokernel_zero_zero_of_small_jumps` (New/Congruences/Lifting/CupProductBound.lean).

*Proof.* Take $\kappa = 0$ in the last lemma: $(p-1)a \leq e\omega \leq p - 2$ forces $a = 0$, so $1 \in J_0^p(i)$. Nakayama with $c = 0$ drops the modulus. $\square$

**Theorem (Unramified charts).** Let the chart be unramified and $p \geq 5$. If all jumps of $S$ are at most $(p - 3)(s + t)$, then $p^sE^t$ kills the cokernel at every letter.

Lean: `New.Lift.KummerChart.PairingSide.killsCokernel_of_isUnramified` (New/Congruences/Lifting/CupProductBound.lean).

*Proof.* Let $N = s + t$. The last lemma gives $z^b \in J_0^{p^{N+1}}(i)$ with $(p-1)b \leq (p-2)N$, as $e = 1$. Legendre's formula gives $(p-1)v_p(b!) \leq b$. So $(p-2)(b + v_p(b!)) \leq (p-1)b \leq (p-2)N$, and $b + v_p(b!) \leq N$. For each $m$, lower $z^{\max(b,m)}$ to $z^m$ by (3) of the moves: $p^{N-m}z^m \in J_0^{p^{N+1}}(i)$. With $m = 0$, Nakayama drops the modulus. As $E = z + p$, $p^sE^t = \sum_m \binom{t}{m}p^{s+t-m}z^m$, and each term lies in the ideal. $\square$

**Theorem (Thin cuts).** Let all jumps of $S$ be at most $\omega$, and let the jumps at the letters $i^{p^t}$ with $1 \leq t \leq J$ be at most $\omega'$. Let $8e \leq p^{J+1}$ and
$$\kappa \geq 2Jd\omega' + \frac{(2J+4)\omega'}{p-1} + \frac{8e\omega}{p^{J+1}}.$$
Then $p^\kappa$ kills the cokernel at $i$.

*Proof.* The last lemma gives $z^a \in J_0^{p^{\kappa+1}}(j)$ for all $j$ with $(p-1)a \leq e(\omega+\kappa)$. Apply (4) of the moves $J$ times, from $i^{p^J}$ back to $i$. This gives $p^cz^n \in J_0^{p^{\kappa+1}}(i)$ with $c \leq J(d\omega' + v_p(\omega'!))$ and $n \leq a/p^J + \omega'/(p-1)$. Lower $z^n$ to $z^0$ by (3) of the moves, at the cost $n + v_p(n!)$. It remains to show $c + n + v_p(n!) \leq \kappa$. By Legendre, $v_p(\omega'!) \leq \omega'/(p-1)$ and $v_p(n!) \leq n$. From $pa \leq 2(p-1)a \leq 2e(\omega+\kappa)$ and $8e \leq p^{J+1}$,
$$\frac{a}{p^J} \leq \frac{2e\omega}{p^{J+1}} + \frac{\kappa}{4}.$$
So $c + 2n \leq J(d\omega' + \tfrac{\omega'}{p-1}) + 2(\tfrac{2e\omega}{p^{J+1}} + \tfrac{\kappa}{4} + \tfrac{\omega'}{p-1})$, which is half the bound on $\kappa$ plus $\kappa/2$. So $p^\kappa \in J_0^{p^{\kappa+1}}(i)$, and Nakayama finishes. $\square$

The thin-cut theorem has no condition on $p$. The unramified theorem needs $p \geq 5$; see the notes.
