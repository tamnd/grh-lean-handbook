## The operator at one place and the deformed norm

This section builds the third main result of the chapter. At each auxiliary place $w_j$ the norm $\mathrm{Nm}_j$ from principal level to base level kills the traces of $\gamma_j - 1$. A first-order term $\partial_j$ turns these traces into traces of $(\varphi_j - 1) H_j(\varphi_j)$. The product $\prod_j (\mathrm{Nm}_j + \alpha_j \partial_j)$ then kills the tied relations modulo $\ell^\nu / (2 n!)$. The root chains of [the last section](/chapter/first-order-deformations/#root-chains-of-weak-lifts) enter as a hypothesis on the systems that the operators must not move.

### The setting

The objects of this section are defined in the next chapter, [tied traces and even classes](/chapter/tied-traces/). The source file of this section imports them from there. We recall them here.

Fix base data $D$ of rank $n$ and Taylor-Wiles places $Q = (w_j)_{j \in J}$ of level $\ell^\nu$, off the base level ([building the representations](/chapter/building-representations/)). Forms of *principal level* have principal level at the $w_j$. Those of *base level* have maximal level there. The forms of base level are the forms of principal level that are fixed by the finite group $\Gamma = \prod_j \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$. Write $\mathrm{Nm} = \sum_{g \in \Gamma} g$ for the norm, and $\mathrm{Nm}_j$ for the sum over the factor $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$. Let $c$ be the common multiple of the stabilizer orders fixed in $D$. It is the constant of [the Higman operator](/decl/New.Patching.BaseLevel.exists_higman_operator/): an integral operator $\Psi$ with $\sum_{g} g^{-1} \Psi g = c$.

- An *integral operator* is an endomorphism of the forms of principal level that keeps the integral forms. The *operators at $w_j$* are the integral operators that come from integral combinations of translations supported at $w_j$ and its conjugate. Operators at two different places commute (see below).
- A *system* is an isotypic space of forms that contains a nonzero form of principal level. The *Hecke algebra* $\mathbb{T}$ is the algebra of $\ell$-adic functions $s$ on systems such that scaling each system $x$ by $s(x)$ keeps the integral forms. It acts by integral operators, and these commute with $\Gamma$ and with the operators at each $w_j$.
- Let $a \colon G \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ be a character with $a(\gamma_j) = 1$ at the tame generators $\gamma_j$, and let $N_x = a \otimes \rho_x$. For a polynomial $P$ and $h, g \in G$, the *trace element* $\mathrm{tr}[P(h) g]$ is the function $x \mapsto \mathrm{tr}(P(N_x(h)) N_x(g))$, an element of $\mathbb{T}$.
- The *tied ideal* $\mathfrak{a}$ is the ideal of $\mathbb{T}[\alpha_j : j \in J]$ generated by the $\ell^\nu \mathrm{tr}[g]$ and the traces $\mathrm{tr}[r_j g]$ of the *tied relations* $r_j = \gamma_j - 1 - \alpha_j (\varphi_j - 1) H_j(\varphi_j)$, for all $g \in G$.
- A ring map $\sigma$ on integral operators is a *reduction modulo $\ell^\nu/(2n!)$* if it kills every $T$ with $2 n!\, T \in \ell^\nu \cdot \mathrm{End}$. We write $T \equiv T'$ for $\sigma(T) = \sigma(T')$.

The factor $2 n!$ comes from the proofs of the next chapter, which compare weights that differ by $n!$.

### The operator at one place

**Theorem (The operator $\partial$).** Let the places $w_j$ be off the base level, let $N_x = a \otimes \rho_x$ with $a(\gamma_j) = 1$ for all $j$, fix $j$, and write $q$ for the norm of $w_j$. Let $t \neq 0$, let $H$ be an integral polynomial, and let $\delta \in \mathbb{T}$. Suppose that the Hecke polynomial at $w_j$ of every system of base level with $\delta(x) \neq 0$ has a root chain of length $t$ at $a(\varphi_j)^{-1}$ with cofactor dividing $H$. Let $\sigma$ be a reduction modulo $\ell^\nu / (2 n!)$. Then there is an operator $\partial$ at $w_j$ with these properties.

1. $\mathrm{Nm}_j \cdot \mathrm{tr}[(\gamma_j - 1) g] = 0$ for all $g \in G$.
2. $\partial \cdot \mathrm{tr}[(\gamma_j - 1) g] \equiv \mathrm{Nm}_j \cdot \mathrm{tr}[(\varphi_j - 1) H(\varphi_j) g]$ for all $g \in G$.
3. Each $g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$ has an integral operator $Z$ with $c\, \partial (g - 1) \equiv \mathrm{Nm}_j Z$ and $\delta\, \mathrm{Nm}\, Z = 0$.

Lean: `New.Patching.BaseLevel.exists_mul_traceElement_tameGenerator_eq_frob` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/AuxiliaryPrimeTie.lean).

Condition 1 says that the tame generator acts trivially at base level, up to the trace. Condition 2 is the first-order tie of tame inertia to Frobenius. Condition 3 says that $\partial$ is a norm on the systems where $\delta \neq 0$, up to $c$. It makes the products of the $\partial_j$ factor through $\mathrm{Nm}$ in the theorem on the family.

*Proof.* Write $t = t' + 1$ and $p = (X - q^{t'}) H$. Let $K \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$ be the stabilizer of a line, and fix a logarithm $\log \colon \mathbb{F}_{w_j}^\times \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu$. It exists since $\ell^\nu \mid q - 1$. Let $L$ be the sum over $K$ weighted by $\log$ of the first entry, and let $U$ be the Hecke operator of $\mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1, \dots, 1)$, with $\tilde U = a(\varphi_j) U$. The next chapter gives an operator $B = p(\tilde U) L$ at $w_j$. Put
$$\partial = -\mathrm{Tr} \cdot B \cdot \mathrm{Tr}',$$
where $\mathrm{Tr}$ and $\mathrm{Tr}'$ are the sums over fixed representatives $h$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})/K$ and over their inverses. It is an operator at $w_j$.

Condition 1 is a result of the next chapter: since $a(\gamma_j) = 1$, the tame generator acts through a diamond element, and the diamond element fixes the norm.

For condition 2, the operator $\mathrm{Tr}'$ commutes with $\mathbb{T}$. In $\partial \cdot \mathrm{tr}[(\gamma_j - 1) g]$, the factor $p(\tilde U)$ becomes $p(\varphi_j)$ inside the trace under $\sigma$, by a result of the next chapter. Conjugation by the representatives sums the logarithms over the lines to the logarithm of the determinant, modulo $\ell^\nu$. The tame generator acts as a diamond element $\langle \gamma \rangle$, and $(\langle \gamma \rangle - 1)$ times the sum weighted by $\log \det$ is $\mathrm{Nm}_j$ under $\sigma$. This leaves $\mathrm{Nm}_j\, \mathrm{tr}[(\varphi_j - q^{t'}) H(\varphi_j) g]$. Since $\ell^\nu$ divides $q - 1$, $\sigma$ maps $q$ to $1$, and the factor $\varphi_j - q^{t'}$ becomes $\varphi_j - 1$ (`New.Patching.BaseLevel.map_traceElement_sub_pow_mul`).

For condition 3, the group $K$ fixes $U' = B_1$, the operator $B$ at the constant weight $1$, and right multiplication by $k \in K$ shifts the weight by $\log$ of the first entry of $k$. The Higman operator for $K$ gives $\Psi$ with $\sum_{k \in K} k^{-1} \Psi k = c$. The next chapter shows that $\delta\, \mathrm{Nm}\, U' = 0$. Here the root chain of length $t$ enters: by [the chain lemma](/chapter/iwahori-satake/), a form of base level in a system with $\delta \neq 0$ is orthogonal to the values of $U'$. The ring of integral operators has no $\#K$-torsion. A result of the next chapter on such derivatives then gives $Z$. $\square$

### Commuting operators

**Lemma.** Operators at two different auxiliary places commute.

*Proof.* The places are apart: $w_i$, $w_j$ and their conjugates are distinct. Translations supported at disjoint sets of places commute, and so do their integral combinations. $\square$

### The deformed norm

For $S \subseteq J$ write $\alpha_S = \prod_{j \in S} \alpha_j$.

**Theorem (The deformed norm).** Let the places $w_j$ be off the base level, and let $N_x = a \otimes \rho_x$ with $a(\gamma_j) = 1$ for all $j$. Let $H_j$ be integral polynomials, let $t \neq 0$, and let $\delta' \in \mathbb{T}$. Suppose that at each $w_j$, of norm $q_j$, the Hecke polynomial of every system of base level with $\delta'(x) \neq 0$ has a root chain of length $t$ at $a(\varphi_j)^{-1}$ with cofactor dividing $H_j$. Let $\sigma$ be a reduction modulo $\ell^\nu/(2 n!)$. Then there are integral operators $\mathcal{N}_S$ and $Y_S$, for $S \subseteq J$, with these properties.

1. Each $\mathcal{N}_S$ commutes with $\mathbb{T}$, and $\mathcal{N}_\emptyset = \mathrm{Nm}$.
2. For every $G \in \mathfrak{a}$, write $G_S$ for the coefficient of $\alpha_S$ in $G$. Then $\sum_{S \subseteq J} \mathcal{N}_{J \setminus S}\, G_S \equiv 0$.
3. For every $S$, $(c^2 \delta')^{\#J}\, \mathcal{N}_S \equiv Y_S\, \mathrm{Nm}$.
4. $Y_\emptyset = (c^2 \delta')^{\#J}$.

Lean: `New.Patching.BaseLevel.exists_deformed_norm_family` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/AuxiliaryPrimeTie.lean).

Read $\mathcal{N} = \sum_S \mathcal{N}_S \alpha_S$ as a polynomial. Condition 2 says that the coefficient of $\alpha_J$ in $\mathcal{N} G$ vanishes under $\sigma$, when only square-free monomials count, as in a ring with $\alpha_j^2 = 0$. Condition 3 says that, after one factor $c^2\delta'$ for each place, every coefficient of $\mathcal{N}$ factors through the norm to base level.

*Proof.* For each $j$ choose $\partial_j$ by the last theorem, with $\delta = \delta'$. The subrings of operators at the $w_j$ commute with each other by the lemma, and they contain $\partial_j$ and the image of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$. Let $\mathcal{N}_S$ be the coefficient of $\alpha_S$ in $\prod_j (\mathrm{Nm}_j + \alpha_j \partial_j)$. Then $\mathcal{N}_\emptyset = \prod_j \mathrm{Nm}_j = \mathrm{Nm}$. For $j \notin S$ there is $\mathcal{N}'$ with $\mathcal{N}_S = \mathcal{N}' \mathrm{Nm}_j$ and $\mathcal{N}_{S \cup \{j\}} = \mathcal{N}' \partial_j$. Each $\mathcal{N}_S$ commutes with every operator that commutes with all the local subrings, so with $\mathbb{T}$.

For condition 3, take the Higman operator for $\Gamma$. Condition 3 of the last theorem lets each factor $\partial_j$ be moved into the norm, at the cost of one factor $c^2\delta'$. The general step is a result of the next chapter. It gives $Y_S$ for $\#S \leq \#J$, with $Y_\emptyset = (c^2\delta')^{\#J}$.

For condition 2, it is enough to prove the claim for all multiples $G' G$, with $G$ a generator, by induction over the span. If $G = \ell^\nu \mathrm{tr}[g]$, then $\sigma$ kills $\ell^\nu$. If $G = \mathrm{tr}[r_j g]$, write $G = \tau - \alpha_j \rho$ with $\tau = \mathrm{tr}[(\gamma_j - 1) g]$ and $\rho = \mathrm{tr}[(\varphi_j - 1) H_j(\varphi_j) g]$. The coefficient of $\alpha_J$ in $\mathcal{N} G' (\tau - \alpha_j \rho)$ is a sum of pairs. Each pair has the form $\mathcal{N}' (\partial_j \tau - \mathrm{Nm}_j \rho)$ or $\mathcal{N}' \mathrm{Nm}_j \tau$, times a coefficient of $G'$. The first vanishes under $\sigma$ by condition 2 of the last theorem, and the second vanishes by condition 1. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The source states condition 2 only for the coefficient of $\alpha_J$. Since $\mathfrak{a}$ is an ideal, this covers every square-free monomial: multiply $G$ by $\alpha_{J \setminus S}$. The source does not form the quotient by the $\alpha_j^2$. It reads only the square-free coefficients, which has the same effect.

### One place

Let $J = \{j\}$. Then $\mathcal{N} = \mathrm{Nm} + \alpha \partial$, and the tied ideal has the generators $\tau - \alpha\rho$ as above. The product is
$$(\mathrm{Nm} + \alpha \partial)(\tau - \alpha \rho) = \mathrm{Nm}\, \tau + \alpha (\partial \tau - \mathrm{Nm}\, \rho) - \alpha^2 \partial \rho.$$
The constant term is $0$ by condition 1. The coefficient of $\alpha$ is $\equiv 0$ by condition 2. The term in $\alpha^2$ does not count. So the deformed norm kills the tied relation to first order. Condition 3 reads $c^2\delta'\, \partial \equiv Y_{\{j\}} \mathrm{Nm}$: on the systems where $\delta' \neq 0$, the first-order term lands in the image of the norm.
