## Selmer data, tied Selmer groups and Sah's lemma

This section defines the objects of the chapter: a Selmer datum, its global and local terms, its tied Selmer groups and the new auxiliary primes. It then proves Sah's lemma in the form the source uses: a cocycle that vanishes at $\gamma_*$ is determined by its values on the group $H$ generated by commutators with $\gamma_*$ and $\gamma_*^c$, and if it has a unit value, its values on $H$ span $W$. All results are in [SelmerData.lean](lean:New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean#L1).

Background used: tied primes and tie relations from [auxiliary primes](/chapter/tied-traces/#auxiliary-primes); relations, the module $\mathrm{Obey}(L, B)$ of functions that obey $L$, and the relations $\mathrm{Rel}(Z)$ that hold on a lattice $Z$, from [relations and cocycles](/chapter/fitting-ideals/#relations-and-cocycles).

### The global and local terms

Let $\rho \colon G \to M_n(O)$ be a monoid homomorphism and $W_a = (O/a)^n$ with $g$ acting by $\rho(g)$. Its *character module* $W_a^* = \mathrm{Hom}(W_a, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ carries the action $g \cdot y = \mathrm{cyc}(g)\, y \circ \rho(g)^{-1}$. On $W_a^{*c}$ the element $g$ acts as $g^c$ acts on $W_a^*$.

**Definition (Conjugate dual maps).** $\mathrm{Hom}_G(W_a, W_a^{*c})$ is the $O$-module of $O$-linear maps $\eta \colon W_a \to W_a^*$ with
$$\eta(\rho(g) u)(m) = \eta(u)\big(\mathrm{cyc}(g^c)\, \rho((g^c)^{-1}) m\big) \qquad \text{for all } g \in G,\ u, m \in W_a.$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.conjDualHom` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

**Definition (Global term).** The global term of $\rho$ is
$$\mathrm{glob}(\rho) = \sup_{a \neq 0} \mathrm{len}\, \mathrm{Hom}_G(W_a, W_a^{*c}) \in \{0, 1, \dots, \infty\}.$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.congruenceTerm` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

For a place $A$ of $\Omega$ and relations $L$, let $\mathrm{Loc}(\rho, L, A, B)$ be the module of restrictions to the decomposition group $D_A$ of the cocycles in $\mathrm{Sel}(\rho, \text{all places}, L, B)$ (`New.Selmer.localCocycles`, from the file TameSelmerCocycles of [Selmer groups and the Greenberg-Wiles formula](/chapter/local-duality/#selmer-groups-and-the-greenberg-wiles-formula)). For a finite place $v$ of $E$ let $A_v$ be the chosen place above it (`New.placeOver`).

**Definition (Local terms).** For a finite place $v$,
$$\mathrm{loc}_v(\rho) = \sup_{a \neq 0} \big(\mathrm{len}\, \mathrm{Loc}(\rho, \emptyset, A_v, O/a) - \mathrm{len}\, W_a\big),$$
with truncated subtraction. For a finite set $S$, $\mathrm{places}(\rho, S) = \sum_{v \in S,\ v \nmid \ell} \mathrm{loc}_v(\rho)$. For $g_0 \in G$, $\mathrm{first}(\rho, g_0) = \sum_{b} v\big(\det(\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1)\big)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.localTerm`, `New.Selmer.placeTerms`, `New.Selmer.firstPrimeTerms` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

The textbook local term at a place $v \nmid \ell$ is the length of $H^0(E_v, W_a^*)$. By the local Euler characteristic formula and local duality (Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, *Cohomology of Number Fields*, Chapter VII, §§2 and 3), the module $Z^1(D_v, W_a)$ of all local cocycles has length $\mathrm{len}\, W_a + \mathrm{len}\, H^0(E_v, W_a^*)$. The source takes the restrictions of global cocycles instead.

**Remark (Formalization).** The source's $\mathrm{Loc}(\rho, \emptyset, A_v, O/a)$ is a submodule of $Z^1(D_v, W_a)$, so its excess is at most the textbook term. The subtraction is truncated at $0$ and a supremum of excesses may be $\infty$. The datum below asks for finiteness as a field.

### Selmer data

**Definition (Selmer datum).** Let $S_0$ be a finite set of finite places and $g_0 \in G$. A *Selmer datum* $T$ for $S_0$ and $g_0$ consists of:

1. a nonempty finite index set $n$ and a monoid homomorphism $\rho \colon G \to M_n(O)$ whose reductions modulo every $\ell^M$ are locally constant;
2. a finite set $S = T.\mathrm{places} \subseteq S_0$ that contains the places above $\ell$, such that $\rho(g) = 1$ for $g$ in the inertia groups above places outside $S$;
3. for each $w \mid \ell$, a finitely generated submodule $Z_w$ of the functions $D_{A_w} \to O^n$ whose members are cocycles and which contains the coboundaries $g \mapsto \rho(g) b - b$, with
$$\sum_{w \mid \ell} \mathrm{rk}\, Z_w \leq (\#\{w \mid \ell\} + r_\infty) \cdot n,$$
where $r_\infty$ is the number of infinite places of $E$;
4. the span over $O$ of the matrices $\rho(g)$ is all of $M_n(O)$;
5. $\mathrm{glob}(\rho) \neq \infty$ and $\mathrm{places}(\rho, S) + \mathrm{first}(\rho, g_0) \neq \infty$;
6. an element $\gamma_*$ in an inertia group above a place of $S$ prime to $\ell$, and $\zeta \in O$, with $\rho(\iota_b(\gamma_*)) = \zeta \cdot 1$ for both $b$ and $\zeta^2 - 1$ a unit.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

Write $\mathrm{glob}(T)$, $\mathrm{places}(T)$ and $\mathrm{first}(T)$ for the three terms of $\rho = T.\rho$ (`globalTerm`, `placesTerm`, `firstPrimeTerm`).

**Remark (Formalization).** Condition 4 is Burnside's form of the absolute irreducibility of $T/\varpi$. It is stronger than the irreducibility of $T \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and it is what the proofs use. Condition 3 is the balance of the local conditions above $\ell$: it reads $\sum_w (\mathrm{rk}\, Z_w - n) \leq r_\infty n$. The element $g_0$ enters only through $\mathrm{first}(T)$, which is finite exactly when $\det(\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1) \neq 0$ for both $b$. Conditions 5 and 6 make the bounds of the chapter finite.

**Definition.** $\beta_T = \mathrm{glob}(T) + \mathrm{places}(T) + \mathrm{first}(T)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.β` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

**Lemma.** $\beta_T \neq \infty$, and $\zeta - 1$ is a unit.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.β_ne_top`, `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.isUnit_ζ_sub_one` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The first claim is condition 5. For the second, $\zeta^2 - 1 = (\zeta + 1)(\zeta - 1)$ is a unit, so each factor is a unit. $\square$

### Local conditions as relations

**Definition (Local relations).** $T.\mathrm{localRelations}$ is the union over $w \mid \ell$ of the relations of $\mathrm{Rel}(Z_w)$, pushed forward from $D_{A_w}$ to $G$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.localRelations` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

**Lemma.** A function $c \colon G \to B^n$ obeys $T.\mathrm{localRelations}$ if and only if, for each $w \mid \ell$, its restriction to $D_{A_w}$ obeys $\mathrm{Rel}(Z_w)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.mem_obeying_localRelations_iff` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* A relation $f$ on $D_{A_w}$, pushed forward to $G$, gives $\sum_{g \in D_{A_w}} f(g) c(g)$ at $c$, which is the value of $f$ at the restriction of $c$. $\square$

**Lemma.** For $b \in W_a$ the coboundary $g \mapsto \rho(g) b - b$ obeys $T.\mathrm{localRelations}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.coboundary_mem_obeying_localRelations` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* Lift $b$ to $\tilde b \in O^n$. The coboundary of $\tilde b$ lies in each $Z_w$, so it obeys every relation of $Z_w$. Reduction modulo $a$ commutes with the evaluation of a relation. Apply the previous lemma. $\square$

**Lemma.** For $a \in O$ and $w \mid \ell$,
$$\mathrm{len}\, \mathrm{Loc}(\rho, T.\mathrm{localRelations}, A_w, O/a) \leq \mathrm{rk}(Z_w) \cdot \mathrm{len}(O/a).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.length_localCocycles_le_finrank_mul` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* A member of the left side is the restriction of a cocycle that obeys $T.\mathrm{localRelations}$, so by the lemma above it obeys $\mathrm{Rel}(Z_w)$. So the left side lies in $\mathrm{Obey}(\mathrm{Rel}(Z_w), O/a)$. The bound by rank and length of [relations and cocycles](/chapter/fitting-ideals/#relations-and-cocycles) (`New.length_obeying_relationsOn_le`) bounds that module by $\mathrm{rk}(Z_w)\,\mathrm{len}(O/a)$, because $Z_w$ is finitely generated and $n$ is nonempty. $\square$

### Tied Selmer groups

**Definition (Class relations).** $T.\mathrm{classRelations} = T.\mathrm{localRelations} \cup \{[\gamma_*]\}$, where the relation $[\gamma_*]$ with coefficient $1$ says $z(\gamma_*) = 0$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.classRelations` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

The relation $[\gamma_*]$ picks one cocycle in each class. A coboundary $g \mapsto \rho(g) b - b$ has value $(\zeta - 1) b$ at $\gamma_*$, and $\zeta - 1$ is a unit, so the only coboundary that vanishes at $\gamma_*$ is $0$. Adding a coboundary to a cocycle $z$ changes $z(\gamma_*)$ by $(\zeta - 1) b$, so exactly one cocycle of each class vanishes at $\gamma_*$.

**Definition (Tied Selmer group).** Let $x_v = (d_v, s_v)$ be auxiliary prime data for $q$ with slopes in $O$, let $R$ and $J$ be finite sets of places, $i$ an index and $a \in O$. Then
$$\mathrm{Sel}^R_J(x, i, a) = \mathrm{Sel}\big(\rho,\ S \cup R \cup \bar R,\ T.\mathrm{classRelations} \cup \mathrm{tie}(J, x, i),\ O/a\big),$$
where $\mathrm{tie}(J, x, i)$ are the tie relations $z(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) = s_v(i)\, z(\iota_b(\varphi_v))$ for $v \in J$ (`New.Selmer.tieRelations`). Write $\mathrm{Sel}(J) = \mathrm{Sel}^J_J$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.tiedSelmer` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

The source uses $R = J$, and $R = J \cup \{P\}$ when it adds a prime $P$ with no tie yet.

**Lemma.** Every $z \in \mathrm{Sel}^R_J(x, i, a)$ has $z(\gamma_*) = 0$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.apply_γ_eq_zero` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The relation $[\gamma_*]$ is one of the defining relations. $\square$

**Definition.** For $g \in G$, $\mathrm{ev}_g(z) = \big(z(\iota_b(g))_k\big)_{(b, k)}$, the values of $z$ at $g$ and at $(g^c)^{-1}$, as a vector indexed by $\{\text{true}, \text{false}\} \times n$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.evalConjPair` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

**Lemma.** Let $z \in \mathrm{Sel}(\rho, S, L, B)$, $S \subseteq P \cup Q$ and $K \subseteq L$. Then $z$ is a Selmer cocycle in the sense of `New.selmerZ1`: unramified outside $P \cup Q$, and at each $v \in P$ its restriction to $D_{A(v)}$ lies in $\mathrm{Loc}(\rho, K, A(v), B)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.mem_selmerZ1_of_mem_selmerModule` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The cocycle $z$ is unramified outside $S$, hence outside $P \cup Q$. Since $K \subseteq L$, $z$ obeys $K$, so $z$ is a global cocycle that obeys $K$, and its restriction to $D_{A(v)}$ is a member of $\mathrm{Loc}(\rho, K, A(v), B)$ by definition. $\square$

### New auxiliary primes

**Definition (New auxiliary prime).** Let $(J, x)$ be tied primes with first prime $P_0$. A place $P$ with auxiliary data $l = (A, \gamma, \varphi, \chi)$ for $\ell^N$ is a *new auxiliary prime* for the datum $i$ if:

1. the norm of $P$ is a prime number;
2. $P \notin J$, $\bar P \notin J$, $P \notin S_0$ and $\bar P \notin S_0$;
3. $P$ does not divide the norm of $P_0$;
4. $l$ is auxiliary prime data at $P$ for every set $X$ of places inside $(J \setminus \{P_0\}) \cup \{w \mid \ell\}$;
5. $\chi(\varphi) = 1$;
6. for $v \in J$, $\chi_v(\varphi) = 1 \in \mathbb{Z}/\ell^N$ if $v = P_0$ and $0$ otherwise;
7. $U_i$ holds at $\iota_b(\varphi)$ for both $b$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsNewAuxiliaryPrime` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

Condition 4 (`anticyclotomic`) makes $\chi$ kill the decomposition groups at the old primes other than $P_0$ and above $\ell$.

**Lemma.** For tied primes and $v, w \in J$, $\chi_w(\gamma_v) = 1$ if $w = v$ and $\chi_w(\gamma_v) = 0$ otherwise.

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.character_tameGenerator_eq_ite` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* If $w = v$, this is condition 6 of auxiliary prime data. If $w \neq v$, then $v \neq \bar w$, since $J$ is auxiliary. The character $\chi_w$ comes from the norm-one torus for $\{w\}$, so it is $0$ on inertia above places outside $\{w, \bar w\}$, and $\gamma_v$ is in the inertia group at $A_v$. $\square$

**Lemma.** If $(J, x)$ are tied primes and $P$ with data $l$ is a new auxiliary prime for $i$, then for every $t \in O$ the set $J \cup \{P\}$, with $x$ changed at $P$ to $(l, t\,e_i)$, are tied primes with the same first prime. Here $t\,e_i$ is the slope $t$ for $i$ and $0$ for the other data.

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.insert_of_isNewAuxiliaryPrime` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* Check the six conditions of tied primes. The set $J \cup \{P\}$ is auxiliary by conditions 1 and 2 and $\bar P \neq P$. The data at $P$ are auxiliary prime data by condition 4 with $X$ the places above $\ell$. For the character values, there are four cases. At $(P, P)$ use condition 5. At $(P, w)$ with $w \in J$ use condition 6. At $(v, P)$ with $v \in J \setminus \{P_0\}$, $\chi(\varphi_v) = 0$ because $\chi$ kills the decomposition group at $v$ by condition 4. At $(v, w)$ the old condition holds. The first prime, its Frobenius element and its slope are unchanged, and condition 3 keeps $P$ away from the norm of $P_0$. At $P$ the slope is nonzero only for $i$, and there condition 7 gives $U_i$. $\square$

### Sah's lemma

**Lemma.** Let $X \subseteq (O/a)^n$ be a submodule that is stable under a set of matrices whose span is $M_n(O)$. If $X$ contains a vector $u$ with a unit coordinate $u_j$, then $X = (O/a)^n$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.eq_top_of_stable_of_isUnit` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* By linearity $X$ is stable under every matrix. Let $m \in (O/a)^n$ and $w = u_j^{-1}$. Lift $m_k w$ to $c_k \in O$ and let $C$ be the matrix whose only nonzero column is column $j$, with entries $c_k$. Then $C u = m$, so $m \in X$. $\square$

**Definition (Sah group).** $H = T.\mathrm{sahGroup}$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated by the commutators $[g, \gamma_*]$ and $[g, \gamma_*^c]$ for all $g \in G$, where $[g, h] = g h g^{-1} h^{-1}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.sahGroup` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

The group $H$ is normal, since $g'[g, \delta]g'^{-1} = [g'g, \delta][g', \delta]^{-1}$, and it is stable under $h \mapsto h^c$.

**Lemma.** $\rho(h) = 1$ for $h \in H$, and every homomorphism from $G$ to a commutative group is trivial on $H$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.ρ_eq_one_of_mem_sahGroup`, `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.map_eq_one_of_mem_sahGroup` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The elements $\gamma_*$ and $(\gamma_*^c)^{-1}$ act by the scalar $\zeta$, so $\rho(\gamma_*)$ and $\rho(\gamma_*^c)$ commute with every $\rho(g)$. So $\rho$ is trivial on the generators of $H$, hence on $H$. A homomorphism to a commutative group kills all commutators. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $R$ contain each place $v \in S$ prime to $\ell$ and its conjugate. Then $H$ lies in the normal subgroup generated by the inertia groups above $R$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.sahGroup_le_normalClosure` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The element $\gamma_*$ is in an inertia group above some $v \in S$ prime to $\ell$, and $(\gamma_*^c)^{-1}$ is in an inertia group above $\bar v$ (`New.Selmer.idOrConjInv_mem_inertiaAbove`). So $\delta = \gamma_*$ and $\delta = \gamma_*^c$ lie in the normal closure $N$, and $[g, \delta] = (g \delta g^{-1}) \delta^{-1} \in N$. $\square$

**Lemma.** The map $b \mapsto \rho(\gamma_*) b - b$ is bijective on $B^n$ for every $O$-module $B$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.bijective_γ_smul_sub` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* It is multiplication by the unit $\zeta - 1$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $z$ be a cocycle with values in $B^n$, and let $\rho(h)$ act trivially on $B^n$. Then $z(g h g^{-1}) = \rho(g) z(h)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.cocycle_conj_of_fixed` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The cocycle rule gives $z(g^{-1}) = -\rho(g)^{-1} z(g)$. So $z(g h g^{-1}) = z(g) + \rho(g) z(h) + \rho(g h) z(g^{-1}) = z(g) + \rho(g) z(h) - z(g)$, since $\rho(gh) = \rho(g)$ on $B^n$. $\square$

**Lemma (Sah's identity).** Let $z$ be a cocycle with $z(\gamma_*) = 0$. Then $z([g, \gamma_*]) = (1 - \zeta) z(g)$ for every $g$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.cocycle_commutator_γ` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* We have $[g, \gamma_*] \cdot \gamma_* g = g \gamma_*$. Apply $z$. Since $\rho([g, \gamma_*]) = 1$, the left side is $z([g, \gamma_*]) + z(\gamma_*) + \zeta z(g) = z([g, \gamma_*]) + \zeta z(g)$. The right side is $z(g) + \rho(g) z(\gamma_*) = z(g)$. $\square$

**Lemma (Sah's lemma).** A cocycle $z$ with $z(\gamma_*) = 0$ that vanishes on $H$ is $0$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.cocycle_eq_zero_of_sahGroup` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* By Sah's identity $(1 - \zeta) z(g) = z([g, \gamma_*]) = 0$, and $1 - \zeta$ is a unit. $\square$

The classical Sah's lemma says that if a central element of a group $\Gamma$ acts on $M$ by a scalar $\zeta$, then $\zeta - 1$ kills $H^1(\Gamma, M)$. Here $\gamma_*$ need not be central, and the identity above gives more: a class is detected on $H$.

**Lemma.** Let $O/a \neq 0$, and let $z$ be a cocycle with values in $W_a$ with $z(\gamma_*) = 0$. If some value $z(g)$ has a unit coordinate, then some $z(h)$ with $h \in H$ has a unit coordinate.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.exists_mem_sahGroup_isUnit_apply` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* The ring $O/a$ is local. Let $z_0$ be the reduction of $z$ to the residue field $k$. It is a cocycle with $z_0(\gamma_*) = 0$. If no value on $H$ had a unit coordinate, $z_0$ would vanish on $H$, so $z_0 = 0$ by Sah's lemma. But $z_0(g)_j \neq 0$ for the unit coordinate $z(g)_j$. $\square$

**Proposition.** Let $z$ be a cocycle with values in $W_a$, $z(\gamma_*) = 0$, and a value with a unit coordinate. Then the values $z(h)$, $h \in H$, span $W_a$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.SelmerDatum.span_image_sahGroup_eq_top` (New/Congruences/Selmer/SelmerData.lean).

*Proof.* Let $X$ be the span. For $g \in G$ and $h \in H$, $g h g^{-1} \in H$ and $\rho(h) = 1$, so $\rho(g) z(h) = z(g h g^{-1}) \in X$. So $X$ is stable under all $\rho(g)$, whose span is $M_n(O)$ by condition 4. By the previous lemma $X$ contains a vector with a unit coordinate. So $X = W_a$ by the first lemma of this part. $\square$

On $H$ the cocycle rule reads $z(h h') = z(h) + z(h')$, so $z|_H$ is a homomorphism.
