## Untied classes

This section bounds how many classes the ties $z(\gamma_w) = s_w z(\varphi_w)$ remove. On the line of twists $X_{P_0} = Y$, $X_v = s_v Y$, the ties at the primes other than $P_0$ cost at most $v(P^1(y))$ for the *tie polynomial* $P^1$. The ties at $P_0$, at $y = 0$, cost at most $\sum_b v(\det(\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1))$. All results are in [Untied.lean](lean:New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean#L1). Descent, in the next section, does not use them: they serve the file Line.lean of [the twisting theorem](/chapter/twisting-theorem/), a later chapter.

Background used: relative lengths, with `New.relLength_iInf_le` and `New.relLength_ker` ([discrete valuation rings and relative lengths](/chapter/fitting-ideals/#discrete-valuation-rings-and-relative-lengths)); the kernel of a square matrix $A$ on $(O/b)^n$ has length at most $v(\det A)$ (`New.length_ker_le_addVal_det`, [lengths and determinants](/chapter/fitting-ideals/#lengths-and-determinants)); tame inertia, $f(\varphi s \varphi^{-1}) = f(s)^k$ for $s$ inertial and $\varphi$ a Frobenius element of norm $k$, when $f$ is trivial near $1$ and $f^q = 1$ on inertia (`New.IsPlaceOver.tame_inertia`, [the Artin map and the second inequality](/chapter/class-field-theory/#the-artin-map-and-the-second-inequality)); the affine action $\mathrm{aff}_c$ of a cocycle is a homomorphism (`New.Selmer.affineAction`, file TameSelmerCocycles of [Selmer groups and the Greenberg-Wiles formula](/chapter/local-duality/#selmer-groups-and-the-greenberg-wiles-formula)).

### The relation from tame inertia

At a prime $v \nmid \ell$ the tame quotient is generated by a Frobenius element $\varphi$ and a tame generator $\gamma$ with $\varphi \gamma \varphi^{-1} = \gamma^{\mathrm{N}v}$. When $\mathrm{N}v \equiv 1$ modulo the order of the inertial action, $\varphi$ and $\gamma$ act by commuting affine maps, and a cocycle satisfies $(\varphi - 1) z(\gamma) = (\gamma - 1) z(\varphi)$. This needs no local Tate duality.

**Lemma.** $\mathrm{aff}_c(g)(u) = c(g) + \rho(g) u$. If $\rho(g) = \alpha \cdot 1$, then $\mathrm{aff}_c(g)^k(u) = \alpha^k u + (\sum_{i < k} \alpha^i) c(g)$. If also $\sum_{i < k} \alpha^i = 0$, then $\mathrm{aff}_c(g)^k = 1$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.affineAction_apply`, `New.Selmer.affineAction_pow_apply`, `New.Selmer.affineAction_pow_eq_one` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Induction on $k$. If the geometric sum is $0$, then $\alpha^k - 1 = (\alpha - 1)\sum_{i<k} \alpha^i = 0$, so the $k$-th power is the identity. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $c$ be a locally constant cocycle, $A$ a place over $v$, and let $\rho$ be trivial on inertia at $A$ near $1$. Let $q$ be prime to $A$ with $\mathrm{aff}_c(g)^q = 1$ for $g$ in inertia at $A$. If $\varphi$ is a Frobenius element at $A$ of norm $k \equiv 1 \bmod q$ and $\gamma$ is in inertia at $A$, then $\mathrm{aff}_c(\varphi)$ and $\mathrm{aff}_c(\gamma)$ commute.

Lean: `New.Selmer.commute_affineAction` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Near $1$ on inertia, $c$ equals $c(1) = 0$ and $\rho = 1$, so $\mathrm{aff}_c = 1$ there. Tame inertia applied to $f = \mathrm{aff}_c$ gives $\mathrm{aff}_c(\varphi \gamma \varphi^{-1}) = \mathrm{aff}_c(\gamma)^k = \mathrm{aff}_c(\gamma)$, as $k \equiv 1 \bmod q$. $\square$

**Lemma.** If $\mathrm{aff}_c(\varphi)$ and $\mathrm{aff}_c(\gamma)$ commute, then $(\rho(\varphi) - 1) c(\gamma) = (\rho(\gamma) - 1) c(\varphi)$. If $\rho(\gamma)$ is a scalar, the same relation holds with $\varphi^{-1}$ in place of $\varphi$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.sub_one_smul_eq_of_commute`, `New.Selmer.inv_sub_one_smul_eq` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Apply both composites to $0$: $c(\varphi) + \rho(\varphi) c(\gamma) = c(\gamma) + \rho(\gamma) c(\varphi)$. For $\varphi^{-1}$, multiply the relation by $-\rho(\varphi^{-1})$, use $c(\varphi^{-1}) = -\rho(\varphi^{-1}) c(\varphi)$, and use that $\rho(\varphi^{-1})$ commutes with the scalar $\rho(\gamma) - 1$. $\square$

**Lemma (The tame relation).** Let $q$ be prime to $A$, $\rho$ trivial on inertia at $A$ near $1$, and let each inertial element act by a scalar $\alpha$ with $\sum_{i<q} \alpha^i = 0$. If $\varphi$ or $\varphi^{-1}$ is a Frobenius element of norm $k \equiv 1 \bmod q$ and $\gamma$ is inertial at $A$, then every locally constant cocycle satisfies $(\rho(\varphi) - 1) c(\gamma) = (\rho(\gamma) - 1) c(\varphi)$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.sub_one_smul_eq_of_tame` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* By the first lemma $\mathrm{aff}_c(g)^q = 1$ on inertia. Apply the last two lemmas. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $v \nmid \ell$ carry auxiliary prime data, let $v, \bar v \in S$, let $\rho$ be trivial on inertia above $S$ near $1$, and let inertia above $S$ act by scalars $\alpha$ with $\sum_{j < \ell^N} \alpha^j = 0$. Then for both $b$
$$(\rho(\iota_b(\varphi)) - 1)\, c(\iota_b(\gamma)) = (\rho(\iota_b(\gamma)) - 1)\, c(\iota_b(\varphi)).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.sub_one_smul_eq_of_isAuxPrimeDataAt` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* The norm of $v$ is $1$ modulo $\ell^N$ and $\ell^N$ is prime to the place. For $b$ true, apply the tame relation at the place of the data. For $b$ false, $(\varphi^c)^{-1}$ is the inverse of a Frobenius element at the conjugate place and $(\gamma^c)^{-1}$ is inertial there. $\square$

### The twist modulo $\ell^M$

**Lemma.** If $\rho(g) \equiv \rho(g')$ modulo $\ell^M$, then $e(\rho(g))$ and $e(\rho(g'))$ act in the same way on $(O'/\ell^M)^n$. If $y$ divides all $x_j$, then $y \mid \psi_x(g) - 1$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.map_smul_eq_of_reduceModPow_eq`, `New.Selmer.dvd_eval_universalChar_sub_one` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* The first claim holds because $O/\ell^M \to O'/\ell^M$ is a ring map. For the second, each factor $(1 + x_j)^k$ is $1$ modulo $y$. $\square$

**Lemma.** If each $1 + x_j$ is a $q$-th root of unity modulo $\ell^M$, the twisted matrices $\psi_x(g) e(\rho(g))$ act on $(O'/\ell^M)^n$ through a representation $R$, and $R(g) = \psi_x(g) \cdot 1$ wherever $\rho(g) \equiv 1 \bmod \ell^M$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_monoidHom_smul_eq_pointTwist_smul` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Modulo $\ell^M$, $g \mapsto \prod_j (1 + x_j)^{h_j(g)}$ is a character $\chi$, since the exponents only matter modulo $q$. Take $R = \chi \cdot (\rho \bmod \ell^M)$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $(J, d)$ be tied primes, $\rho \bmod \ell^M$ trivial on inertia outside $S_0$, $x$ a point over $O'$ whose coordinates are divisible by $y$, and assume (G): $\ell^M \mid \sum_{j < \ell^N} \alpha^j$ whenever $y \mid \alpha - 1$. For $z \in \mathrm{Sel}(x)$ and $v \in J$, both $b$, with $T_x(g) = \psi_x(g) e(\rho(g))$,
$$(T_x(\iota_b(\varphi_v)) - 1)\, z(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) = (T_x(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) - 1)\, z(\iota_b(\varphi_v)).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.frob_sub_one_smul_eq` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Each coordinate $x_j$ is $\equiv 0 \bmod y$, so $(1 + x_j)^{\ell^N} \equiv 1 \bmod \ell^M$ by (G) with $\alpha = 1 + x_j$, using $\alpha^{\ell^N} - 1 = (\alpha - 1)\sum_j \alpha^j$. So the last lemma gives a representation $R$. Inertia outside $S_0$ acts by the scalar $\psi_x(g)$, which is $\equiv 1 \bmod y$, so its geometric sum vanishes by (G). Near $1$ the characters are trivial, so $R$ is trivial on inertia near $1$. Apply the previous part to $R$ at $v$ and $\bar v$, which lie outside $S_0$. $\square$

### The line and the tie polynomial

**Definition.** Fix an index $i$. The *line* is the set of points $x_{P_0} = y$, $x_v = e(s_v(i))\, y$ for $v \in J \setminus \{P_0\}$. $\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y) = \mathrm{Sel}(x)$ at that point, with the characters $\chi_v$ of the data.

Lean: `New.Selmer.lineDirection`, `New.Selmer.selmerOnLine` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

**Lemma.** At $y = 0$ the point is $0$, the twist is trivial, and $\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(0) = \mathrm{Sel}(\rho, S, L, O/\ell^M)$ for $e = \mathrm{id}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.selmerAtPoint_zero`, `New.Selmer.selmerOnLine_zero` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* At $x = 0$, $\psi_0(g) = 1$ and $\mathrm{mapRel}_{\mathrm{id}}$ is the identity. $\square$

**Definition (Tie polynomial).** For a matrix $A$, $D_A(Y) = \det((1 + Y) A - 1) \in O[Y]$. For $I \subseteq J$,
$$P^1_I(Y) = \prod_{v \in I} \prod_{b} D_{\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v))}(Y).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.shiftedDet`, `New.Selmer.tiePolynomial` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

**Lemma.** $D_A$ evaluated at $y$ through $e$ is $\det((1 + y) e(A) - 1)$, and the coefficient of $Y^n$ in $D_A$ is $\det A$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.eval₂_shiftedDet`, `New.Selmer.coeff_shiftedDet_card` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Evaluation commutes with determinants. Write $(1 + Y)A - 1 = Y A + (A - 1)$. The coefficient of $Y^n$ in its determinant is $\det A$. $\square$

So $P^1_I$ is a nonzero polynomial whenever all $\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v))$ are invertible, which they are, since $\rho$ is a representation.

**Lemma (Ties on the line).** Assume (G), that $\rho \bmod \ell^M$ is trivial on inertia outside $S_0$, and that it is trivial at every $g$ with $V_i(g)$. Let $z \in \mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y)$, $v \in J$, and $y = 0$ if $v = P_0$. Then
$$\big((1 + y)\, e(\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v))) - 1\big)\big(z(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) - e(s_v)\, z(\iota_b(\varphi_v))\big) = 0.$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.frob_sub_one_smul_tie_eq_zero_of_selmerOnLine` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* By the values of $\psi_X$ at tied primes, $\psi_x(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) = 1 + e(s_v) y$ and $\psi_x(\iota_b(\varphi_v)) = 1 + y$ for $v \neq P_0$. At $v = P_0$, $y = 0$ and both are $1$. The element $\iota_b(\gamma_v)$ is inertial outside $S_0$, so it acts by the scalar $1 + e(s_v) y$. Put $\Phi = (1 + y) e(\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v)))$. The last lemma gives $(\Phi - 1) z(\gamma) = e(s_v) y\, z(\varphi)$. If $s_v \neq 0$, condition 6 of tied primes gives $V_i$ at $\iota_b(\varphi_v)$, so $\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v)) \equiv 1$ and $(\Phi - 1) z(\varphi) = y\, z(\varphi)$. If $s_v = 0$ both sides below are $0$. In each case $e(s_v)(\Phi - 1) z(\varphi) = e(s_v) y\, z(\varphi) = (\Phi - 1) z(\gamma)$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Under the same hypotheses, let $I \subseteq J$, with $y = 0$ if $P_0 \in I$. Then
$$\mathrm{relLength}\big(\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y; L),\ \mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y; L \cup \mathrm{tie}(I))\big) \leq \sum_{v \in I} \sum_b \mathrm{len}\, \ker\big((1 + y) e(\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_v))) - 1\big).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.relLength_tied_le_sum_ker` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Let $\mathrm{tie}_{v, b}(z) = z(\iota_b(\gamma_v)) - e(s_v) z(\iota_b(\varphi_v))$, a linear map. A member of $\mathrm{Sel}(L)$ in all the kernels obeys the ties, so it lies in $\mathrm{Sel}(L \cup \mathrm{tie}(I))$. So the relative length is at most that of $\mathrm{Sel}(L)$ modulo $\bigcap \ker \mathrm{tie}_{v,b}$, which is at most $\sum_{v,b} \mathrm{relLength}(\mathrm{Sel}(L), \ker \mathrm{tie}_{v,b}) = \sum_{v,b} \mathrm{len}\, \mathrm{tie}_{v,b}(\mathrm{Sel}(L))$. By the last lemma the image lies in the kernel of the matrix. $\square$

**Theorem (Cost of the ties).** Under the same hypotheses,
$$\mathrm{relLength}\big(\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y; L),\ \mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{line}}(y; L \cup \mathrm{tie}(J \setminus \{P_0\}))\big) \leq v\big(P^1_{J \setminus \{P_0\}}(y)\big),$$
with the coefficients of $P^1$ mapped by $e$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.relLength_tied_le` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Take $I = J \setminus \{P_0\}$ in the last lemma, so $P_0 \notin I$ and $y$ is arbitrary. Bound each kernel by the valuation of the determinant, and sum: the valuation of a product is the sum of the valuations. $\square$

**Theorem (Release of the first prime).** Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring, let $\rho \bmod \ell^M$ be trivial on inertia outside $S_0$ and at the elements with $V_i$, let $M \leq N$, and let $\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_{P_0})) \equiv \rho(\iota_b(g_0))$ modulo $\ell^M$ for both $b$. The first prime has slope $0$, so its ties say $z(\gamma_{P_0}) = 0 = z(\iota_{\mathrm{false}}(\gamma_{P_0}))$. For the Selmer modules with values in $(O/\ell^M)^n$,
$$\mathrm{relLength}\big(\mathrm{Sel}(\rho, S, L),\ \mathrm{Sel}(\rho, S, L \cup \mathrm{tie}(\{P_0\}))\big) \leq \sum_b v\big(\det(\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1)\big).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsTiedPrimes.relLength_tieRelations_le_sum_addVal_det` (New/Congruences/Selmer/Untied.lean).

*Proof.* Take $y = 0$, $e = \mathrm{id}$ and $I = \{P_0\}$. Hypothesis (G) holds: $0 \mid \alpha - 1$ forces $\alpha = 1$, and then $\sum_{j < \ell^N} 1 = \ell^N$ is divisible by $\ell^M$ as $M \leq N$. At $y = 0$ the line gives $\mathrm{Sel}(\rho, S, L)$. The previous lemma bounds the relative length by the lengths of the kernels of $\rho(\iota_b(\varphi_{P_0})) - 1$ on $(O/\ell^M)^n$. These kernels lie in those of $\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1$, since the two matrices act in the same way modulo $\ell^M$. Bound each by the valuation of its determinant. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The valuation of $0$ is $\infty$, so if $\det(\rho(\iota_b(g_0)) - 1) = 0$ the theorem says nothing. Hypothesis (G) links $y$ to the level $\ell^N$.
