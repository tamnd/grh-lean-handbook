## The main lemma

This section joins the block, the characters and Ribet's method into one theorem. It holds for any group and any discrete valuation ring $O$. Let $\mathbf{1} = \prod_{j \in J} X_j$, and write $v$ for the valuation of $O$.

**Definition (Common Ribet element).** Let $\beta_i : Y \to L$ be functions and $\pi \in \mathcal{X} \subseteq Y$. The value $c \in L$ is a common Ribet coefficient at $\pi$ if every $x \mapsto \beta_i(x)$ lies in $S$ and vanishes on $\mathcal{X}$, and some $f \in S$ has: $f\beta_i \in \mathcal{J}$ for all $i$; for $x \in \mathcal{X}$, $f(x)$ has no monomial that properly divides $\mathbf{1}$; and the coefficient of $\mathbf{1}$ in $f(\pi)$ is $c$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.IsCommonRibetElement` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

**Theorem (Ribet's lemma to first order).** Let $Y$ be finite and $J$ finite and linearly ordered. Suppose:

1. on $\mathcal{X}$, $u_x$ is the block sum of $\rho_x$ of degree $r$ and characters $\xi_0 = 1, \xi_1, \ldots, \xi_{t-1}$, and the $\rho_\pi(g)$ span $M_r(O)$;
2. $\rho_x(\gamma_x) = y_x \cdot 1$ with $y_x - 1$ a unit, and $\xi_e(\gamma_x) = 1$;
3. $p \neq 0$, $\mathcal{J}$ is a trace ideal, and $p \mid \varepsilon_j(\xi_e(\varphi_k)) - 1$;
4. $\prod_{k \neq e}(\xi_e(a) - \xi_k(a))$ divides $C$ for each $e$, and $\mathrm{ty} : J \to \{0, \ldots, t - 1\}$ is monotone;
5. $\operatorname{len} \mathrm{Coc}_{\{\tau_k\}}(\rho_\pi, K/O) \leq b_0$, with vanishing at $\gamma_\pi$;
6. $\xi_e(\delta_w) = 1$ and $o_w(X)(X - 1)$ kills every $u_x(\delta_w)$;
7. every module of character cocycles for $\xi_e$ has triangle cost $b_e$ along $\{\mathrm{ty}(j) = e\}$ and is detected by $s$.

Then some $c \in O$ is a common Ribet coefficient at $\pi$ for the family $\beta_\omega(x) = \det \sum_g \omega_g(u_x(g) - 1)$, $\omega$ finitely supported in $O$, with
$$v(c) \leq b_0 + \sum_e b_e + t\Big(\sum_w v(o_w(1)) + 2\#s\,v(C)\Big) + (\#J + 1)v(C).$$

The proof uses the following lemmas. The first group is bookkeeping.

**Lemma.** Under the hypotheses of the theorem:

1. the block form of $u_x$ extends to $R[G]$, with $\tilde\rho_x$ and the $\tilde\xi_e$;
2. if $z$ are coordinates, then $\tilde u_x(\langle \underline{\sum_g \omega_g z(g)} \rangle)$ has determinant $\beta_\omega(x)$;
3. the constant map $R \to (Y \to L[X_j])$ factors through $S$, and the characteristic polynomials of $\tilde\rho(g)$ have the constant coefficients of those of $\rho(g)$;
4. each $\beta_\omega$ lies in $S$;
5. each $\beta_\omega$ vanishes on $\mathcal{X}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.polyAction_eq_reindex`, `New.Selmer.det_polyAction_rowValue`, `New.Selmer.exists_ringHom_comp_eq_algebraMap`, `New.Selmer.coeff_charpoly_map_polyAction_single`, `New.Selmer.C_det_linearCombination_mem`, `New.Selmer.det_linearCombination_sub_one_eq_zero` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/RibetLemma.lean).

*Proof.* Statements 1 to 3 follow from the definitions and the lemma on actions. Statement 2 uses the lemma on rows: the value acts as $\sum_g \omega_g(u_x(g) - 1)$. For 4, the determinant of a combination of the $u_x(g)$ lies in the algebra generated by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomials, by [Amitsur and Cayley-Hamilton](/chapter/ribet-elements/#amitsur-and-cayley-hamilton). For 5, the column of $\sum_g \omega_g(u_x(g) - 1)$ at $\xi_0 = 1$ is zero. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $\mathcal{J}$ be a trace ideal and let $M$ be a matrix of tied rows. Then each entry of $M\,(\mathrm{gen} - 1)$ is $b + m\rho_0$, with $b \in \mathfrak{a}_0$ and $m$ in the left ideal of the $\mathrm{gen}_i - 1$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_mem_span_add_mul_eq_add_mul`, `New.Selmer.exists_mulVec_eq_add_mul_of_mem_tiedRows` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean, New/Congruences/FirstOrder/RibetLemma.lean).

*Proof.* By the proposition on the trace ideal, the entry is $b + m\rho_0$ with $m \in I$. Under every $\tilde u_x$, an element $l$ is some $l'$ in the left ideal of the $\mathrm{gen}_i - 1$ plus the scalar $\tilde 1(l)$. As $\tilde 1(m) = 0$, the difference $(m - m')\rho_0$ is killed by every $\tilde u_x$, so it lies in $\mathfrak{a}_0$. $\square$

**Proposition (Gluing into one element).** Let $k$ be $1$ on the characters and $0$ on the blocks, and $q_0$ be $\mu_0$ on $\xi_0$ and $0$ on the other characters. Let $B_0$ and $M_i$ be matrices of tied rows, and $\mathrm{wt}_i \in R$ weights. Then one $f \in S$ has $f\beta_\omega \in \mathcal{J}$ for all $\omega$, and for $x \in \mathcal{X}$,
$$f(x) = \sum_i \mathrm{wt}_i\,\mu_0 \det \tilde\rho_x(B_0) \prod_e \det \tilde\xi_e(M_i).$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_traceIdeal_multiplier_eq_sum_det` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/RibetLemma.lean).

*Proof.* The matrix $(1 - k)B_0 + kM_i$ has tied rows. On $\tilde\rho_x$ it is $B_0$, and on $\tilde\xi_e$ it is $M_i$. Apply Ribet's element for tied rows with $\rho_1 = kq_0$, using the last lemma for the hypothesis on rows. Sum the elements with the weights $\mathrm{wt}_i$. $\square$

**Lemma (Projectors).** If each $\tilde\rho_x$ kills an element on which all characters are $1$, then one such element is killed by all $\tilde\rho_x$. Such an element exists: $C'(y_x - \gamma_x)$ with $C'(y_x - 1) = 1$. If $c$ is a multiple of $\prod_{k \neq e}(\eta_e(a) - \eta_k(a))$, some element is $c$ on $\eta_e$ and $0$ on the other characters.

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_forall_eq_one_and_forall_eq_zero`, `New.Selmer.exists_apply_eq_ite_of_prod_dvd` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean), `New.Selmer.exists_character_projector` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/RibetLemma.lean).

*Proof.* For the first claim, if $k_x$ works for $x$, the product $\prod_x k_x$ works for all $x$. For the second, $\gamma_x$ acts as $y_x$ on $\rho_x$ and as $1$ on the characters. For the third, take $c'\prod_{k \neq e}(a - \eta_k(a))$ with $c = c'\prod_{k \neq e}(\eta_e(a) - \eta_k(a))$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Under the hypotheses of the theorem:

1. there are a finite $s_0 \subseteq G$ and coordinates $z$ for the Ribet generators on $W \sqcup s_0 \sqcup s$;
2. for character rows, $\sum_\mathbf{i} \prod_e w_{e, i_e} \prod_e \det \tilde\xi_e(M_\mathbf{i}) = c_0^t \prod_e d_e$, and $v\big((\prod_e \Gamma_e)(0)\big) \leq \sum_e b_e + (\#J + t\#s)v(C)$;
3. a cocycle in $\mathrm{Coc}(\rho, B)$ obeys every linear relation among the $u_x(g) - 1$, membership is preserved by injective maps of coefficients, and every member of $\mathrm{Coc}(\rho, K/O)$ is torsion;
4. if the cocycles modulo every $p^M$ have length at most $b$, so do those with values in $K/O$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_areCoordinates_ribetGenerators`, `New.Selmer.CharacterRows.sum_prod_coeff_mul_prod_det_tiedMatrix`, `New.Selmer.CharacterRows.addVal_constantCoeff_prod_le`, `New.Selmer.mem_ribetCocyclesIn_iff`, `New.Selmer.comp_mem_ribetCocyclesIn_iff`, `New.Selmer.exists_smul_eq_zero_of_mem_ribetCocyclesIn`, `New.Selmer.length_ribetCocyclesVanishingOn_divisibleQuot_le` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/RibetLemma.lean).

*Proof.* For 1, the families $(u_x(g) - 1)_x$ lie in a finitely generated $O$-module, as $Y$ is finite and $O$ is noetherian. Choose $s_0$ so that the generators span it. For 2, expand the product of the sums $\sum_i w_{e,i}\det B_{e,i}$ and use $\det \tilde\xi_e(M_\mathbf{i}) = c_0 \det B_{e,i_e}$. The sets $\{\mathrm{ty}(j) = e\}$ partition $J$, so the bounds add. Statement 3 follows from 1, as each value is a combination of finitely many values. For 4, put $a = p^{b+1}$. Division by $a$ maps the cocycles modulo $a$ onto the $a$-torsion, and since $v(a) > b$ this torsion is everything. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* Take the projector $k$ and the elements $q_e$ with $\tilde\xi_{e'}(q_e) = C$ for $e' = e$ and $0$ otherwise. Take $s_0$ and $z$ from the last lemma, and $c_1$ with $c_1(y_\pi - 1) = 1$. The corollary on the block gives $B_0$ with $v(\det \tilde\rho_{\pi,0}(B_0)) \leq b_0$. The theorem on character rows gives $w_{e,i}$, $B_{e,i}$, $M_\mathbf{i}$, $\Gamma_e$ and $\zeta_e$. Glue with $\mu_0 = C$ and the weights $\prod_e w_{e, i_e}$. By the last lemma, on $\mathcal{X}$,
$$f(x) = F_x \prod_e d_e, \qquad F_x = \det \tilde\rho_x(B_0) \cdot C c_0^t.$$
The lemma on coefficients of [Fitting ideals and the triangle cost](/chapter/ribet-elements/#fitting-ideals-and-the-triangle-cost) shows that $f(x)$ has no monomial that properly divides $\mathbf{1}$, and that the coefficient of $\mathbf{1}$ in $f(\pi)$ is $c = (F_\pi \prod_e \Gamma_e)(0)$. Add the valuations:
$$v(c) \leq b_0 + v(C) + t\big(\textstyle\sum_w v(o_w(1)) + \#s\,v(C)\big) + \sum_e b_e + (\#J + t\#s)v(C).$$
This is the bound of the theorem. $\square$

**Example (One character).** Let $t = 1$, so the only character is $\xi_0 = 1$ and $C = 1$ works. Let $W$ and $s$ be empty. The bound is $v(c) \leq b_0 + b_0'$, where $b_0'$ is the triangle cost for the trivial character. The block term and the cost of the single character are the only terms.

**Remark (Formalization).** The coefficient $c$ is in $O$, but the Ribet element lies in $S$, whose values are polynomials over $L$. The theorem maps $c$ to $L$. The proof chooses $k$, $q_e$, $s_0$, $z$, $B_0$ and the character rows by choice.
