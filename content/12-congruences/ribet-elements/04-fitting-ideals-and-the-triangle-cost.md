## Fitting ideals and the triangle cost

The Ribet element of the last section has the value $\mu_0 \det(\psi_x B) \prod_e \det(\eta_e B)$ on $\mathcal{X}$. To make this value small, the chapter needs square matrices of relations whose determinants have small valuation. For the characters, the determinants are polynomials in the tie variables $X_j$, and only one coefficient counts: the coefficient of $\prod_j X_j$. This section proves the bound for that coefficient. It is a statement about Fitting ideals over $R = O[X_j : j \in J]$.

### Background

The section uses these results of [Fitting ideals](/chapter/fitting-ideals/) in Volume 7. $O$ is a discrete valuation ring with valuation $v$.

- For $N \subseteq R^\iota$, the Fitting ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ is the ideal generated by the determinants of the $\iota \times \iota$ matrices with all rows in $N$ ([`New.Selmer.fittingIdeal`](/decl/New.Selmer.fittingIdeal/)).
- For an ideal $I$ of $R$, $\operatorname{Fitt}(N + I R^\iota) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N) + I$ ([`New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.sup_smul_top_le`](/decl/New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.sup_smul_top_le/)).
- Adding vectors $v_j$ along a linear order, when $r_j v_j$ lies in the module built so far, costs the factor $\prod_j r_j$ ([`New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_ge_mul_prod_mem`](/decl/New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_ge_mul_prod_mem/)).
- For $\Phi : O^n \to R^\iota$, $z : G \to O^n$ and $p \in O$, the module $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$ of *forms modulo $p$* is the set of functions $g \mapsto \Psi(z(g))$ with values in $O/p$, where $\Psi : O^n \to O/p$ is $O$-linear and vanishes on $\Phi^{-1}(N)$ ([`New.Selmer.formsMod`](/decl/New.Selmer.formsMod/)). If every such $\Psi$ kills $w$ and $\Phi(pO^n) \subseteq N$, then $\Phi(w) \in N$ ([`New.Selmer.mem_of_forall_form_eq_zero`](/decl/New.Selmer.mem_of_forall_form_eq_zero/)).
- For any $O$-submodule $V$ of functions $G \to O/p$, there are $c \in O$ and $K \supseteq N$ with $v(c) \leq \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(N, p) \bmod V)$, with $\Phi(w) \in K$ for every $w$ killed by all $\Psi$ with $\Psi \circ z \in V$, and with $c \operatorname{Fitt}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ ([`New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_addVal_le_relLength`](/decl/New.Selmer.fittingIdeal.exists_addVal_le_relLength/)).

Here $\operatorname{len}(S \bmod T)$, the relative length, is the length of $S/(S \cap T)$ ([`New.relLength`](/decl/New.relLength/)).

### The triangle cost

Each index $j \in J$ carries two pairs of group elements, $\tau_j, \tau'_j$ and $\varphi_j, \varphi'_j$. In the application, $\tau_j$ is a tame generator at a new place and $\tau'_j$ is the inverse of its conjugate, so that $c(\tau_j) - c(\tau'_j)$ is the value at $\tau_j$ of an even class.

**Definition (Triangle cost).** Let $Z$ be an $O$-module of functions $G \to B$, let $I \subseteq J$ be finite, $C \in O$ and $b \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. Then $Z$ has triangle cost $b$ along $I$ if there is a set $A \subseteq G$ with these two properties, where $Z_0$ is the set of $c \in Z$ that vanish on $A$:

1. (the triangle) for $j \in I$ and $c \in Z_0$, if $c(\tau_i) = c(\tau'_i)$ for all $i < j$, and $c(\varphi_i) = c(\varphi'_i)$ for all $i < j$ in $I$, then $C c(\tau_j) = C c(\tau'_j)$;
2. (the cost) for every submodule $Z_1 \subseteq Z_0$ whose members vanish at all $\tau_j$ and $\tau'_j$ and have $c(\varphi_j) = c(\varphi'_j)$ for $j \in I$, $\operatorname{len}(Z \bmod Z_0) + \operatorname{len}(Z_1 \bmod Z_1[C]) \leq b$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.HasTriangleCost` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

Here $Z_1[C]$ is the $C$-torsion of $Z_1$. The triangle says that, after the earlier ties hold, the next tie holds up to $C$. In [Tied traces and even classes](/chapter/tied-traces/), a triangle of primes with prescribed Frobenius elements gives this property.

### Three stages

Fix $\Phi : O^n \to R^s$, with $s$ a finite subset of $G$, and $z : G \to O^n$. Write $y_g = \Phi(z(g))$. Let $p \neq 0$ with $\Phi(pO^n) \subseteq N$.

**Lemma (The third stage: reading at $X = 0$).** Let $T \subseteq R^s$ with $\Phi(pO^n) \subseteq T$ and $y_g \equiv e_g$ modulo $T$ for $g \in s$, where $e_g$ is the unit vector. Let $T_0 = T + \mathfrak{m}_0 R^s$, where $\mathfrak{m}_0$ is the ideal of polynomials without constant term. Then some $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(T)$ has
$$v(d(0)) \leq \operatorname{len}\big(\mathcal{F}(T_0, p) \bmod \mathcal{F}(T_0, p)[C]\big) + \#s\, v(C) .$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_mem_fittingIdeal_addVal_constantCoeff_le` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* Apply the background theorem to $T_0$ with $V$ the $C$-torsion functions. It gives $f$ with the bound on $v(f)$ and $K \supseteq T_0$ with $f \operatorname{Fitt}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(T_0)$. The row $g$ of $C \cdot 1$ is $\Phi(C z(g)) - C(y_g - e_g)$. The second term lies in $T \subseteq K$. The first lies in $K$, since every $\Psi$ with $\Psi \circ z$ killed by $C$ has $\Psi(C z(g)) = 0$. So $C^{\#s} = \det(C \cdot 1) \in \operatorname{Fitt}(K)$, and $f C^{\#s} \in \operatorname{Fitt}(T_0) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(T) + \mathfrak{m}_0$. Write $f C^{\#s} = d + r$ with $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(T)$ and $r(0) = 0$. Then $d(0) = f C^{\#s}$. $\square$

**Lemma (One step of the triangle).** Let $T \supseteq \Phi(pO^n)$. Suppose that for all $i$, $\Phi(z(\tau_i) - z(\tau'_i)) \equiv X_i\, \Phi(z(\varphi_i) - z(\varphi'_i))$ modulo $T$, and that $X_i\, \Phi(z(\varphi_i) - z(\varphi'_i)) \in T$ for $i < j$. Suppose that every $\Psi$ that vanishes on $\Phi^{-1}(T)$ and has $\Psi(z(\tau_i)) = \Psi(z(\tau'_i))$ for $i < j$ also has $C\Psi(z(\tau_j)) = C\Psi(z(\tau'_j))$. Then $C X_j\, \Phi(z(\varphi_j) - z(\varphi'_j)) \in T$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.smul_X_smul_mem_of_forall_form` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* Let $\Psi$ vanish on $\Phi^{-1}(T)$. For $i < j$, the vector $\Phi(z(\tau_i) - z(\tau'_i))$ lies in $T$ by the two hypotheses, so $\Psi(z(\tau_i)) = \Psi(z(\tau'_i))$. By the last hypothesis, $\Psi$ kills $C(z(\tau_j) - z(\tau'_j))$. By duality, $\Phi(C(z(\tau_j) - z(\tau'_j))) \in T$. Subtract $C$ times the congruence for $j$. $\square$

**Theorem (Fitting ideal along a triangle).** Suppose that $y_g \equiv e_g$ modulo $N$ for $g \in s$, and that $y_{\tau_j} \equiv X_j y_{\varphi_j}$ and $y_{\tau'_j} \equiv X_j y_{\varphi'_j}$ modulo $N$ for all $j$. Suppose that each $i$ below an element of $I$ lies in $I$ or has $X_i R^s \subseteq N$. Suppose that $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$ has triangle cost $b$ along $I$. Then there is $F \in R$ with
$$v(F(0)) \leq b + (\#I + \#s)\, v(C) \quad \text{and} \quad F \prod_{j \in I} X_j \in \operatorname{Fitt}(N) .$$

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_mul_prod_X_mem_fittingIdeal` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* Take $A$ from the triangle cost, and enlarge $N$ in three stages.

*Stage 1.* Apply the background theorem with $V$ the functions that vanish on $A$. It gives $e$ with $v(e) \leq \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(N, p) \bmod \text{vanishing on } A)$ and $K \supseteq N$ with $e \operatorname{Fitt}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$. For every $K' \supseteq K$, a form in $\mathcal{F}(K', p)$ lies in $\mathcal{F}(N, p)$ and vanishes on $A$, by duality.

*Stage 2.* Put $v_j = \Phi(z(\varphi_j) - z(\varphi'_j))$. Then $\Phi(z(\tau_i) - z(\tau'_i)) \equiv X_i v_i$ modulo $K$. Let $j \in I$ and let $T \supseteq K$ contain $v_i$ for $i < j$ in $I$. Then $X_i v_i \in T$ for all $i < j$: for $i \in I$ since $v_i \in T$, and otherwise since $X_i R^s \subseteq N$. A form $\Psi$ on $T$ with $\Psi(z(\tau_i)) = \Psi(z(\tau'_i))$ for $i < j$ gives $\Psi \circ z$ in $Z_0$ that also satisfies $\Psi(z(\varphi_i)) = \Psi(z(\varphi'_i))$ for $i < j$ in $I$. The triangle gives $C\Psi(z(\tau_j)) = C\Psi(z(\tau'_j))$. So the last lemma gives $C X_j v_j \in T$. The background corollary on added vectors, with $r_j = C X_j$, gives $T \supseteq K$ that contains all $v_j$, $j \in I$, with $\operatorname{Fitt}(T) \prod_{j \in I} C X_j \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(K)$.

*Stage 3.* The lemma on reading at $X = 0$ gives $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(T)$ with $v(d(0)) \leq \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(T_0, p) \bmod C\text{-torsion}) + \#s\,v(C)$. The forms in $\mathcal{F}(T_0, p)$ vanish at all $\tau_j$, since $y_{\tau_j} \equiv X_j y_{\varphi_j}$ modulo $N$ and $X_j R^s \subseteq T_0$. They vanish at all $\tau'_j$ for the same reason. They have $c(\varphi_j) = c(\varphi'_j)$ for $j \in I$, since $v_j \in T \subseteq T_0$. And they lie in $Z_0$ by Stage 1. So the cost gives $v(e) + \operatorname{len}(\mathcal{F}(T_0, p) \bmod C\text{-torsion}) \leq b$.

Put $F = e\, C^{\#I} d$. Then $F \prod_{j \in I} X_j = e\, (d \prod_{j \in I} C X_j) \in e \operatorname{Fitt}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}(N)$. And $v(F(0)) = v(e) + \#I\, v(C) + v(d(0)) \leq b + (\#I + \#s)\, v(C)$. $\square$

### Reading the coefficient of the product

**Definition (Non-divisor ideal).** Let $\mathfrak{n} \subseteq R$ be the set of polynomials none of whose monomials divides $\prod_{j \in J} X_j$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.nonDivisorIdeal` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

It is an ideal, since a multiple of a monomial that does not divide $\prod_j X_j$ does not divide it either.

**Lemma.** A polynomial lies in $\mathfrak{n}$ if and only if no monomial of its support divides $\prod_j X_j$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.mem_nonDivisorIdeal` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* This is the definition. $\square$

Let each variable $X_j$ have a type $\mathrm{ty}(j) \in \{0, \dots, t-1\}$, with $\mathrm{ty}$ monotone. For a type $e$, write $X_{<e} = \prod_{\mathrm{ty}(j) < e} X_j$ and $X_{=e} = \prod_{\mathrm{ty}(j) = e} X_j$.

**Lemma.** If $\mathrm{ty}(i) < e$, then $X_i X_{<e} \in \mathfrak{n}$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.X_mem_colon_nonDivisorIdeal` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* The product contains $X_i^2$. $\square$

**Lemma (The coefficient of the product).** For each type $e$, let $d_e + \zeta_e = \Gamma_e X_{=e}$ with $X_{<e} \zeta_e \in \mathfrak{n}$. Then for every $F \in R$ and every monomial $X^i$ that divides $\prod_j X_j$, the coefficient of $X^i$ in $F \prod_e d_e$ is $0$ if $X^i \neq \prod_j X_j$, and it is $(F \prod_e \Gamma_e)(0)$ if $X^i = \prod_j X_j$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.coeff_mul_prod_of_mem_colon` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* Expand the product in the order of the types:
$$\prod_e d_e = \prod_e (d_e + \zeta_e) - \sum_e \zeta_e \prod_{k < e} (d_k + \zeta_k) \prod_{k > e} d_k .$$
For each $e$, $\prod_{k < e}(d_k + \zeta_k) = \prod_{k<e} \Gamma_k X_{=k}$ is a multiple of $X_{<e}$. So the error term lies in $\mathfrak{n}$, and so does $F$ times it. Its coefficients at divisors of $\prod_j X_j$ vanish. The main term is $F \prod_e \Gamma_e \cdot \prod_j X_j$. Its coefficient at a divisor $X^i$ of $\prod_j X_j$ is the constant term of $F \prod_e \Gamma_e$ if $X^i = \prod_j X_j$, and $0$ otherwise. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $f : M \to R^\iota$ be linear and $N \subseteq M$. Every $d \in \operatorname{Fitt}(f(N))$ is a finite sum $\sum_i w_i \det B_i$, where each row of $B_i$ is $f$ of an element of $N$.

Lean: `New.Selmer.exists_sum_mul_det_of_mem_fittingIdeal_map` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/FittingBlock.lean).

*Proof.* The ideal is spanned by the determinants of matrices with rows in $f(N)$. Choose a preimage in $N$ for each row, and use induction on the span. $\square$
