## Norms at one prime

This section is pure algebra. A finite group $\Gamma$ maps to a ring $R$ by a multiplicative map $\rho$, and we write $g$ for $\rho(g)$. The *norm* is $\mathrm{Nm} = \sum_{g \in \Gamma} g$. In the application, $R$ is a ring of operators on automorphic forms, $\Gamma = \prod_j \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{w_j})$ acts at the auxiliary places, and forms of base level are the forms fixed by $\Gamma$. The section proves three things: a product of first-order deformations $\prod_j (\mathrm{Nm}_j + X_j\,\partial_j)$ factors through $\mathrm{Nm}$; the transfer of the first entry on a line stabilizer is the determinant; and logarithms, which are norms weighted by a homomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}/m$, satisfy three congruences.

Nothing here uses number theory except the last part, which uses the residue field of a local field and the pro-$p$ Iwahori subgroup of [Iwahori-Hecke algebras and the Satake map](/chapter/iwahori-satake/).

### Congruences as ring maps

A congruence $a \equiv b$ is written as $\sigma(a) = \sigma(b)$ for a ring map $\sigma \colon R \to S$.

**Definition (Reduction modulo $m/k$).** A ring map $\sigma \colon R \to S$ is a *reduction modulo $m/k$* if $\sigma(x) = 0$ whenever $kx = my$ for some $y \in R$.

Lean: `New.Patching.IsReductionMod` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Lemma.** A reduction modulo $m/k$ has $m = 0$ in $S$.

Lean: `New.Patching.IsReductionMod.natCast_eq_zero` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Take $x = m$ and $y = k$. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $R$ be an $O$-algebra without $k$-torsion, and let $\mu \in O$ with $k\mu = m$. Then there is a reduction $\sigma$ modulo $m/k$ such that $\sigma(a) = \sigma(b)$ implies $a - b \in \mu R$.

Lean: `New.Patching.exists_isReductionMod` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Let $\sigma$ be the quotient map $R \to R/\mu R$, where $\mu R$ is the two-sided ideal generated by $\mu$. If $kx = my = k\mu y$, then $x = \mu y$ because $R$ has no $k$-torsion, so $\sigma(x) = 0$. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** A reduction is an abstract ring map, not a fixed quotient. All results of this section and the next are stated for every such $\sigma$, and the last lemma shows that the statements are not empty. In the application $m = \ell^\nu$ and $k = 2\,n!$, so the congruences hold modulo $\ell^\nu/(2n!)$ in $R$.

### Norms

**Lemma.** For every $g \in \Gamma$, $g\,\mathrm{Nm} = \mathrm{Nm} = \mathrm{Nm}\,g$.

Lean: `New.Patching.mul_sum_monoidHom`, `New.Patching.sum_monoidHom_mul` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Multiplication by $g$ permutes $\Gamma$. $\square$

An element $\Psi \in R$ with $\sum_g g^{-1} \Psi g = n$ plays the part of $1/\#\Gamma$ when $\#\Gamma$ is not a unit. In the application it comes from [the Higman operator](/decl/New.Patching.BaseLevel.exists_higman_operator/).

**Lemma.** Let $\sum_g g^{-1}\Psi g = n$. If $gA = A$ for all $g$, then $nA = \mathrm{Nm}\,\Psi A$. If $Ag = A$ for all $g$, then $nA = A \Psi\,\mathrm{Nm}$.

Lean: `New.Patching.nsmul_eq_norm_mul`, `New.Patching.nsmul_eq_mul_norm` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* For the second claim, $A\Psi\,\mathrm{Nm} = \sum_g A \Psi g = \sum_g A g^{-1}\Psi g = A \cdot n$. The first is symmetric. $\square$

**Lemma.** Let $\Gamma = \prod_j \Gamma_j$, and let $\sum_g g^{-1}\Psi g = n$. If $\sigma(A(g - 1)) = 0$ for all $g$ in every factor $\Gamma_j$, then $\sigma(nA) = \sigma(A\Psi\,\mathrm{Nm})$.

Lean: `New.Patching.map_nsmul_eq_mul_norm` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* The factors generate $\Gamma$, so $\sigma(Ag) = \sigma(A)$ for all $g \in \Gamma$. The computation of the last proof holds after $\sigma$. $\square$

### The family of first-order deformations

Let $\mathcal{A}_j$, for $j$ in a finite set $J$, be subrings of $R$ that commute pairwise: $ab = ba$ for $a \in \mathcal{A}_i$, $b \in \mathcal{A}_j$, $i \neq j$. Then a product $\prod_j x_j$ with $x_j \in \mathcal{A}_j$ does not depend on the order. Any factor can be written last, and a product of sums expands as a sum of products.

Lean: `New.Patching.commutingProd`, `New.Patching.commutingProd_update`, `New.Patching.commutingProd_sum` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Definition (Coefficients of the family).** For $\mathrm{Nm}_j, \partial_j \in \mathcal{A}_j$ and $S \subseteq J$, put
$$N(S) = \prod_{j \in S} \partial_j \prod_{j \notin S} \mathrm{Nm}_j .$$
This is the coefficient of $\prod_{j \in S} X_j$ in $\prod_j (\mathrm{Nm}_j + X_j\,\partial_j)$, with commuting variables $X_j$.

Lean: `New.Patching.familyCoeff` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Lemma.** The coefficients have these properties.

1. An element that commutes with all $\mathcal{A}_j$ commutes with every $N(S)$.
2. If $\mathrm{Nm}_j = \sum_{g \in \Gamma_j} g$ and $\Gamma_j$ maps into $\mathcal{A}_j$, then $N(\emptyset) = \mathrm{Nm}$.
3. For $j \notin S$ there is $N'$ with $N(S) = N'\,\mathrm{Nm}_j$ and $N(S \cup \{j\}) = N'\,\partial_j$.

Lean: `New.Patching.commute_familyCoeff`, `New.Patching.familyCoeff_empty`, `New.Patching.exists_familyCoeff_eq_mul` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Claim 1 holds factor by factor. For claim 2, the product of the sums $\mathrm{Nm}_j$ expands to the sum over $\Gamma$. For claim 3, write the factor at $j$ last; $N'$ is the product of the other factors. $\square$

The later results use only properties 1 to 3, so the source packs them into one statement.

**Lemma.** Let $\Gamma_j$ and $\partial_j$ lie in commuting subrings $\mathcal{A}_j$. There are elements $N(S)$ with properties 1 to 3 of the last lemma, for $\mathrm{Nm}_j = \sum_{g \in \Gamma_j} g$.

Lean: `New.Patching.exists_empty_eq_sum_and_insert_eq_mul` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

Two coefficient identities are needed for polynomials over a commutative ring $T$. For $j \notin S$ and $G \in T[X_i : i \in J]$, the coefficient of $\prod_{i \in S} X_i$ in $G \cdot (t - X_j r)$ is $t$ times that in $G$. The coefficient of $X_j \prod_{i \in S} X_i$ is $t$ times that in $G$ minus $r$ times the coefficient of $\prod_{i \in S} X_i$ in $G$.

Lean: `New.Patching.coeff_mul_C_sub_X_mul_C_of_notMem`, `New.Patching.coeff_mul_C_sub_X_mul_C_insert` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Proposition (The family kills ties).** Let $T$ be a commutative ring with a ring map $T \to R$, and let $N(S)$ satisfy property 3. Let $\mathcal{N} = \sum_S N(S) \prod_{i \in S} X_i$. Fix $j$ and $t, r \in T$ with
$$\mathrm{Nm}_j\, t = 0, \qquad \sigma(\partial_j\, t) = \sigma(\mathrm{Nm}_j\, r).$$
Then for every $G \in T[X_i]$, the coefficient of $\prod_{i \in J} X_i$ in $\mathcal{N} \cdot G \cdot (t - X_j r)$ is $0$ after $\sigma$. That coefficient is $\sum_S N(S^c)\,\mathrm{coeff}_S(G(t - X_j r))$.

Lean: `New.Patching.map_top_coeff_mul_C_sub_X_mul_C_eq_zero` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Split the sum over $S$ into pairs $S$ and $S \cup \{j\}$ with $j \notin S$. Write $b$ and $b'$ for the coefficients of $G$ at $S$ and at $S \cup \{j\}$. By property 3, applied to the complement, $N(S^c) = N'\,\partial_j$ and $N((S \cup \{j\})^c) = N'\,\mathrm{Nm}_j$. The pair contributes
$$N'\partial_j\, t b + N'\mathrm{Nm}_j\,(t b' - r b) .$$
The term $N'\mathrm{Nm}_j\, t b'$ is $0$ because $\mathrm{Nm}_j t = 0$. After $\sigma$, the term $N'\partial_j t b$ equals $N'\mathrm{Nm}_j r b$. So the pair contributes $0$. $\square$

In the application, $t$ is a trace $\mathrm{tr}[(\gamma_j - 1)g]$ and $r$ is a trace at $\varphi_j$. So $t - X_j r$ is a generator of the tied ideal, and the proposition says that the top coefficient of $\mathcal{N}$ kills the tied ideal modulo $\sigma$.

**Theorem (The family factors through the norm).** Let $\Gamma = \prod_j \Gamma_j$, let $N(S)$ satisfy $N(\emptyset) = \mathrm{Nm}$ and property 3, and let $\sum_g g^{-1}\Psi g = n$. Let $\delta'$ commute with $\Gamma$ and with all $N(S)$, and put $\delta = n^2\delta'$. Suppose that for each $j$ and each $g \in \Gamma_j$ there is $Z$ with
$$\sigma(n\,\partial_j (g - 1)) = \sigma(\mathrm{Nm}_j Z), \qquad \delta'\,\mathrm{Nm}\, Z = 0 .$$
Then for each $S$ and each $i \geq \#S$ there is $Y$ with $\sigma(\delta^i N(S)) = \sigma(Y\,\mathrm{Nm})$, and $Y = \delta^i$ if $S = \emptyset$.

Lean: `New.Patching.exists_map_pow_mul_family_eq_mul_norm` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* By strong induction on $S$. For $S = \emptyset$ take $Y = \delta^i$. Otherwise $i = e + 1$ for some $e \geq \#S - 1$. Put $A = n\,\delta'\delta^e N(S)$. Then $\delta^{e+1} N(S) = nA$, so by the lemma on norms of a product group it is enough to show $\sigma(A(g - 1)) = 0$ for $g \in \Gamma_j$, for every $j$. Then $Y = A\Psi$.

If $j \notin S$, then $N(S) = N'\,\mathrm{Nm}_j$, and $\mathrm{Nm}_j (g - 1) = 0$.

If $j \in S$, write $N(S) = N'\partial_j$ and $N(S \setminus \{j\}) = N'\,\mathrm{Nm}_j$. By induction, $\sigma(\delta^e N(S \setminus \{j\})) = \sigma(Y'\,\mathrm{Nm})$ for some $Y'$. Then
$$\sigma(A(g - 1)) = \sigma(\delta'\delta^e N')\,\sigma(n\,\partial_j(g - 1)) = \sigma(\delta'\delta^e N'\,\mathrm{Nm}_j Z) = \sigma(Y'\,\mathrm{Nm}\,\delta' Z) .$$
Here $\delta'$ moves to the right of $\delta^e N(S \setminus \{j\})$ because it commutes with $\Gamma$ and with $N(S \setminus \{j\})$. It also commutes with $\mathrm{Nm}$. So the last term is $\sigma(Y' \delta'\,\mathrm{Nm}\, Z) = 0$. $\square$

**Example.** Let $J = \{j\}$. Then $N(\emptyset) = \mathrm{Nm}_j = \mathrm{Nm}$ and $N(\{j\}) = \partial_j$. The theorem for $S = \{j\}$ and $i = 1$ says that $\sigma(n^2\delta'\partial_j) = \sigma(Y\,\mathrm{Nm})$ with $Y = n\delta'\partial_j\Psi$. So $n^2\delta'\partial_j$ maps every form into the forms of base level, up to $\sigma$. The cost is the factor $n^2\delta'$, one factor of $n$ for each use of $\Psi$.

### The first entry and the determinant

Let $F$ be a finite field and $N \geq 1$. The stabilizer of the line of the first basis vector in $\mathrm{GL}_N(F)$ consists of the matrices whose first column vanishes below the first entry. The *first entry* $g \mapsto g_{00}$ is a character of it to $F^\times$: the scalar by which $g$ acts on the line.

Lean: `New.Patching.residueLineStabilizer`, `New.Patching.firstEntry` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Theorem.** The transfer of the first entry from the line stabilizer to $\mathrm{GL}_N(F)$ is the determinant. So for every homomorphism $l \colon F^\times \to A$ to a commutative group, the transfer of $l \circ \mathrm{firstEntry}$ is $l \circ \det$.

Lean: `New.Patching.transfer_firstEntry`, `New.Patching.transfer_comp_firstEntry` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* The cosets of the stabilizer are the lines of $F^N$. The transfer at $g$ is a product over the orbits of $g$ on lines: an orbit of length $f$ through the line of $v$ contributes the scalar by which $g^f$ multiplies $v$. For $g = \mathrm{diag}(x, 1, \ldots, 1)$, the line of the first basis vector contributes $x$. Every other orbit passes through a vector $v$ with a nonzero entry that $g$ fixes, so it contributes $1$. Conjugation by a permutation matrix gives the same value $x$ for $x$ in any diagonal place. A character of $\mathrm{GL}_N(F)$ with values in $F^\times$ is trivial on transvections, because $c \mapsto \tau(\text{transvection } c)$ is a homomorphism from a group of order $\#F$ to one of order $\#F - 1$, and these orders are coprime. Both the transfer and $\det$ are characters that agree on diagonal matrices and on transvections, which generate $\mathrm{GL}_N(F)$. $\square$

Now let $w$ be a finite place of a number field with residue field $\mathbb{F}_w$. For $g \in \mathrm{GL}_N(O_w)$, the reduction of $g$ stabilizes the line exactly when $g$ lies in the parahoric subgroup with blocks of sizes $1$ and $N - 1$. If $g$ lies in the pro-$p$ Iwahori subgroup, so that its reduction is upper triangular unipotent, then the reduction has determinant $1$ and first entry $1$.

Lean: `New.Patching.reduceGL_mem_residueLineStabilizer_iff`, `New.Patching.det_firstEntry_reduceGL_eq_one_of_mem_proIwahori` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

If $m$ divides $\#\mathbb{F}_w - 1$ and $z$ generates $\mathbb{F}_w^\times$, there is a homomorphism $\mathbb{F}_w^\times \to \mathbb{Z}/m$ that sends $z$ to $1$. It exists because $\mathbb{F}_w^\times$ is cyclic of order divisible by $m$.

Lean: `New.Patching.exists_residue_log` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

### Logarithms

From here on, $\sigma \colon R \to S$ is a ring map with $m = 0$ in $S$. An additive map $B$ on weights $f \colon \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}$ takes weights that agree modulo $m$ to elements with the same image under $\sigma$.

Lean: `New.Patching.map_eq_of_weights_modEq` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Definition (Weighted sum).** For a homomorphism $c \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}/m$ and a weight $f$, put $\mathrm{W}_c(f) = \sum_g f(c(g))\, g$. The weight $1$ gives $\mathrm{Nm}$. The weight $\mathrm{valMinAbs}$, which lifts $x \in \mathbb{Z}/m$ to its representative of least absolute value, gives the *logarithm* $\mathcal{L}_c = \mathrm{W}_c(\mathrm{valMinAbs})$.

Lean: `New.Patching.weightedSum`, `New.Patching.weightedSum_one` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

Left or right multiplication by $g$ shifts the weight: $g\,\mathrm{W}_c(f) = \mathrm{W}_c(f(\cdot - c(g)))$, and the same on the right.

Lean: `New.Patching.mul_weightedSum`, `New.Patching.weightedSum_mul` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

For a subgroup $H$, let $h$ run over fixed representatives of $\Gamma/H$. Put $\mathrm{Nm}_{\Gamma/H} = \sum_h h$ and $\mathrm{Nm}^-_{\Gamma/H} = \sum_h h^{-1}$.

Lean: `New.Patching.cosetNorm`, `New.Patching.cosetNormInv` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

Then $\mathrm{Nm}_{\Gamma/H} \cdot \sum_{p \in H} p = \mathrm{Nm}$. The transfer of $a \colon H \to A$ at $g$ is the product over the representatives $h$ of $a(p)$, where $gh = h'p$ with $h'$ a representative.

Lean: `New.Patching.cosetNorm_mul_norm`, `New.Patching.transfer_eq_prod_cosetComponent` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

**Definition (Coset derivative).** For an additive map $B$ on weights, put $\partial = -\mathrm{Nm}_{\Gamma/H}\, B(\mathrm{valMinAbs})\, \mathrm{Nm}^-_{\Gamma/H}$.

Lean: `New.Patching.cosetDerivative` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

For $B = \mathrm{W}_a$ with $a \colon H \to \mathbb{Z}/m$, the middle factor is the logarithm of $a$, and $\partial$ is minus its induction to $\Gamma$.

**Lemma (Three congruences).** Let $m = 0$ in $S$.

1. For $a \colon H \to \mathbb{Z}/m$, $\sigma(\mathrm{Nm}_{\Gamma/H}\,\mathcal{L}_a\,\mathrm{Nm}^-_{\Gamma/H}) = \sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tr}(a)})$, where $\mathrm{tr}(a)$ is the transfer of $a$ to $\Gamma$.
2. If $c(\gamma) = 1$, then $\sigma((\gamma - 1)\mathcal{L}_c) = -\sigma(\mathrm{Nm})$.
3. If right multiplication by $p \in H$ shifts the weights of $B$ by $a(p)$, then for each $g \in \Gamma$ there is $Q$ with $\sigma(B(\mathrm{valMinAbs})\,\mathrm{Nm}^-_{\Gamma/H}(g - 1)) = \sigma(B(1)\,Q)$.

Lean: `New.Patching.map_cosetNorm_mul_weightedSum_mul_cosetNormInv`, `New.Patching.map_sub_one_mul_weightedSum_valMinAbs`, `New.Patching.exists_map_valMinAbs_mul_sub_one` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* For 1, the coefficient of $g$ on the left is the sum over the representatives of the logarithms of the components of $g$. Modulo $m$ this is the logarithm of the transfer at $g$. For 2, $\gamma\mathcal{L}_c = \sum_g \mathrm{valMinAbs}(c(g) - 1)\, g$, and $\mathrm{valMinAbs}(x - 1) \equiv \mathrm{valMinAbs}(x) - 1$ modulo $m$. So $\sigma(\gamma\mathcal{L}_c) = \sigma(\mathcal{L}_c) - \sigma(\mathrm{Nm})$. For 3, right multiplication by $g$ permutes the $h^{-1}$ up to factors in $H$, which shift the weight by constants. A shift by a constant $s$ changes $\mathrm{valMinAbs}$ by $-s$ modulo $m$, which gives $-s\,B(1)$. $\square$

**Lemma (Shifts kill the logarithm).** Let $\sigma$ be a reduction modulo $m/(2k)$, and let $C$ be an additive map on weights with $C(f(\cdot - k) - f) = 0$ for all $f$. Then $\sigma(C(\mathrm{valMinAbs})) = 0$.

Lean: `New.Patching.map_valMinAbs_eq_zero_of_shift` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* Let $f(x) = \mathrm{valMinAbs}(x)\,(\mathrm{valMinAbs}(x) + k)$. Then $f(x - k) - f(x) \equiv -2k\,\mathrm{valMinAbs}(x)$ modulo $m$, so $f(\cdot - k) - f + 2k\,\mathrm{valMinAbs} = m\,d$ for an integer weight $d$. Apply $C$: the first part is $0$ by hypothesis, so $2k\, C(\mathrm{valMinAbs}) = m\, C(d)$. As $\sigma$ is a reduction modulo $m/(2k)$, $\sigma(C(\mathrm{valMinAbs})) = 0$. $\square$

This is the origin of the factor $2$ in $\ell^\nu/(2n!)$: the quadratic weight $f$ is needed to turn a shift into a multiple of $\mathrm{valMinAbs}$.

**Theorem (The derivative is a norm up to $n$).** Let $m = 0$ in $S$, $a \colon H \to \mathbb{Z}/m$, and let $B$ be an additive map on weights such that right multiplication by $p \in H$ shifts weights by $a(p)$ and $p\,B(1) = B(1)$. Let $\sum_{p \in H} p^{-1}\Psi p = n$, let $L g = L$ for all $g \in \Gamma$, and let $L\,B(1) = 0$. If $R$ has no $\#H$-torsion, then for each $g \in \Gamma$ there is $Z$ with
$$\sigma(n\,\partial\,(g - 1)) = \sigma(\mathrm{Nm}\, Z), \qquad L Z = 0,$$
where $\partial$ is the coset derivative of $B$.

Lean: `New.Patching.exists_map_nsmul_mul_sub_one_eq_sum_mul` (New/Congruences/FirstOrder/OnePrime.lean).

*Proof.* By congruence 3, $\sigma(B(\mathrm{valMinAbs})\,\mathrm{Nm}^-_{\Gamma/H}(g - 1)) = \sigma(B(1) Q)$. Since $H$ fixes $B(1)$ from the left, the lemma on norms gives $n B(1) = (\sum_{p \in H} p)\,\Psi\,B(1)$. So
$$\sigma(n\,\partial(g - 1)) = -\sigma(\mathrm{Nm}_{\Gamma/H} \cdot nB(1) Q) = \sigma(\mathrm{Nm} \cdot (-\Psi B(1) Q)) .$$
Put $Z = -\Psi B(1) Q$. For $LZ = 0$: $\#H \cdot L\Psi B(1) = L\,(\sum_p p)\,\Psi B(1) = L \cdot nB(1) = 0$, since $Lp = L$. As $R$ has no $\#H$-torsion, $L\Psi B(1) = 0$. $\square$

This theorem gives condition 3 of the theorem on the operator $\partial$ in [the operator at one place](/chapter/first-order-deformations/#the-operator-at-one-place-and-the-deformed-norm), with $L = \delta\,\mathrm{Nm}$.
