## The field $E$ and its roots of unity

This section defines the CM field $E = K(\zeta)$ and finds its roots of unity. Then it explains the standing hypothesis of a primitive root of unity of order $5040$.

### The tower degree and the moduli

**Definition (Tower degree).** For $s \ge 0$ let $c(s) = \lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor + 5$, with the convention $\lfloor \log_3 0 \rfloor = 0$. Put
$$l_0(s) = 3^{c(s) - 1}, \qquad \mathrm{towerDeg}(s) = 3^{c(s)} \cdot 2^s = 3 \cdot l_0(s) \cdot 2^s, \qquad q_1 = q_0 \cdot \mathrm{towerDeg}(s).$$

Lean: `New.l₀`, `New.towerDeg`, `New.towerDeg_eq`, `New.Parameters.q₁` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

For $s \ge 1$ the number $l_0(s)$ is the least power of $3$ above $27s$. The source proves $27 s < l_0(s)$ for all $s$, and $l_0(s) \le 81 s$ for $s \neq 0$ (Lean: `New.mul_lt_l₀`, `New.l₀_le_mul`). In the data, $2^s = m^{22}$, the number $l_0$ bounds the exponents in the sparseness condition on the $b$-places, and $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$ is the number of $a$-places (Section 4). See [the data and its sizes](/chapter/data-and-sizes/). Here only two facts matter: $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$ has no prime factors other than $2$ and $3$, and $3 \mid \mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$.

**Definition.** For parameters $(q_0, d)$ and $s \ge 0$ put $q_2 = (q_0 d)^{s+6}$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.q₂` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

The source proves $q_1 d \mid q_2$ (Lean: `New.Parameters.q₁_mul_d_dvd_q₂`), since $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s) \mid 3^{s+5} 2^{s+5}$ and $6 \mid q_0$. The primes of the data are chosen congruent to $1$ modulo $q_2^2$. Section 6 uses this.

### The field $E$

**Definition (The field E).** Let $(q_0, d)$ be parameters and $s \ge 0$. Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{d}) \subset \mathbb{C}$. The field $E$ is the cyclotomic extension $K(\zeta)$ of $K$ generated by a primitive root of unity $\zeta$ of order $q_1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.E`, `New.Parameters.K`, `New.Parameters.ζ` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The field $K$ is the subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by $i\sqrt{d}$. The field $E$ is not a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$. It is Mathlib's `CyclotomicField q₁ K`, an abstract splitting field of $X^{q_1} - 1$ over $K$, and $\zeta$ is a chosen primitive root in $E$. The source fixes one $K$-embedding of $E$ into $\mathbb{C}$ and builds all others from it (Section 4).

**Theorem.** The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{q_1}$ is irreducible over $K$. So $[E : K] = \varphi(q_1)$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times$, with $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times$ acting by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^a$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.irreducible_cyclotomic` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* Let $M = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_d)$. For $d \equiv 3 \bmod 4$ the Gauss sum $\sum_a (a/d) \zeta_d^a$ is a square root of $-d$, so $K \subset M$, and it is enough to prove that $\Phi_{q_1}$ is irreducible over $M$. Since $q_1$ is prime to $d$, the field $L = M(\zeta_{q_1})$ contains a primitive root of order $q_1 d$. So $[L : \mathbb{Q}] \ge \varphi(q_1)\varphi(d)$ and $[L : M] \ge \varphi(q_1) = \deg \Phi_{q_1}$. $\square$

The proof uses only that $q_1$ and $d$ are coprime (Section 2). The classical form is: if $m$ and $n$ are coprime then $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) = \mathbb{Q}$ (Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, Chapter 2).

**Proposition.** There is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra embedding of $E$ into the cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1 d})$. So $E$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ and over $K$. The field $E$ is totally complex, and it is a CM field.

Lean: `New.Parameters.toCycloField` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* The Gauss sum of a primitive $d$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1 d})$ embeds $K$, and $\Phi_{q_1}$ splits there. The universal property of the splitting field gives the embedding. $\square$

The degree of $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $2\varphi(q_1)$. Since $E = K \cdot \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1})$ and the two fields are linearly disjoint,
$$\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \times \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \{\pm 1\} \times (\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times.$$
Complex conjugation is the element $(-1, -1)$: it moves $\sqrt{-d}$ and inverts $\zeta$ (Lean: `New.Parameters.star_zeta`). The maximal real subfield $E^+$ is its fixed field. Section 5 uses this description to compute the splitting of primes.

### The roots of unity of $E$

**Theorem.** Every root of unity $y$ of $E$ is a power $\zeta^k$ with $0 \le k < q_1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.exists_zeta_pow_eq` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* Embed $E$ into $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1 d})$. The roots of unity there form a cyclic group of order $q_1 d$, since $q_1 d$ is even. So $y^{q_1 d} = 1$. Suppose $y^{q_1} \neq 1$. Then $y^{q_1}$ has order dividing the prime $d$, and not $1$, so it is a primitive $d$-th root of unity. So $E$ contains primitive roots of unity of the coprime orders $q_1$ and $d$, and
$$\varphi(d) \varphi(q_1) \le [E : \mathbb{Q}] = 2\varphi(q_1).$$
This gives $d - 1 \le 2$, against $d > 64$. So $y^{q_1} = 1$, and $y$ is a power of the primitive root $\zeta$. $\square$

So the torsion of $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$ is the cyclic group $\langle \zeta \rangle$ of order $q_1$.

**Lemma.** $N_{E/K}(\zeta) = 1$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.norm_zeta_eq_one` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* The norm is the product of the conjugates $\zeta^a$, $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times$, that is, $\zeta^{\sigma}$ with $\sigma$ the sum of the units modulo $q_1$. For $q_1 > 2$ the units come in pairs $\{a, -a\}$, so $\sigma \equiv 0 \bmod q_1$. Here $q_1 \ge 18480$. $\square$

### The standing hypothesis: a root of unity of order $5040$

Many results of the library of the source are stated for any CM field $E$ that contains a primitive $5040$-th root of unity. The source calls such a field stably unramified. The reason for the name is the next proposition.

**Definition.** A CM field $E$ satisfies the hypothesis $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$ if it contains a primitive root of unity of order $5040 = 7!$.

Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040` (Lib/Adelic/Adeles/Basic.lean).

**Proposition.** The field $E$ of the parameters satisfies $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$, for every $s$.

Lean: an instance of `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040` (New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean).

*Proof.* We have $3 \mid \mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$, so $55440 = 3 \cdot 18480 \mid q_1$. And $55440 = 11 \cdot 5040$. So $\zeta^{q_1/5040}$ is a primitive $5040$-th root of unity. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The instance has no name in the source. It is at [PrimeDecomposition.lean](lean:New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean#L137). So every result of the library under the hypothesis applies to $E$ at once.

The source uses the divisors $4$, $3$, $7$, $80$ and $63$ of $5040$. A field with a primitive $5040$-th root of unity contains a primitive $n$-th root of unity in its ring of integers for every $n \mid 5040$ (Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.exists_isPrimitiveRoot_of_dvd`). Complex conjugation inverts every root of unity of a CM field. We now list the consequences.

**Lemma.** Let $E$ be a CM field with $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$. Then $\mathcal{O}_E$ contains an element $i$ with $i^2 = -1$ and $\bar\imath = -i$, and an element $\omega$ with $\omega + \bar\omega = 1$ and $\omega \bar\omega = 2$.

Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.exists_add_star_eq_one_mul_star_eq_two` (Lib/Adelic/Adeles/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* A primitive $4$-th root of unity $i$ has $i^2 = -1$ and $\bar\imath = i^{-1} = -i$ (Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.exists_mul_self_eq_neg_one`). Let $\eta$ be a primitive $7$-th root of unity, so $\bar\eta = \eta^6$, and let $\omega = -(\eta + \eta^2 + \eta^4)$. Then $\bar\omega = -(\eta^3 + \eta^5 + \eta^6)$, so $\omega + \bar\omega = -(\eta + \cdots + \eta^6) = 1$. The product $(\eta + \eta^2 + \eta^4)(\eta^3 + \eta^5 + \eta^6)$ is a sum of nine powers of $\eta$, in which each of $\eta, \ldots, \eta^6$ occurs once and $\eta^7 = 1$ occurs three times. So it equals $3 + (-1) = 2$. $\square$

**Proposition.** Let $E$ be a CM field with $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$. Then some $y \in \mathcal{O}_E$ has $y - \bar y$ a unit.

Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.exists_isUnit_sub_star` (Lib/Adelic/Adeles/Basic.lean).

*Proof.* Take $y = i\omega$. Then $\bar y = -i\bar\omega$, so $y - \bar y = i(\omega + \bar\omega) = i$, with inverse $-i$. $\square$

The proposition gives the name. Let $\sigma$ be complex conjugation, the generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(E/E^+)$. If a prime $\mathfrak{P}$ of $E$ were ramified over $E^+$, then $\sigma$ would lie in its inertia group, and $\sigma(y) \equiv y \bmod \mathfrak{P}$ for all $y \in \mathcal{O}_E$. The element $y - \bar y$ is a unit, so this is impossible. So $E/E^+$ is unramified at every finite place. Every CM field that contains $E$ inherits the property. This is one reading of the word "stably".

**Proposition.** Let $E$ be a CM field with $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$, and let $w$ be a finite place of $E$.

1. If $2 \in w$, that is, $w$ lies over $2$, then $\bar w \neq w$.
2. If $\bar w = w$, then $q_w = r^2$ for a natural number $r$ with $r \equiv 3 \bmod 4$.
3. If $q_w$ is prime, then $\bar w \neq w$.

Lean: `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.isSplit_of_two_mem`, `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040.exists_sq_eq_q_of_inert`, `New.isSplit_of_q_prime` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/FrobeniusOrbits.lean).

*Proof.* For 1: $\omega \bar\omega = 2 \in w$, so $\omega \in w$ or $\bar\omega \in w$. If $\bar w = w$, both lie in $w$, and then $1 = \omega + \bar\omega \in w$, which is false. For 2: if $\bar w = w$, then conjugation acts on the residue field $\mathbb{F}_{q_w}$, and it is not trivial there, since $w$ is unramified over $E^+$. So $q_w = r^2$ and conjugation is the Frobenius $x \mapsto x^r$. It sends the image of $i$ to the image of $-i$, so $i^{r} = -i$ in $\mathbb{F}_{q_w}$. By part 1 the residue characteristic is odd, so $i \neq -i$ there, and $i^{r-1} = -1$ gives $r \equiv 3 \bmod 4$. Part 3 follows from part 2, since a prime is not a square. $\square$

**Proposition.** Let $E$ be a CM field with $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$. Then every finite place $w$ of $E$ has $q_w \ge 4$.

Lean: `New.FinPlace.four_le_q` (Lib/SiegelWeil/UnramifiedSeries/VacuumCoefficients.lean).

*Proof.* The residue field contains the images of a primitive cube root of unity $z$ and of $i$. If $q_w = 2$, the image of $z$ is a root of $1 + X + X^2$ in $\mathbb{F}_2$, and there is none. If $q_w = 3$, the image of $i$ is a root of $1 + X^2$ in $\mathbb{F}_3$, and there is none. $\square$

The last use takes all of $5040$. Let $u$ be a prime of $K$ over a rational prime $p$ that splits in $K$ and is prime to $d$, in the precise form of the source: $u$ contains an element $\beta_K - n$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, prime to its conjugate.

**Theorem.** Let $E \supset K$ be a CM field with $\mathrm{HasPrimitiveRoot5040}$, let $u$ be such a prime of $K$, and let $a$ and $b$ be finite places of $E$. Then some place $v$ of $E$ over $u$ satisfies $v \ne a$, $\bar v \ne a$, $v \ne b$ and $\bar v \ne b$.

Lean: `New.GaloisRep.IsSplitPrimeToDisc.exists_place_above_ne` (Lib/GaloisRep/Main/QuadraticStep.lean).

*Proof.* Suppose not. Then every place of $E$ over $p$ is one of $a, b, \bar a, \bar b$. Here we use that the places over $\bar u$ are the conjugates of the places over $u$. Let $m \mid 5040$ with $p \nmid m$, and let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m) \subset E$. Then $p$ is unramified in $F$, and every prime of $F$ over $p$ has residue degree $f = \mathrm{ord}_m(p)$ (Neukirch, Chapter I, Proposition 10.3). The number of these primes is $\varphi(m)/f$, and it is at most $4$. So $\varphi(m) \le 4 f \le 4k$ for any exponent $k$ of the group $(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times$. Take $m = 80$: $\varphi(80) = 32$ and the exponent of $(\mathbb{Z}/80)^\times$ is $4$, so $32 \le 16$, which is false. So $p \mid 80$. Take $m = 63$: $\varphi(63) = 36$ and the exponent is $6$, so $36 \le 24$, which is false. So $p \mid 63$. But $\gcd(80, 63) = 1$. $\square$

Here $5040 = 80 \cdot 63$, and both factors are needed. The theorem lets the source choose a place over a given split prime of $K$ while it avoids two places that are already in use.

**Remark (Formalization).** The source also uses three weaker consequences of the hypothesis. The cyclotomic character of $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)$ is $1$ modulo $4$, since $4 \mid 5040$ (Lean: `New.Selmer.four_dvd_cyclotomicChar_sub_one`). A CM field with the hypothesis has degree at least $\varphi(5040) = 1152$, hence at least two infinite places. This book has found no use of the factorial $7!$ itself. The source uses the divisors $3, 4, 7, 63, 80$, and $5040 = \mathrm{lcm}(63, 80)$ is the least number with all of them as divisors.
