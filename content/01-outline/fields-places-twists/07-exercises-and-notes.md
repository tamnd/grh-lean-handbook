## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** Let $8 \mid q_0$, $q_0 \mid 2(d+1)$ and $q_0 \nmid d + 1$. Show that $d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \bmod q_0$, that $d \equiv 7 \bmod 8$ and that $d^2 \equiv 1 \bmod q_0$.

*Hint.* Write $2(d+1) = q_0 k$. Then $k$ is odd, so $d + 1 = (q_0/2) k \equiv q_0/2 \bmod q_0$. For the square, expand $(q_0/2 \cdot k - 1)^2$ and use $4 \mid q_0/2$.

**Exercise 2.** Let $p$ be a prime with $p \mid 18480$. Show with the criterion of Dedekind and Kummer that $p$ splits in $K$. Then show that for $q_0 = 18480$ and each sign $\epsilon$ there is a valid prime $d$ with $\chi_K(13) = \epsilon$. So the bound $13 \le p$ for inert primes $p$ is the best that the class of $d$ gives.

*Hint.* For the second part, quadratic reciprocity gives $(13/d) = (d/13)$. Choose the class of $d$ modulo $13$ and use the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem on primes in progressions.

**Exercise 3.** Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1})$. Show that the discriminant of $E$ is $(-d)^{\varphi(q_1)} \cdot \mathrm{disc}(L)^2$, and deduce $\mathrm{rd}_E = \sqrt{d} \cdot \mathrm{rd}_L$. With the formula for $\mathrm{disc}(L)$, show
$$\mathrm{rd}_E = \sqrt{d} \cdot \frac{q_1}{\prod_{p \mid q_0} p^{1/(p-1)}}.$$
Compare this with the bound $\mathrm{rd}_E \le q_1 d$ of Section 4.

*Hint.* The discriminants of $K$ and $L$ are prime to each other, so the rings of integers satisfy $\mathcal{O}_E = \mathcal{O}_K \otimes \mathcal{O}_L$. For $\mathrm{disc}(L)$ see Washington, Proposition 2.7. The primes of $q_1$ are the primes of $q_0$.

**Exercise 4.** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be prime and let $w$ be a place of $E$ over $p$. Show that $\bar w = w$ if and only if $\chi_K(p) = -1$ and $p^k \equiv -1 \bmod q_1$ for some odd $k$. Deduce that no place over a prime $p \equiv 1 \bmod 5$ is fixed by conjugation.

*Hint.* The decomposition group of $w$ is generated by $(\chi_K(p), p \bmod q_1)$, and complex conjugation is $(-1, -1)$. For the last part, use $5 \mid q_1$.

**Exercise 5.** Let $\eta$ be a primitive $7$-th root of unity and $\omega = -(\eta + \eta^2 + \eta^4)$. Show $\omega + \bar\omega = 1$ and $\omega \bar\omega = 2$. Then show $\omega^2 - \omega + 2 = 0$, so $\omega = (1 \pm \sqrt{-7})/2$.

*Hint.* The conjugate of $\eta$ is $\eta^{-1} = \eta^6$, so $\bar\omega = -(\eta^3 + \eta^5 + \eta^6)$. The sum of all six primitive roots is $-1$.

**Exercise 6.** Let $\vartheta$ be a twist with set $Q$ and tame part $\psi$. Show that $\vartheta$ is an $m$-fold twist if and only if every $\psi_w$ with $w \in Q$ has order greater than $m$. Show also that if no prime $\ell \le m$ divides $q_w - 1$ for $w \in Q$, then every twist with set $Q$ is an $m$-fold twist.

*Hint.* The order of $\psi_w$ divides $q_w - 1$, and $\psi_w^n = 1$ for some $1 \le n \le m$ exactly when the order is at most $m$.

## Notes and references

The facts about quadratic and cyclotomic fields in this chapter are classical. For the decomposition of primes, the criterion of Dedekind and Kummer and the arithmetic of cyclotomic fields, see Neukirch, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Chapter I, Sections 8 and 10. For the splitting of primes in quadratic fields and the Legendre symbol, see Ireland and Rosen, *A Classical Introduction to Modern Number Theory*, Chapter 13, Section 1. For cyclotomic fields, their discriminants and the theorem of Kronecker on algebraic integers whose conjugates all have absolute value $1$, see Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, Lemma 1.6 and Chapter 2. The constant $\mathrm{CS}(d)$ is the constant of the formula of Chowla and Selberg, in S. Chowla and A. Selberg, On Epstein's zeta-function, *J. Reine Angew. Math.* 227 (1967), 86 to 110. Its use in the source is the subject of [the weighted prime remainder](/chapter/weighted-prime-remainder/).

The choices of $18480$, of the class of $d$ and of the form of the tower degree are particular to the source. The source states its constants in its guide, `GUIDE.md`, and in the module docstrings of `New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean` and `New/Reduction/Data/LevelAndTwists.lean`. The standing hypothesis `New.HasPrimitiveRoot5040` and its consequences are in `New/Derivatives/Field/PrimeDecomposition.lean`. The characters of Section 6 are in `New/Derivatives/Field/ChosenPlaces.lean` and `Lib/ClassField/HeckeCharacter/Basic.lean`.

This chapter does not claim that the argument of the source is correct. Lean accepts the statements, if the reported checks of the source hold.
