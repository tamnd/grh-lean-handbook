## The parameters, the fields and the places

This section gives the objects that depend only on the parameters $(q_0, d)$ and on the exponent $s$ of the tower. They are the modulus $q_0$, the prime $d$, the field $K$ and its character $\chi_K$, the numbers $l_0$, $q_1$ and $q_2$, the field $E$ with its root of unity $\zeta$, the group $\Phi$ of infinite places, the number $f$ of places, the root discriminant, and the three constants $C_d$, $\Omega$ and $C_P$. Volume 1, [the fields, the places and the twists](/chapter/fields-places-twists/), explains why the argument needs them.

### The modulus and the prime

The letter $q_0$ is the modulus of the main theorem, and $d$ is the prime that defines the auxiliary field. Section 1 gives the definition of the parameters. In the source, `P.q₀` and `P.d` are the two fields of `P : New.Parameters`. The exponent $\varepsilon$ of the scales is not a parameter; it enters only the existence theorem of Section 3.

**Lemma.** Let $(q_0, d)$ be parameters. Then $d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \bmod q_0$, $d \equiv 7 \bmod 8$ and $d > 64$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.instPrimeSevenModEight` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.sixty_four_lt_d` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* Since $q_0 \mid 2(d + 1)$, write $2(d + 1) = c q_0$. Since $q_0$ is even, $d + 1 = c (q_0/2)$. Since $q_0 \nmid d + 1$, the number $c$ is odd. So $d + 1 \equiv q_0/2 \bmod q_0$, which is the first claim. Since $16 \mid q_0$, $8 \mid q_0/2$, so $d + 1 \equiv 0 \bmod 8$ and $d \equiv 7 \bmod 8$. Since $d + 1 = c\, q_0/2 \geq 9240$, one has $d > 64$. $\square$

In particular $d \equiv 3 \bmod 4$, so the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ has discriminant $-d$.

**Example.** The least modulus is $q_0 = 18480 = 16 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11$. Then $q_0/2 - 1 = 9239$, and $9239$ is prime. So $(18480, 9239)$ are parameters, with $\varphi(18480) = 3840$. The next primes in the class $9239 \bmod 18480$ are $46199$ and $64679$.

### The field K and its character

**Definition (The field $K$).** The field $K = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt d)$ is the subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by $i\sqrt d$. Its generator $\sqrt{-d} = i\sqrt d$ satisfies $(\sqrt{-d})^2 = -d$.

Lean: `New.QuadField` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/ImaginaryQuadraticField.lean), `New.QuadField.sqrtNegD` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/ImaginaryQuadraticField.lean), `New.Parameters.K` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

The field $K$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$, not an abstract field. So its embedding in $\mathbb{C}$ is fixed, and $\sqrt{-d}$ has positive imaginary part. Volume 1 writes $\beta_K = (1 + \sqrt{-d})/2$ for the generator of the integers of $K$; this is a letter of the book, and it has no Lean name of its own in the files of this chapter.

**Definition (The character $\chi_K$).** For the prime $d$, $\chi_K(n) = (n/d)$ is the Legendre symbol, as a Dirichlet character modulo $d$ with values in $\mathbb{Z}$. For $d \equiv 3 \bmod 4$ it is the character of $K$: a prime $p \neq d$ splits in $K$ if $\chi_K(p) = 1$ and is inert if $\chi_K(p) = -1$.

Lean: `New.chiK` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/ImaginaryQuadraticField.lean).

The source names this character `chiK d` and defines it as `quadraticChar (ZMod d)`. The book writes $\chi_K$. Section 4 uses it to define the twists $\psi\chi_K$.

### The tower exponent and the moduli

The letter $s$ is the exponent of the tower. The data fix it by $2^s = m^{22}$ (Section 3). It is a natural number; Lean writes `D.towerExp` for the value in the data, and `s` for a free exponent.

**Definition (The numbers $l_0$, $3 l_0 2^s$, $q_1$, $q_2$).** For $s \in \mathbb{N}$ put
$$l_0(s) = 3^{\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor + 4}, \qquad \mathrm{towerDeg}(s) = 3^{\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor + 5}\, 2^s,$$
$$q_1 = q_0 \cdot \mathrm{towerDeg}(s), \qquad q_2 = (q_0 d)^{s + 6}.$$
Here $\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor$ is Lean's `Nat.log 3 s`, which is $0$ for $s = 0$.

Lean: `New.l₀` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.towerDeg` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.q₁` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.q₂` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

**Lemma.** For every $s$, $27 s < l_0(s)$ and $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s) = 3\, l_0(s)\, 2^s$. If $s \neq 0$, then $l_0(s) \leq 81 s$. For every $s$, $q_1 d$ divides $q_2$.

Lean: `New.mul_lt_l₀` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.towerDeg_eq` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.l₀_le_mul` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.q₁_mul_d_dvd_q₂` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* Let $c = \lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor$. Then $s < 3^{c+1}$, so $27 s < 3^{c+4} = l_0$. The formula for $\mathrm{towerDeg}$ is $3^{c+5} = 3 \cdot 3^{c+4}$. If $s \neq 0$ then $3^c \leq s$, so $l_0 = 81 \cdot 3^c \leq 81 s$. For the last claim, $c \leq s$, so $3^{c+5} 2^s$ divides $3^{s+5} 2^{s+5} = 6^{s+5}$. Since $6 \mid q_0$, the number $6^{s+5}$ divides $(q_0 d)^{s+5}$. So $q_1 d = q_0 d \cdot 3^{c+5} 2^s$ divides $q_0 d (q_0 d)^{s+5} = q_2$. $\square$

So $l_0$ is, for $s \geq 1$, the least power of $3$ above $27 s$. The book writes $3 l_0 2^s$ for $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$ when the factors matter, and $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$ when they do not. The three primes $p_0$, $p_1$, $N$ of the data are $\equiv 1 \bmod q_2^2$ (Section 3). Since $q_1 d \mid q_2$, they are also $\equiv 1 \bmod q_1 d$.

### The field E and its places

**Definition (The field $E$ and the root $\zeta$).** The field $E$ is the cyclotomic extension $K(\zeta)$, with $\zeta$ a primitive root of unity of order $q_1$. In Lean it is `CyclotomicField q₁ K`, and $\zeta$ is the chosen primitive root of Mathlib's cyclotomic field.

Lean: `New.Parameters.E` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.ζ` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

The field $E$ is a CM field, abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ and totally complex. It is an abstract number field, not a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$. The source proves that the cyclotomic polynomial of order $q_1$ is irreducible over $K$, so $[E : K] = \varphi(q_1)$.

**Definition (The places $\Phi$).** The set $\Phi$ is the set of infinite places of $E$. It is a group by the bijection $v \mapsto \sigma_v$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$, and so it is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times$: the place of $a$ goes to the automorphism $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^a$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.Φ` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.galOfPlace` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.placeMulEquiv` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

The group law of $\Phi$ is written as a product. The letters $\sigma$, $\tau$, $\rho$ and $v$ denote places. The CM type of $E$ in the source is the family of embeddings $\Phi(v)$, one at each place $v$; it is a fixed embedding of $E$ over $K$ composed with $\sigma_v$.

**Definition (The place of an integer and the class map).** For $\nu \in \mathbb{Z}$, the place $[\nu]$ is the place of $\nu \bmod q_1$ if $\gcd(\nu, q_1) = 1$, and the identity place $1$ otherwise. For a divisor $m$ of $q_1$, the class map $\mathrm{cl}_m : \Phi \to (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is reduction modulo $m$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.placeOfInt` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), `New.Parameters.placeClass` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The value $[\nu] = 1$ for $\nu$ not prime to $q_1$ is a junk value. It matters in the prime sum $s_{\mathrm{fin}}$ of Section 4, where the place $[-\nu]\sigma$ appears for every $\nu \leq a$. A $\nu$ that is not prime to $q_1$ gives the place $\sigma$ itself. Since $2$ and $3$ divide $q_0$, every prime factor of $q_1$ divides $q_0$, so these $\nu$ are the $\nu$ with a prime factor in $q_0$. Volume 1, [the prime sums](/chapter/prime-sums/), explains why these terms do no harm.

**Definition (The number of places).** The number $f$ is the number of infinite places of $E$, so $[E : \mathbb{Q}] = 2f$.

Lean: `New.Core.numInfPlaces` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/EmbeddingsAndDifferent.lean).

**Lemma.** $f = \mathrm{towerDeg}(s)\, \varphi(q_0)$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.numInfPlaces_E` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

*Proof.* The places correspond to $(\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times$, so $f = \varphi(q_1)$. Every prime factor of $\mathrm{towerDeg}(s)$ is $2$ or $3$, and both divide $q_0$. For $q_0 \mid q_1$ with the same prime factors, $\varphi(q_1) = \varphi(q_0)\, q_1/q_0$. $\square$

The root discriminant of $E$ is $\mathrm{rd}_E = |\mathrm{disc}\, E|^{1/[E:\mathbb{Q}]}$, Mathlib's `rootDiscr`. So $|\mathrm{disc}\, E| = \mathrm{rd}_E^{2f}$. In the data the source writes `D.rootDisc`.

**Example.** Take $(q_0, d) = (18480, 9239)$ and $s = 22$. Then $\lfloor \log_3 22 \rfloor = 2$, $l_0 = 3^6 = 729$, and indeed $27 \cdot 22 = 594 < 729 \leq 81 \cdot 22 = 1782$. So $\mathrm{towerDeg}(22) = 3 \cdot 729 \cdot 2^{22} = 9172942848$, $q_1 = 18480 \cdot 9172942848$, and $f = 9172942848 \cdot 3840$. Real data have $s \geq 22 \cdot 14$, since $m \geq 10^4$ forces $m \geq 2^{14}$; the example only shows the arithmetic.

### The three constants

**Definition (The Chowla and Selberg constant $C_d$).** $C_d = \gamma - \operatorname{Re} \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi_K)$, where $\gamma$ is Euler's constant.

Lean: `New.chowlaSelbergConst` (Lib/General/NumberTheory/ImaginaryQuadraticField.lean).

In the source, the character is written as the twist of the character modulo $1$ by $(\cdot/d)$, so it is a character modulo $1 \cdot d$, and the $L$-function is `entireL` of it (Section 4). For odd $d$ it is not principal, so this is $L(s, \chi_K)$.

**Definition (The period $\Omega$).** $\Omega = e^{C_d} \cdot 2\pi / \mathrm{rd}_E$.

Lean: `New.Parameters.bigOmega` (New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean).

The source proves $x^{-13} \leq \Omega \leq 1$ for the data (Section 3). The number $\Omega$ enters the jet size $s_\infty$ of Section 4 through $\log(M/\Omega)$.

**Definition (The constant of the parameters $C_P$).** 
$$C_P = C_{\mathrm{vac}} + (2d + 6) + q_0 + |C_d| + \sum_{w} \bigl|\log |\Phi_{q_0}'(w)|\bigr|,$$
where $w$ runs over the primitive $q_0$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb{C}$, $\Phi_{q_0}$ is the cyclotomic polynomial of order $q_0$, and $C_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is the natural number of the bound for the conjugates of the integral vacuum.

Lean: `New.Parameters.constantBound` (New/Reduction/Data/AdmissibleData.lean), `New.Parameters.vacuumBoundConst` (New/Derivatives/Vacuum/IntegralVacuum.lean).

**Remark (Formalization).** The number $C_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is defined by `choose` from an existence theorem. It has no formula, and it may be $0$. Only the lower bounds of the next lemma and the first scale inequality of Section 3 use $C_P$, so the argument needs no value of $C_{\mathrm{vac}}$. The letter $\Phi_{q_0}$ is the cyclotomic polynomial only inside this formula; elsewhere $\Phi$ is the group of places.

**Lemma.** $64 \leq C_P$, $q_0 \leq C_P$, $d \leq C_P$ and $\varphi(q_0) \leq C_P$.

Lean: `New.Data.le_constantBound` (New/Reduction/Data/Magnitudes.lean).

*Proof.* All terms of $C_P$ are at least $0$, so $C_P \geq q_0 + 2d + 6$. Then use $d > 64$ and $\varphi(q_0) \leq q_0$. $\square$

### The sparse sets

**Definition (Sparse set).** A set $T \subseteq \Phi$ is *sparse for the primes up to $L$* if for every prime $\ell \leq L$ and all integers $0 < i < j < l_0(s)$ there are no places $\sigma, \tau, \rho \in T$, distinct or not, with $\tau/\sigma \equiv \pm \ell^i$ and $\rho/\sigma \equiv \pm \ell^j$ modulo the part of $q_1$ prime to $\ell$. The two signs are independent.

Lean: `New.Parameters.IsSparse` (New/Reduction/Data/AdmissibleData.lean).

The congruences are taken through the class map $\mathrm{cl}_{q'}$ with $q' = q_1 / \ell^{v_\ell(q_1)}$, Lean's `ordCompl[ℓ] q₁`. The letter $L$ is local to this definition. The set of $b$-places of Section 3 is sparse for $L = k t$.
