## Reading paths

The book has 114 chapters. Few readers need all of them. This section gives four paths through the book, each as an exact list of chapters in the order in which to read them. A path is a list of what a reader needs for one purpose, with nothing left out. Each later path starts where the first one ends.

The lists are built from the chapters themselves. Where an opening chapter of an argument volume names the background chapters that its volume uses, the path takes that list. Where a chapter is not yet written, the link names it and gives no number. Section 5 says how to see which chapters exist at a given edition.

### Path 1: the outline only

This path is for a reader who wants the whole design of the proof and every size, and who is ready to take Theorems A, B and C and the background results as stated. It is also the start of every other path. It has 17 chapters.

1. [The claim and its status](/chapter/claim-and-status/) (Volume 0, chapter 2).
2. [The statements](/chapter/statements/) (Volume 0, chapter 3).
3. [Notation and conventions](/chapter/notation/) (Volume 0, chapter 5). Read it fast, and come back to it when a letter is unclear.
4. [The theorem and the plan](/chapter/theorem-and-plan/) (Volume 1, chapter 1).
5. [From the half-plane to the line](/chapter/half-plane-to-line/) (Volume 1, chapter 2).
6. [The weighted prime remainder](/chapter/weighted-prime-remainder/) (Volume 1, chapter 3).
7. [Landau's criterion](/chapter/landau-criterion/) (Volume 1, chapter 4).
8. [Why a lattice can bound a prime sum](/chapter/lattice-and-prime-sum/) (Volume 1, chapter 5).
9. [Fields, places and twists](/chapter/fields-places-twists/) (Volume 1, chapter 6).
10. [The data and its sizes](/chapter/data-and-sizes/) (Volume 1, chapter 7).
11. [The data exist](/chapter/data-exist/) (Volume 1, chapter 8).
12. [Prime sums and the total remainder](/chapter/prime-sums/) (Volume 1, chapter 9).
13. [Twisted lattices](/chapter/twisted-lattices/) (Volume 1, chapter 10).
14. [The lattice of integral target forms](/chapter/integral-target-forms/) (Volume 1, chapter 11).
15. [Theorems A, B and C](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/) (Volume 1, chapter 12).
16. [The main inequality and the end of the proof](/chapter/main-inequality/) (Volume 1, chapter 13).
17. [The register of findings](/chapter/register-of-findings/) (Volume 0, chapter 8).

A reader with less time can read chapters 4, 5, 15 and 16 of this list, in that order. Those four chapters state every link of the chain of Section 2 and prove the links above Theorems A, B and C. The other chapters of Volume 1 prove the facts about the data, the prime sums and the lattices that the end of the proof uses.

At the end of this path a reader knows what remains: the three theorems, and the background results that Volume 1 states with a link to their proofs. [The table of sizes](/chapter/sizes/) (Volume 0, chapter 6) is a reference for this path. It is not reading for a first pass.

Then it takes the opening chapter of the argument volume, the background chapters that this opening chapter names, and the rest of the argument volume.

### Path 3: a background reader

This path is for an analytic number theorist who wants to judge the reduction and the sizes, and not the automorphic part. It is also the model for a reader of any one background volume. After Path 1:

1. [The table of sizes](/chapter/sizes/) (Volume 0, chapter 6), read in full this time.
2. Volume 2, all seven chapters: [Dirichlet L-functions and zero symmetry](/chapter/dirichlet-l-functions/), [polar functions and the explicit formula](/chapter/polar-functions/), [Landau's theorem](/chapter/landau-theorem/), [the prime number theorem in progressions](/chapter/prime-number-theorem/), [primes in windows](/chapter/primes-in-windows/), [Pólya-Vinogradov and quadratic twists](/chapter/polya-vinogradov/) and [sets with no three-term progressions](/chapter/progression-free-sets/).
3. The size chapters of Volume 9: [Theorem A and its plan](/chapter/theorem-a-plan/), [the archimedean size of the composite lift](/chapter/archimedean-size/) and [the proof of Theorem A](/chapter/proof-of-theorem-a/).
4. The size chapters of Volume 10: [Theorem B and its plan](/chapter/theorem-b-plan/), [moments of the Poincaré map](/chapter/moments/), [the remaining terms](/chapter/remaining-terms/), [the main term](/chapter/main-term/), [local densities at the real places](/chapter/real-densities/) and [the proof of Theorem B](/chapter/proof-of-theorem-b/).

Volume 2 needs only the outline and the analytic theory of Davenport. It is the background of the analytic steps of Volume 1: the reflection of zeros, the poles of the Mellin transform, Landau's theorem, the prime number theorem at the second scale, the primes in windows for the level, the zero density that chooses the prime $d$, and the sets of places. The size chapters of Volumes 9 and 10 are the places where the argument spends its error budget against $m \approx \sqrt{x}$. A reader of this path can compare each loss there with the error term $f a \log^5 b$ of the star inequality, which [the main inequality and the end of the proof](/chapter/main-inequality/) states with every part of its budget.

The same pattern serves a reader of any background volume. Read Path 1. Then read the volume in its own order: its first chapter lists its prerequisites, with textbook chapters. Then read the opening chapters of the argument volumes that use it, and follow their links back into the volume. Path 2 says which argument volume uses which background chapter.

### Path 4: a Lean reader

This path is for a reader who knows Lean and Mathlib and wants to check the formalization. It does not need the mathematics in order. It needs the conventions of the source and the place of each declaration.

1. [The claim and its status](/chapter/claim-and-status/) (Volume 0, chapter 2): the reported checks and what a kernel check guarantees.
2. [The statements](/chapter/statements/) (Volume 0, chapter 3): the five types against Mathlib.
3. [Reading the Lean](/chapter/reading-the-lean/) (Volume 0, chapter 7): junk values, objects defined by choice, the settings of the source, and how to find a declaration.
4. [Notation and conventions](/chapter/notation/) (Volume 0, chapter 5), with its list of clashes between the letters of the book and the names of the source.
5. [The table of sizes](/chapter/sizes/) (Volume 0, chapter 6): each size with the file and line that imposes it.
6. [The register of findings](/chapter/register-of-findings/) (Volume 0, chapter 8).
7. [The two editions](/chapter/two-editions/) (Volume 0, chapter 9): the map from the names of the compact edition to the names of the readable edition.
8. [The glossary and index of Lean names](/chapter/lean-index/) (Volume 0, chapter 10).
9. Then any chapter of the book, with its `Lean:` lines and its remarks on the formalization.

In item 9 the reader compares each numbered statement with the type of the declaration on its `Lean:` line: the same objects, hypotheses, quantifiers and conclusion. This is the check of the status `checked` in Section 5. A good first chapter for it is [from the half-plane to the line](/chapter/half-plane-to-line/). It is short, its declarations sit close to Mathlib, and it has remarks on every junk value that matters.

### The whole book

The main reader, a graduate student, reads all the volumes in order: Volume 0 (chapters 2 to 5, with the others as reference), Volume 1, the background Volumes 2 to 8, and then Volumes 9 to 12. A student who prefers to see each background theory used soon after it is learned can follow Path 2 three times instead: Theorem A, then Theorem B, then Theorem C. The three passes read Volume 4 and Volume 6 in parts. Together they cover every chapter of Volumes 3 to 12 except [Iwahori exponents, Satake and the chain lemma](/chapter/iwahori-satake/) of Volume 5, [schemes through their points](/chapter/schemes-through-points/), [cohomology as Ext on test rings](/chapter/cohomology-ext/) and [the comparison theorems on test objects](/chapter/comparison-theorems/) of Volume 7, and [complex abelian varieties through theta functions](/chapter/complex-abelian-varieties/), [an integral model without a moduli problem](/chapter/integral-model/) and [the congruence relation](/chapter/congruence-relation/) of Volume 8. A reader meets them through the links of the chapters that Path 2 names, or reads them with the rest of their volumes.
