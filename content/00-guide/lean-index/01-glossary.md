## Glossary

This chapter has two parts. The first part is this glossary. It gives each term of the book in one sentence, with a link to the chapter that defines it. The definition in the glossary is a reminder, not the definition: the exact statement, with all of its hypotheses, is in the linked chapter. The second part is the index of Lean names, in the sections that follow. It gives each Lean declaration that a written chapter cites, with its file and its line at the pin, and the chapters that cite it. The [table of the status of the chapters](/chapter/lean-index/#the-status-of-the-chapters) comes first. The glossary is written by hand. The table and the index are made from the text of the chapters by a script, as the [notes](/chapter/lean-index/#notes-and-references) of this chapter say.

The terms are in alphabetical order. A term of the Lean language is in code font. A letter of the notation, such as $q_0$, $b$ or $\Omega$, is in the chapter on [notation](/chapter/notation/), and a size, such as $m$ or $r$, is in the [table of sizes](/chapter/sizes/).

### Terms of the mathematics

- **Admissible data.** Admissible data at $x$ and $W$ for parameters $P$ are the scales $m$, $r$, $s$, $b$, the prime $N$, a finite set $T$ of places, two primes $p_0$, $p_1$ and integers $\alpha$ of $K$ and $\beta$ of $E$ that satisfy eleven explicit conditions, the first of which is that $m$ is the least power of $2$ with $m \geq \sqrt{x}$. See [the existence theorem](/chapter/data-exist/#the-existence-theorem).
- **Chain determinant.** The chain determinant of an $m$-fold twist $\vartheta$ is $Z(\vartheta) = \det(\Theta_\vartheta^*\Theta_\vartheta)$, the square of the norm determinant of the map $\Theta_\vartheta$. See [the volume and the chain determinant](/chapter/integral-target-forms/#the-volume-and-the-chain-determinant).
- **Chowla and Selberg constant.** For a prime $d$ the constant is $C_d = \gamma - \operatorname{Re} (\tilde L'/\tilde L)(1, \chi_K)$, with $\chi_K = (\cdot/d)$ and $\gamma$ the constant of Euler. See [the Dirichlet series and the constant](/chapter/weighted-prime-remainder/#the-dirichlet-series-and-the-constant).
- **CM field.** A CM field is a totally complex field of characteristic $0$ that is a quadratic extension of its maximal real subfield. See [CM fields and two criteria](/chapter/cm-fields/#cm-fields-and-two-criteria).
- **Colength.** The colength of a submodule $N$ in a module $M$ over a commutative ring $O$ divides $z \in O$ if every finite family in $M$ is carried into $N$ by a square matrix over $O$ whose determinant divides $z$. See [colength](/chapter/twisted-lattices/#colength).
- **Completed L-function.** The completed L-function of a Dirichlet character is its L-function times the gamma factor of its parity, the function that has the functional equation. See [the completed L-function](/chapter/dirichlet-l-functions/#the-completed-l-function).
- **Covolume at most $c$.** A finite family $y$ in a twisted lattice has covolume at most $c$ if the absolute value of the Gram determinant of its trivial images, for the rational Gram matrix of the lattice, is at most $e^c$ under every automorphism of $\mathbb{C}$. See [twisted lattices, index and covolume](/chapter/twisted-lattices/#twisted-lattices-index-and-covolume).
- **Data.** For parameters $P$, the data are the scales, the ranks, the places, the weights and the primes of the argument, with the conditions that their definition states, among them the five scale inequalities. See [the data and its sizes](/chapter/data-and-sizes/).
- **Direction amount.** The direction amount is the sum over all places $\sigma \in \Phi$ of the jet size at the weight $\kappa(\sigma)$ minus the prime sum at $\kappa(\sigma)$ and $\sigma$. See [jet sizes, the direction amount and the main amount](/chapter/prime-sums/#jet-sizes-the-direction-amount-and-the-main-amount).
- **Divisibility error.** The divisibility error $\mathrm{err} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ of the data is a function chosen by `choose` from the theorem on the divisibility of the composite lift, and only its four properties in that theorem are known. See [the normalized map and its two chosen constants](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#the-normalized-map-and-its-two-chosen-constants).
- **Entire L-function.** For a Dirichlet character $\chi$, $\tilde L(s, \chi)$ is $L(s, \chi)$ if $\chi$ is not principal, and the entire continuation of $(s - 1)L(s, \chi)$ if $\chi$ is principal. See [the sign of the residues](/chapter/landau-criterion/#the-sign-of-the-residues).
- **Euclidean lattice.** A Euclidean lattice is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank with a Euclidean norm on its real span, and its covolume is the square root of the Gram determinant of a basis. See [the slope inequality](/chapter/lattice-and-prime-sum/#the-slope-inequality).
- **Gauge.** The gauge of a value $\xi = \varphi(g)$ of a form at $g$ is the largest $\ell$-adic absolute value of a coordinate of $g^{-1} \cdot \iota(\xi)$, and a form is integral at $\iota$ if all of its gauges are at most $1$. See [the target lattice](/chapter/integral-target-forms/#the-target-lattice).
- **Good forms.** The good forms on a space $V$ are the image of the idempotent $e_0$ on the principal forms on $V$. See [the target forms and the chain](/chapter/integral-target-forms/#the-target-forms-and-the-chain).
- **Half-plane statement.** The half-plane statement says that $L(s, \chi) \neq 0$ for every Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $q \geq 1$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 1/2$, except $\chi$ principal and $s = 1$. See [the four statements](/chapter/half-plane-to-line/#the-four-statements).
- **Height at most $c$.** A family of complex numbers, one for each prime with an isomorphism $\iota$, has height at most $c$ if one nonzero number $H$ and local bounds $h(\ell)$ control all of its members, with the total bounded by $c$. See [heights over the isomorphisms with the l-adic numbers](/chapter/twisted-lattices/#heights-over-the-isomorphisms-with-the-l-adic-numbers).
- **Index at most $\delta$.** A finite family $y$ in a twisted lattice has index at most $\delta$ if there are losses $e(\ell) \geq 0$ with $\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell)\log \ell \leq \delta$ for every finite set $U$ of primes, such that at each $\iota \mid \ell$ a square matrix over $\mathcal{O}_\iota$ carries $y$ into the integral vectors with loss $e(\ell)$. See [twisted lattices, index and covolume](/chapter/twisted-lattices/#twisted-lattices-index-and-covolume).
- **Jet size.** For an integer $j \geq 0$ the jet size is $s_\infty(j) = \log j! + j\log(M/\Omega)$. See [jet sizes, the direction amount and the main amount](/chapter/prime-sums/#jet-sizes-the-direction-amount-and-the-main-amount).
- **Landau's criterion.** Landau's criterion says that if the remainder $R_{q_0}$ of every prime $d$ in a fixed class satisfies $R_{q_0}(y) \leq y^{\beta - 1}$ for large $y$, then no $L(\cdot, \psi)$ with $\psi$ modulo $q_0$ has a zero $\rho \neq 1$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho > \beta$. See [the criterion](/chapter/landau-criterion/#the-criterion).
- **Main amount.** The main amount is $\mathrm{main} = m k\, \mathrm{dir}$, where $\mathrm{dir}$ is the direction amount. See [jet sizes, the direction amount and the main amount](/chapter/prime-sums/#jet-sizes-the-direction-amount-and-the-main-amount).
- **Main inequality.** The main inequality says that admissible data satisfy $m \cdot \mathrm{rem} \leq f a \log^5 b$. See [the main inequality](/chapter/main-inequality/#the-main-inequality).
- **$m$-fold twist.** An $m$-fold twist is a twist $\vartheta$ whose tame part satisfies $\psi_w^n \neq 1$ for every place $w \in Q$ and every $1 \leq n \leq m$, so that each power $\vartheta^n$ with $n \leq m$ is again a twist. See [characters, twists and m-fold twists](/chapter/fields-places-twists/#characters-twists-and-m-fold-twists).
- **Near isometry.** A linear map $A : S \to T$ is a near isometry with the error $\varepsilon_0$ if $\|A\,\mathrm{pr}_U\|^2 \geq \dim U - \varepsilon_0 \dim S$ for every subspace $U$ of $S$. See [Theorem B, enough forms](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#theorem-b-enough-forms).
- **Normalized Poincaré map.** For $s \in \bar G$ the normalized map is $s\mathfrak{p}(\varphi) = c_B \cdot \#\bar G \cdot e_0\, s\, \mathcal{P}(c_{ss}^{-1}\varphi)$, which carries source forms to target forms. See [the normalized map and its two chosen constants](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#the-normalized-map-and-its-two-chosen-constants).
- **One-sided bound.** The one-sided bound says that for parameters $q_0$, $d$ and every $\eta > 0$ the remainder satisfies $R_{q_0}(y) \leq y^{-1/2 + \eta}$ for all large real $y$. See [the one-sided bound and the half-plane theorem](/chapter/main-inequality/#the-one-sided-bound-and-the-half-plane-theorem).
- **Parameters.** The parameters are a pair $(q_0, d)$ of natural numbers with $18480 \mid q_0$, $d$ prime, $q_0 \mid 2(d + 1)$ and $q_0 \nmid d + 1$. See [the parameters](/chapter/fields-places-twists/#the-parameters-q0-and-d).
- **Polar function.** A measurable function $E$ on $\mathbb{R}$ is polar with residues $r$ if $|E(v)| \leq Ce^v$ for $v \geq 0$ and its transform $\int_0^\infty E(v)e^{(1-s)v}\,dv$ on $\operatorname{Re} s > 2$ extends to a meromorphic function on $\mathbb{C}$ with residue $r(\rho)$ at each $\rho$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho > 0$. See [polar functions](/chapter/polar-functions/#polar-functions).
- **Polar pair.** A polar pair $(E, r)$ is the form of a polar function that the criterion of Landau uses, with three conditions in place of four. See [polar pairs and Landau's lemma](/chapter/landau-criterion/#polar-pairs-and-landaus-lemma).
- **Prime sum.** For real $y$ and a place $\sigma$, the prime sum is $s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma) = \sum_{1 \leq \nu \leq a} \Lambda_K(\nu)\bigl(y/\nu - \kappa(-\nu\sigma)\bigr)_+$. See [the prime sums](/chapter/prime-sums/).
- **Regular family.** A family of $m$-fold twists is regular if it has small ratios and, for every $1 \leq j \leq m$, a denominator bound. See [the four inputs](/chapter/theorem-c-plan/#the-four-inputs).
- **Scale inequalities.** The scale inequalities are the five inequalities (S1) to (S5) between the scales of the data, the first of which is $10^{12} C_P \leq \log\log b$. See [the scales and the five inequalities](/chapter/data-and-sizes/#the-scales-and-the-five-inequalities).
- **Seesaw constant.** The seesaw constant $c_{ss} \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ is a unit chosen by `choose` from the seesaw identity, which relates the lower chain of a Poincaré lift to the composite lift. See [the normalized map and its two chosen constants](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#the-normalized-map-and-its-two-chosen-constants).
- **Short forms.** A form on a definite unitary group is $B$-short if its values are integral for the Weyl lattice at every $p$-adic place of $\mathbb{C}$ and the squared Weyl norm of every conjugate of every value is at most $B$. See [how the inputs meet](/chapter/theorem-a-plan/#how-the-inputs-meet).
- **Sparse set of places.** A finite set $T$ of places is sparse for the primes up to $L$ if no two quotients of its members have classes $\pm\ell^i$ and $\pm\ell^j$ with $0 < i < j < l_0$ for a prime $\ell \leq L$. See [a sparse set of places](/chapter/data-exist/#a-sparse-set-of-places).
- **Spread.** Linear maps $B_s : V \to W$ spread with the error $\eta$ if for every subspace $F$ of $W$ some $B_s^{-1}(F)$ has dimension at most $\bigl((1 + \alpha)/2 + \eta\bigr)\dim V$, where $\alpha = \dim F/\dim W$. See [Theorem B, enough forms](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#theorem-b-enough-forms).
- **Tame character.** A tame character is an automorphic character of weight $0$ with a finite set $Q$ of finite places and a tame part $\psi$ trivial outside $Q$, with conditions on $Q$; a twist is a tame character whose tame part is nontrivial at every place of $Q$. See [characters, twists and m-fold twists](/chapter/fields-places-twists/#characters-twists-and-m-fold-twists).
- **Target forms.** The target forms are the good forms on the space $Y_0$, and their space is $T$ of dimension $d_T$. See [the target forms and the chain](/chapter/integral-target-forms/#the-target-forms-and-the-chain).
- **Target lattice.** The target lattice is the twisted lattice on the target forms whose twists are the $m$-fold twists, with the forms integral at each $\iota$ as its integral vectors. See [the target lattice](/chapter/integral-target-forms/#the-target-lattice).
- **Theorem A.** Theorem A says that integral source forms with short images span the source, and that a family of their images has small index in the target lattice. See [Theorem A, short forms of small index](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#theorem-a-short-forms-of-small-index).
- **Theorem B.** Theorem B says that the target is not zero and that a small set of normalized maps has images that span at least $(1 - 4/b^2)\,d_T$ dimensions of it. See [Theorem B, enough forms](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#theorem-b-enough-forms).
- **Theorem C.** Theorem C says that $d_T(\mathrm{main} - 3fa\log^4 b) \leq c + 2\delta$ for enough independent target forms of index at most $\delta$ and covolume at most $c$. See [Theorem C, long forms](/chapter/theorems-a-b-c/#theorem-c-long-forms).
- **Total remainder.** The total remainder is $\mathrm{rem} = \sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a}\bigl(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) - s_{\mathrm{main}}\bigr)$, the sum over the $a$-places of the prime sum minus its expected value. See [the expected value and the total remainder](/chapter/prime-sums/#the-expected-value-and-the-total-remainder).
- **Twisted lattice.** A twisted lattice on a complex space $V$ is a family of linear maps $\Theta_\xi$ from $V$ to inner product spaces, one for each twist $\xi$, with integral submodules at each $\iota$ that pair integrally and a rational positive Gram matrix for the trivial twist. See [twisted lattices, index and covolume](/chapter/twisted-lattices/#twisted-lattices-index-and-covolume).
- **Weighted prime remainder.** The weighted prime remainder is $R_q(x) = \sum_{1 \leq n \leq x} \lambda(n)(1/n - 1/x) - \log x/\varphi(q) - c_q$, with the weights $\lambda = \Lambda(1 - \chi_K)$ on $n \equiv 1 \bmod q$. See [the weights and the remainder](/chapter/weighted-prime-remainder/#the-weights-and-the-remainder).

### Terms of the Lean language

- **`choose`.** Applied to a proof of $\exists\, e,\ P(e)$, `choose` gives some $e$ with $P(e)$, and nothing else about $e$ is known to a later proof. See [existence, choice and data](/chapter/reading-the-lean/#existence-choice-and-data).
- **Comparator.** Comparator is a tool that compares the five statements of `grh/Challenge.lean` with the theorems of `Main` and checks the proofs again with the three permitted axioms, and the README of the source reports that it accepted them. The repository holds no output of that run. A third party has published a log of `lake comparator --paranoid` at the pin (`https://math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/`). See [the reported checks](/chapter/claim-and-status/#the-reported-checks).
- **Constant that may vanish.** A constant that may vanish is a constant $c$ of an identity $X = cY$ that the statement does not show to be nonzero, or a nonzero $c$ in an identity whose two sides may both be $0$. See [junk values and constants that may vanish](/chapter/reading-the-lean/#junk-values-and-constants-that-may-vanish).
- **`HasLogBound`.** The statement $\mathrm{HasLogBound}(D, u, F, l)$ says $|F| \leq e^l$ if $u$ is true and $e^l \leq F$ if $u$ is false, and the source proves its sizes with it. See [the table of sizes](/chapter/data-and-sizes/#the-table-of-sizes).
- **Junk value.** A junk value is the value that Lean gives to an expression that a mathematician calls undefined, such as $x/0 = 0$, or $0$ for a sum or an integral that does not converge. See [junk values and constants that may vanish](/chapter/reading-the-lean/#junk-values-and-constants-that-may-vanish).
- **Kernel check.** A kernel check is the check of each proof term by the kernel of Lean, and if the build succeeds with the three standard axioms, the kernel is correct, the type theory is consistent and the five statements mean the classical ones, then the theorems hold, a conditional statement that this book explains and does not assert. See [what a kernel check guarantees](/chapter/claim-and-status/#what-a-kernel-check-guarantees).
- **Pin.** The pin is the commit `72fead1` of the source, named in the file `EDITION` of this book, at which the book reads every file, name and line. See [the claim and its status](/chapter/claim-and-status/).
- **`seal`.** The command `seal f in` makes the definition `f` irreducible for the next declaration only, which changes how Lean finds a proof and not what a statement says. See [the settings of the source](/chapter/reading-the-lean/#the-settings-of-the-source).
