## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** Let $n \ge 1$ with $\gcd(n, d) = 1$. Show that $K \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) = \mathbb{Q}$ inside $\mathbb{C}$, and deduce that $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \{\pm 1\} \times (\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$. Show that complex conjugation corresponds to $(-1, -1)$.

*Hint.* The prime $d$ ramifies in $K$ and not in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$. A pair $(\epsilon, a)$ acts by $\sqrt{-d} \mapsto \epsilon \sqrt{-d}$ and $\zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^a$. Complex conjugation sends $\sqrt{-d}$ to $-\sqrt{-d}$ and $\zeta_n$ to $\zeta_n^{-1}$.

**Exercise 2.** Let $E = K(\zeta)$ with $\zeta$ a primitive $q_1$-th root of unity. Show that the maximal real subfield $E^+$ has degree $\varphi(q_1)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and that
$$E^+ = \mathbb{Q}\big(\zeta + \zeta^{-1},\ \sqrt{-d}\,(\zeta - \zeta^{-1})\big).$$
Show that $E^+$ is not $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta + \zeta^{-1})$.

*Hint.* By Exercise 1, $E^+$ is the fixed field of $(-1,-1)$. Both generators are fixed. The field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta + \zeta^{-1})$ has degree $\varphi(q_1)/2$.

**Exercise 3.** Let $F$ be a totally complex number field. Show that every $x \in F$, including $x = 0$, satisfies `New.IsTotallyPositive x`. Deduce that an open subgroup of the finite ideles of $F$ that contains all principal ideles has finite index. For $F = \mathbb{Q}$, give an open subgroup $H$ of $\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f}^\times$ that contains the principal ideles of the positive rationals and does not contain the principal idele of $-1$.

*Hint.* There is no ring homomorphism $F \to \mathbb{R}$, since $F$ contains a square root of a negative rational. For $\mathbb{Q}$, use $\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f}^\times = \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \times \hat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$ and take $H = \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \times U$, where $U$ is the kernel of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times \to (\mathbb{Z}/4)^\times$.

**Exercise 4.** Let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q})$ and $H = \{1\}$. Show that $H$ is closed and that both sides of `New.finrank_fixedFieldOf` are $0$. Give a closed subgroup of infinite index that is not trivial.

*Hint.* The fixed field of $\{1\}$ is $\Omega$, of infinite degree. Mathlib's `Module.finrank` and `Subgroup.index` are $0$ in the infinite case. For the second part, take the subgroup generated by complex conjugation for an embedding of $\Omega$ in $\mathbb{C}$.

**Exercise 5.** Let $A$ be a valuation subring of $\Omega$ over a prime $p$, and suppose that `New.IsFrob A q σ` holds for some $q \in \mathbb{N}$. Show that $q$ is a power of $p$. Show that $q$ is unique, given $A$ and $\sigma$.

*Hint.* The residue field $k_A$ of $A$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$. The element $\sigma$ induces a ring automorphism of $k_A$, and the condition says that it is $x \mapsto x^q$. For $q = 0$, test $x = 0$. A map $x \mapsto x^q$ with $q \ge 1$ that is additive on $k_A$ forces $q$ to be a power of $p$, by the coefficients of $(x + 1)^q$. For uniqueness, $x^q = x^{q'}$ for all $x \in k_A$ forces $q = q'$, since $k_A$ is infinite.

**Exercise 6.** Let $p \nmid q_1 d$ be a prime and $w$ a place of $E$ over $p$. Show that if $p$ splits in $K$, then $w$ is split, that is, $\bar w \ne w$. Then let $p$ be inert in $K$, and suppose that $q_1$ has a prime factor $\ell \equiv 3 \bmod 4$ such that $p$ is a square modulo $\ell$. Show that $w$ is split also in this case.

*Hint.* For the first part, $w \cap \mathcal{O}_K$ and $\bar w \cap \mathcal{O}_K$ are the two distinct primes of $K$ over $p$. For the second part, use Exercise 4 of [the field E](/chapter/fields-places-twists/): $\bar w = w$ needs $p^k \equiv -1 \bmod q_1$ for some odd $k$, hence modulo $\ell$. Every power of $p$ is a square modulo $\ell$, and $-1$ is not, since $\ell \equiv 3 \bmod 4$.

## Notes and references

The classical theory of this chapter is in standard texts. For number fields, prime decomposition, the different and the discriminant, Hilbert's theory of decomposition and inertia groups, and cyclotomic fields, see Neukirch, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Springer, 1999: Ch. I, §§8 to 10, Ch. III, §2, and Ch. IV, §1 for infinite Galois theory. Ideles are in Ch. VI, §1, and henselian fields in Ch. II, §6. For cyclotomic fields and CM fields, see Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, second edition, Springer, 1997: Ch. 2, with Proposition 2.7 for the discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, and Ch. 4 for CM fields. For the local theory and the Frobenius, see Serre, *Local Fields*, Springer, 1979, Ch. I, §7. For quadratic fields and Gauss sums, see Ireland and Rosen, *A Classical Introduction to Modern Number Theory*, second edition, Springer, 1990, Ch. 6, §3, and Ch. 13, §1.

In Mathlib, CM fields are in `Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/CMField.lean`, infinite places in `Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/InfinitePlace/`, finite adeles in `Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/FiniteAdeleRing.lean`, and the root discriminant and the different in `Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/Discriminant/`. In the source, the places of the algebraic closure, the decomposition groups and the Frobenius elements are in the directory `Lib/ClassField/Places/`, and the CM criteria are in `Lib/ClassField/Places/CMFields.lean`.

The source makes four choices that differ from the classical form, and the sections above give each in a formalization remark:

1. The field $E$ is the abstract type `CyclotomicField q₁ K`, not a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ or of $\Omega$. The family $E_s$ has no maps $E_s \to E_{s+1}$ in the source (Section 4).
2. The set $\Phi$ of the main theorem is the set of infinite places of $E$. A CM type is one chosen embedding at each place, with a default instance from Mathlib's `choose` (Section 5).
3. A place of the algebraic closure is a valuation subring. A Frobenius is a property `New.IsFrob A q σ`, not a function, so it is defined only up to inertia, and the chosen place `New.placeOver v` comes from `Classical.epsilon` (Section 6).
4. The idele groups are the finite ideles. For the CM fields of the argument, the condition "totally positive" is empty, and the adele type `New.𝔸` is the adele ring only because these fields are totally complex (Sections 5 and 7).

Junk values in this chapter: `Module.finrank` and `Subgroup.index` are $0$ in the infinite case (Section 4), `Set.ncard` is $0$ for an infinite set (Section 6), `New.residueCard` is $0$ for an infinite residue field (Section 6), and $\nu / 0 = 0$ in the second CM criterion (Section 3). Choices by `choose` or `Classical.epsilon`: `NumberField.IsCMField.complexConj` (Mathlib), `NumberField.InfinitePlace.embedding` (Mathlib), `New.Ω.fieldEmb`, `New.placeOver`, `New.ordRat` and `New.placeOfEmbExtension`.

The next chapter of this volume uses these fields for class field theory: [class field theory](/chapter/class-field-theory/). The notation of the argument is in [the field E](/chapter/fields-places-twists/) of Volume 1.
