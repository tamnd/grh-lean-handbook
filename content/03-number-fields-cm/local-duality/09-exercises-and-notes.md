## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $n$, and let $M = \mathbb{Z}/l$ with trivial action and $\mu_l \subseteq k$. Show that $\#H^1(k, M) = p^{n+2}$ when $l = p$, and $l^2$ when $l \neq p$.

*Hint.* Use Kummer theory, $H^1(k, \mu_l) \cong k^\times/k^{\times l}$, and the structure $k^\times \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mu(k) \times \mathbb{Z}_p^n$.

**Exercise 2.** Let $X \subseteq Z^1(M^\vee)$ and $W \subseteq Z^1(M)$ be orthogonal, and let $M$ be killed by a prime $l \neq p$. Deduce $\#X \cdot \#W \leq \#M^2$ from local Tate duality and the local Euler characteristic formula.

*Hint.* The orthogonal of the image of $W$ in $H^1(M)$ has order $\#H^1(M)/\#\bar W$. Count the coboundaries in $X$ and $W$ with $H^0(M)$ and $H^0(M^\vee) \cong H^2(M)^\vee$.

**Exercise 3.** Let $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$ be exact. Show that $\#Z^1(M) \leq \#Z^1(M') \cdot \#Z^1(M'')$. This is the shape of the dévissage in `New.hasFewCocycles_of_isShortExact`.

*Hint.* The map $Z^1(M) \to Z^1(M'')$ has kernel $Z^1(M')$.

**Exercise 4.** Let $K$ be a totally complex number field. Show that $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ contains no complex conjugation, and that $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E^+)$ does for a CM field $E$. Explain why the conjugation fields of `New.RelationSetting` matter only over a field with a real place.

*Hint.* A complex conjugation fixes a real embedding of its fixed field.

**Exercise 5.** Show that $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, with either CM type, has no splitting character.

*Hint.* The idele $i$ is principal and is a unit at every finite place. Compute $\prod_w \Phi_w(i)$.

**Exercise 6.** Show that $x^8 - 16$ has a root in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ for every odd prime $p$, but no root in $\mathbb{Q}$. Deduce that $16$ is an $8$-th power at every odd place of $\mathbb{Q}$ but not in $\mathbb{Q}$.

*Hint.* Use the factorisation in the section [Grunwald's theorem and CM steps](/chapter/local-duality/#grunwalds-theorem-and-cm-steps), and the fact that one of $2$, $-2$ and $-1$ is a square modulo $p$.

**Exercise 7.** Let $E$ be a CM field and let $\upsilon$ be a splitting character. Show that $\upsilon(x)^2 = 1$ for every idele $x$ with $\bar x = x$, and that $\upsilon(a z \bar z) = 1$ for $a \in E^\times$ real and $z$ an idele. This is the easy half of `New.Cohomology.upsilon_eq_quadraticIdeleChar`.

*Hint.* Use $\upsilon(\bar x) = \upsilon(x)^{-1}$ and the triviality of $\upsilon$ on $E^\times$.

**Exercise 8.** Let $v$ be a finite place of a number field $K$, let $M$ be finite and unramified at $v$, and let $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_v, M)$ be the image of $H^1(G_v/I_v, M)$. Show that $\#H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_v, M) = \#H^0(K_v, M)$. Deduce that the places outside $\Sigma$ add the factor $1$ to the Greenberg-Wiles formula.

*Hint.* The group $G_v/I_v$ is $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, generated by Frobenius, and $H^1(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}, M) = M/(\mathrm{Frob} - 1)M$. On a finite group, the kernel and the cokernel of an endomorphism have the same order.

## Notes and references

Local Tate duality, the local Euler characteristic formula and the Poitou-Tate sequence are in J. Neukirch, A. Schmidt and K. Wingberg, *Cohomology of Number Fields*, second edition, Springer, 2008, Chapters VII and VIII, and in J. S. Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, second edition, BookSurge, 2006, Chapter I. See also J.-P. Serre, *Galois Cohomology*, Springer, 1997, Chapter II, and, for the cohomological approach to local class field theory, J.-P. Serre, *Local Fields*, Springer, 1979. The facts behind Exercises 1, 2 and 8 are in Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Chapter I, §2. The cohomology of local fields with the Cartier dual is also in J. W. S. Cassels and A. Fröhlich (eds.), *Algebraic Number Theory*, Academic Press, 1967, Chapter VI (Tate).

The Bloch-Kato conditions are in S. Bloch and K. Kato, "L-functions and Tamagawa numbers of motives", in *The Grothendieck Festschrift*, Volume I, Birkhäuser, 1990. The Greenberg-Wiles formula is Proposition 1.6 of A. Wiles, *Annals of Mathematics* 141 (1995), and Theorem 2.19 of H. Darmon, F. Diamond and R. Taylor, "Fermat's Last Theorem", 1995.

The Grunwald-Wang theorem is in E. Artin and J. Tate, *Class Field Theory*, Chapter X, and in Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, Chapter IX, §2. An effective form is S. Wang, arXiv:1401.0389. Splitting characters go back to A. Weil, Tokyo-Nikko 1955; see A. Raghuram, arXiv:2207.03399, §4.

The source replaces each duality theorem by an inequality or a hypothesis. The local inequality $\#X \cdot \#W \leq \#M^{2+d}$ needs no hypothesis beyond the action of $\Gamma$ through an $l$-group. The divisibility needs a maximal orthogonal pair (`h_max`), which the source supplies for crystalline cocycles. The dual Selmer group is replaced by relations, whose reciprocity law is a field of `New.RelationSetting.IsRelation`. The existence of splitting characters needs a condition on roots of unity, which the user `New.Parameters.instWeilDataE` proves. The class field theory that the proofs use is in the chapter [Class field theory on finite ideles](/chapter/class-field-theory/).
