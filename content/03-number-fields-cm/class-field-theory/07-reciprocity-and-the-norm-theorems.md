## Reciprocity and the norm theorems

This section proves Artin's reciprocity law on principal finite ideles, the sign law, the surjectivity of the Artin map, and the norm theorems for cyclic extensions. It uses the notation of Section 6, and three results from there: the lemma on totally positive symbols (`New.IsTotallyPositive.exists_resH2_cyclicClass_eq_zero`), the norm criterion for symbols (`New.cyclicClass_eq_zero_iff_norm`), and the theorem of Albert, Brauer, Hasse and Noether (`New.albert_brauer_hasse_noether`). For a character $\chi$ of $\Gamma$ and $x \in K^\times$, $(\chi, x)$ is the symbol in $H^2(\Gamma, \Omega^\times)$, and $\operatorname{res}_U$ is restriction to $U$.

### Artin's reciprocity law

The source proves reciprocity by Artin's method. It starts from the cyclotomic case, where the law is explicit, and it moves to a general character by an auxiliary cyclotomic character chosen with care.

**Lemma (Cyclotomic reciprocity).** Let $q$ be an odd prime. There is a character $\chi$ of $\Gamma$ with the same kernel as the cyclotomic character modulo $q$, such that for all $x \in K^\times$,
$$\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x)) = 1 \iff 0 < N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x).$$
Lean: `New.artin_reciprocity_cyclotomic` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof sketch.* Prove the law over $\mathbb{Q}$ for a character $\xi$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q})$ with kernel $\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_q))$, and pull $\xi$ back to $\Gamma$. The norm moves the law from $K$ down to $\mathbb{Q}$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the group $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ is generated by $-1$ and the primes, and both sides are multiplicative. At a prime $p \neq q$ the local symbol is a power of a Frobenius, and a tame formula in the same file (`New.abelianLift_artinAt_eq_toHom_iff_of_tame`) computes it. At $q$ one uses $q = N(\zeta - 1)$, and a unit at $q$ multiplies $\zeta - 1$ by its residue. $\square$

**Lemma (Artin's lemma).** Let $A$ lie over the finite place $v$, let $\sigma$ be a Frobenius at $A$, let $l$ be a prime and let $m \neq 0$. Then there is a character $\chi$ of $\Gamma$ such that $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(y)) = 1$ for all $y \in K^\times$, and $a \cdot m \cdot \chi(\sigma) \neq 0$ for every $a$ prime to $l$.
Lean: `New.exists_reciprocity_character` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof.* Choose an odd prime $p$ such that the order of $q_v^{2m}$ modulo $p$ is divisible by $l$. Let $\chi_p$ be the character of cyclotomic reciprocity for $p$, and let $\chi = 2\chi_p$. The value of $\chi$ at the symbol of $y$ is the value of $\chi_p$ at the symbol of $y^2$, and $N(y^2) > 0$. So $\chi$ satisfies the law. If $a \cdot m \cdot \chi(\sigma) = 0$ with $a$ prime to $l$, then $\sigma^{2am}$ fixes $\mu_p$, so $q_v^{2am} \equiv 1 \pmod p$, and the order of $q_v^{2m}$ divides $a$. This is false, since $l$ divides that order. $\square$

**Theorem (Artin reciprocity).** Let $\xi$ be a character of $\Gamma$ and let $x \in K^\times$. Suppose that there is an open subgroup $U$ that contains every complex conjugation, with $\operatorname{res}_U (\xi, x) = 0$. Then $\xi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x)) = 1$.
Lean: `New.artin_reciprocity` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof.* Let $s_v = \xi(\operatorname{artinAt}_v(x))$ and let $n_v$ be its order. Only finitely many $s_v$ are nontrivial. Choose a Frobenius $\sigma_v$ at each $v$. Let $G$ be the group of characters that satisfy the law. For a place $v$ and a prime $l$, let $H_{v,l}$ be the subgroup of $\chi \in G$ such that $a \cdot n_v \cdot \chi(\sigma_v) = 0$ for some $a$ prime to $l$. By Artin's lemma each $H_{v,l}$ has infinite index in $G$: if the index $i$ were finite, then $i \chi \in H_{v,l}$ for the character $\chi$ of Artin's lemma with $m = n_v i$, which is false. A group is not the union of finitely many cosets of subgroups of infinite index (B. H. Neumann's lemma, `AddSubgroup.exists_finiteIndex_of_leftCoset_cover`). So some $\chi \in G$ lies in no $H_{v,l}$ with $s_v \neq 1$ and $l \mid n_v$. For this $\chi$ the symbol $(\xi, x)$ dies on $\ker \chi$ (`New.resH2_ker_cyclicClass_eq_zero`). So $(\xi, x) = (\chi, y)$ for some $y \in K^\times$. Equal symbols have equal local symbols at every $v$ (`New.abelianLift_artinAt_eq_of_cyclicClass_eq`). With the product formula of Section 5,
$$\xi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x)) = \prod_v \xi(\operatorname{artinAt}_v(x)) = \prod_v \chi(\operatorname{artinAt}_v(y)) = \chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(y)) = 1,$$
since $\chi \in G$. $\square$

The proof of `New.resH2_ker_cyclicClass_eq_zero` is the step where the second inequality enters. It uses Albert, Brauer, Hasse and Noether over the fixed field of $\ker \chi$.

**Corollary (Characters that kill the conjugations).** If $\chi(c) = 0$ for every complex conjugation $c$, then $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x)) = 1$ for every $x \in K^\times$.
Lean: `New.abelianLift_artinMap_principalIdele_eq_one` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof.* The symbol $(\chi, x)$ dies on the open subgroup $\ker \chi$, which contains the conjugations. Apply the theorem. $\square$

**Corollary (Totally positive elements).** If $x \in K^\times$ is totally positive, then $\operatorname{artinMap}(x) = 1$.
Lean: `New.artinMap_principalIdele_eq_one_of_isTotallyPositive` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof.* By duality it suffices that $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x)) = 1$ for every character $\chi$. The lemma on totally positive symbols gives the open subgroup $U$ for $(\chi, x)$. Apply the theorem. $\square$

**Theorem (Sign law).** Let $\chi$ be a character of $\Gamma$ with $\chi(c) \neq 0$ for every complex conjugation $c$. Then for $b \in K^\times$,
$$\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(b)) = 1 \iff 0 < N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(b).$$
Lean: `New.abelianLift_artinMap_principalIdele_eq_one_iff` (Lib/ClassField/Global/ArtinReciprocity.lean).

*Proof.* Let $\xi$ be the character of cyclotomic reciprocity for $q = 3$. Let $c$ be a complex conjugation. Since $c^2 = 1$, each nonzero value $\psi(c)$ of a character is the element $1/2$ of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. The element $c$ acts on the cube roots of unity by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1} \neq \zeta$, so $c \notin \ker \xi$ and $\xi(c) = 1/2$. By hypothesis $\chi(c) = 1/2$. So $\chi - \xi$ kills every conjugation, and by the first corollary $(\chi - \xi)(\operatorname{artinMap}(b)) = 1$. So $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(b)) = \xi(\operatorname{artinMap}(b))$, and cyclotomic reciprocity gives the claim. $\square$

**Example (The sign law from the textbook law).** On full ideles the textbook law says $\theta_K(x) = 1$ for $x \in K^\times$. Split the principal idele of $x$ into its finite part and its infinite part. Then the image of the finite part is the inverse of $\prod_v \theta_{K_v}(x)$ over the real places $v$, and $\theta_{K_v}(x)$ is $c_v$ if $x <_v 0$ and $1$ if $x >_v 0$. So $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(x))$ is $\chi(c_v)$ summed over the real places where $x$ is negative. If every $\chi(c_v)$ is $1/2$, this is $0$ if and only if the number of such places is even, that is, if and only if $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x) > 0$. The sign convention does not matter here, since $c_v^2 = 1$.

**Remark (Formalization).** The textbook law is the triviality of $\theta_K$ on all of $K^\times$, for the map on full ideles. On finite ideles this cannot hold: the source proves triviality only for totally positive $x$, or for characters that kill the conjugations, and the sign law covers the characters that kill no conjugation. A character that kills some conjugations and not others is not covered by a single statement. The general case follows from the textbook law by the method of the example, but the source does not state it.

### Surjectivity and the norm theorems

**Lemma (Closed image).** The image of $\operatorname{artinMap}$ in $\Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is closed.
Lean: `New.isClosed_range_artinMap` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* Let $H$ be the subgroup generated by the unit ideles $U_\emptyset$ and the kernel of $\operatorname{artinMap}$. It is open, and it contains the totally positive principal ideles by the second corollary. So it has finite index (`New.finiteIndex_idele_subgroup_of_isOpen`, Section 3). The image is the union of the translates of the compact set $\operatorname{artinMap}(U_\emptyset)$ by representatives of the finitely many cosets of $H$. $\square$

**Theorem (Surjectivity).** The map $\operatorname{artinMap} : (\mathbb{A}_{K,f})^\times \to \Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is surjective.
Lean: `New.artinMap_surjective` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* The image is closed. By duality it suffices to show that a character $\psi$ that kills the image is $0$. Let $F$ be the fixed field of $\ker \psi$. The character $\psi$ kills every local symbol, so every decomposition group lies in $\ker \psi$. So every finite place $v$ of $K$ splits completely in $F$: it has $[F : K]$ places over it, all with residue field of size $q_v$. The family $v \mapsto \#\{w \mid v : q_w = q_v\}$ has prime density $1$ (`New.hasPrimeDensity_card_degree_one`, Section 5). Here it is the constant $[F : K]$. So $[F : K] \leq 1$, and $\ker \psi = \Gamma$. $\square$

**Lemma (Cyclic extensions are cut out by characters).** Let $L/K$ be a cyclic extension inside $\Omega$. There is a character $\chi$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ whose kernel acts on $\Omega$ as the Galois group of $\Omega/L$.
Lean: `New.exists_isProGalois_ker_of_isCyclic` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* Pull back a faithful character of the cyclic group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ along restriction. $\square$

**Theorem (Hasse's norm theorem).** Let $L/K$ be a cyclic extension of number fields. Let $x \in K^\times$ be totally positive, and suppose that at every finite place $v$, the image of $x$ in $K_v$ lies in the closure of $\{N_{L/K}(y) : y \in L\}$ in $K_v$. Then $x = N_{L/K}(z)$ for some $z \in L^\times$.
Lean: `New.hasse_norm` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* Embed $L$ in $\Omega$ and let $\chi$ cut it out. Let $\alpha = (\chi, x)$. The lemma on totally positive symbols gives an open subgroup $U$ that contains the conjugations, with $\operatorname{res}_U \alpha = 0$. At a finite place, $x$ is a limit of norms, so its local symbol is trivial, and $\alpha$ dies on each decomposition group. By Albert, Brauer, Hasse and Noether, $\alpha = 0$. By the norm criterion, $x$ is a norm. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The local condition at $v$ is that $x$ is in the closure in $K_v$ of the global norms. This is the same as being a norm from $L_w^\times$, since $N(L_w^\times)$ is open and $L$ is dense in $L_w$. At the real places the source asks for total positivity. A local norm at a real place that stays real in $L$ can be negative. So the source hypothesis is stronger than the textbook one when some real place of $K$ stays real in $L$. Then $-1$, if it is a local norm at all finite places, is not covered.

**Theorem (Index of the norm group).** Let $\ker \chi$ act as the Galois group of $\Omega/L$. Then the kernel of $\chi \circ \operatorname{artinMap}$ has index $[L : K]$ in $(\mathbb{A}_{K,f})^\times$.
Lean: `New.index_ker_abelianLift_comp_artinMap` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* Pull back along the two surjections $(\mathbb{A}_{K,f})^\times \to \Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $\Gamma \to \Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$. The index does not change, and $\ker \chi$ has index $[L : K]$ in $\Gamma$. $\square$

**Theorem (The norm group).** Let $\ker \chi$ act as the Galois group of $\Omega/L$, with $L/K$ Galois. A finite idele $a$ of $K$ has $\chi(\operatorname{artinMap}(a)) = 1$ if and only if
$$a = x \cdot N_{L/K}(y)$$
for some totally positive $x \in K^\times$ and some $y \in (\mathbb{A}_{L,f})^\times$.
Lean: `New.abelianLift_artinMap_eq_one_iff` (Lib/ClassField/Global/NormTheorems.lean).

*Proof.* Let $W$ be the subgroup generated by the totally positive principal ideles and the norms from $L$. It lies in the kernel of $\chi \circ \operatorname{artinMap}$, by the second corollary and the compatibility of the Artin map with norms (`New.abelianLift_artinMap_adeleNorm`). The kernel has index $[L : K]$. The group $W$ is open: an idele near $1$ is a unit at every place and $\chi$ kills its local symbols, so each component is a local norm (`New.exists_localNorm_of_abelianLift_artinAt`), and the norms of the unit ideles form a closed group. So the second inequality applies to $W$ and gives $[(\mathbb{A}_{K,f})^\times : W] \leq [L : K]$. Hence $W$ is the kernel. $\square$

**Remark (Formalization).** The textbook norm group is the closure of $K^\times N(J_L)$ in $J_K$, or $K^\times N(C_L)$ in $C_K$. On finite ideles the principal factor must be totally positive. The source states the theorem only for the field cut out by one character, so for cyclic $L/K$. It does not state the general bijection between abelian extensions and open subgroups of finite index. The existence half of that bijection is Section 8.
